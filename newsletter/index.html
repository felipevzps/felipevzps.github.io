GOLDEN: Gene expression and allele-informed decomposition for genetic heterogeneity in spatial transcriptomic data

Paik, H.

Abstract: Motivation Spatial transcriptomics (ST) enables exploration of cellular interactions, including immune responses. However, current methods rely on pathologists, with limitations in addressing genetic heterogeneity, where niches harboring nonclonal cells, including placental or allograft tissues, are present. Deciphering these interindividual mixtures remains challenging. Results For deciphering interindividual heterogeneity in ST, we developed GOLDEN (Gene expressiOn and aLlele-informed DEcomposition for geNetic heterogeneity), which involves an allele-aware multitask network that encodes spot-level expressions into shared latent representations. Incorporating allele features, dual heads classify spot identity (target, nontarget, or mixed) and estimate target fractions. We utilized synthetic ST data with spot-level ground truth using semiallogeneic single-cell profiles. For these synthetic benchmarks, GOLDEN achieved reliable deconvolution, with high correlation (Pearson's r = 0.977) and low error (mean absolute error (MAE) = 0.045) in fraction estimation and consistent predictions across real-world STs. These predictions were genetically validated. Using GOLDEN, resolving spot-level genetic heterogeneity is feasible. Availability and implementation GOLDEN and the simulation code for ST are available at https://github.com/othertics/GOLDEN.

CytoRipple: Mapping Tissue Polarization through Subcellular Cytoskeleton Dynamics

Chen, D. G., Yee, T., +6 authors, Campbell, K. M.

Abstract: Spatial multi-omic technologies entail simultaneous measurement of tens-to-thousands of molecular features (e.g. RNA, protein). While cell type and state profiling is the major focus of spatial multi-omic analyses, cytoskeletal proteins are often solely utilized for cell segmentation and quality control. Here, we introduce a new computational framework, CytoRipple, that leverages these concomitant spatially-resolved single-cell measurements of cytoskeleton proteins to identify tissue-level vector fields. Polarization vectors are known to associate with cell movement and phenotype, inferring their physical orientation. We validate these associations across biological contexts and reveal how polarization vector fields can be harnessed to discover multi-cellular structures (e.g. "streams" of cells with similar polarizations). These multi-cellular cytoskeleton dynamics associate with distinct phenomena, such as vector "divergence" with CD8+ T cell activation and "streams" with spatially segmented stages of the anti-tumor immune response. Together, these data support a role for CytoRipple in revealing a dynamic layer across spatial multi-omic platforms.

Unsupervised source separation and clustering of soundscape recordings predicts avian richness in hyperdiverse systems

Hopping, W. A., Srinivasan, S., +5 authors, Barlow, J.

Abstract: 1: Passive Acoustic Monitoring (PAM) has emerged as a leading approach for biodiversity research, but analysing the vast quantities of data produced by this method remains challenging. Acoustic indices are often employed to estimate species richness in soundscape recordings, but their generalisability and overall efficacy in speciose regions has increasingly been challenged. An alternative, multi-species classification models, still suffer from performance limitations, particularly in the tropics. 2: We introduce an unsupervised approach that estimates avian species richness using an integrated source separation model trained on birds (BirdMixIT), a leading bird vocalization embedding model (Perch 2.0), and hierarchical agglomerative clustering. We count the clusters of embeddings generated from separated channels to estimate the species richness in recordings at differing timescales. 3: We test the generalisability and predictive performance of our approach, "source-clustering", using a large combined dataset featuring 103 total hours of manually-annotated soundscape recordings from the Amazon rainforest in Peru and Brazil. To compare the performance of source-clustering to existing approaches, we estimated species richness in the same recordings with both traditional acoustic indices and Perch classifications at various confidence thresholds. 4: Our source-clustering method performed consistently well between our separate datasets. Source-clustering performed better for predicting avian species richness than traditional acoustic indices at all levels, and was complementary with Perch, performing similarly overall to its best settings combinations, though it was more robust for short recordings, and should be more resilient in locations with less training data. Source-clustering generally predicted species richness better than the best-performing Perch settings at the 15-second, 1-minute, and 15-minute temporal resolutions, while most settings of Perch performed better at the 1-hour resolution. 5: Source-clustering can complement or exceed the performance of presently established methods for estimating avian species richness from soundscape recordings, namely traditional acoustic indices and the out-of-box versions of leading automated identification modes. This is particularly true at narrow temporal resolutions, in systems where training data is scarce, and where species density is high enough to generate significant overlapping vocalisations, rendering it especially useful in hyperdiverse tropical ecosystems where other methods for interpreting soundscape recordings face persistent challenges.

OFC-SV: Interpreting structural variants in African and Asian cohorts with non-syndromic orofacial clefts

Chen, K., Chung, Y., +2 authors, Shin, D.-G.

Abstract: OFC-SV is a web-based data portal designed to help interpret structural variants (SVs) in non-syndromic orofacial clefts genomic data published by The Gabriella Miller Kids First Pediatric Research Program. The portal offers a user-friendly way to explore 41 novel SV genes (of these 13 sharing regulatory perturbations already reported in the literature as SNV genes) potentially responsible for non-syndromic OFC (nsOFC) from a cohort of children of African and Asian ancestry. A salient feature of the portal is its unique ability to capture epigenomic disruptions and complex oligogenic interactions in visualized regulatory pathways, thus advancing the frontier of disease etiology through convenient translation of computational signals into biological interpretations.

LongVerse: verified AI agents for Nanopore and PacBio DNA methylation analysis

Liu, Y., Zhang, Q., Li, S.

Abstract: AI agents now run sequencing pipelines, but their outputs are checked by proxy, with rubrics or language-model judges. LongVerse turns one English sentence into haplotype-resolved DNA methylation from Nanopore or PacBio reads, through agents that read the data, supply arguments to a pinned pipeline and never write code. On HG002, a healthy reference genome, agent and human expert produced byte-identical calls (r = 1.000) that agreed with bisulfite sequencing (r = 0.943, 0.884), at $0.12 to $0.54 of conversation per run. A consensus agent that admits any per-site caller reduced error 10.7 percent against the best published caller.

CROSS-PLATFORM CONCORDANCE ANALYSIS REVEALS ROBUST AND CLINICALLY RELEVANT GENE SIGNATURES IN BREAST CANCER

Imhanbor, J. O., Fatai, A.

Abstract: Abstract Background: Breast cancer is a molecularly heterogeneous disease characterized by diverse transcriptional programs across tumor subtypes. RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) has become a central tool for profiling these alterations; however, variability in data processing pipelines can introduce inconsistencies in downstream analyses, limiting the identification of robust and reproducible gene signatures. Methods: We implemented a three-phase integrative framework to identify high-confidence gene signatures in breast cancer. The framework comprised: (i) a discovery phase involving differential expression analysis across two independent RNA-seq workflows; a STAR-counts pipeline applied to a balanced TCGA-BRCA cohort (30 tumor vs. 30 normal samples) processed via TCGAbiolinks, and a Salmon-based pseudo-alignment pipeline applied to an independent SRA-derived dataset (3 tumor vs. 3 normal samples) processed from raw sequencing reads; (ii) a cross-dataset concordance phase identifying consensus upregulated genes through gene symbol intersection analysis; and (iii) a validation and clinical characterisation phase comprising large-scale validation in the full TCGA-BRCA cohort (n = 1,093 tumors, 112 normal samples), functional enrichment, PAM50 subtype stratification, survival analysis, and cancer cell line expression profiling. A multi-criteria scoring framework was applied to prioritize potential drug targets. Results: A total of 666 genes were consistently upregulated across both discovery datasets, indicating strong cross-platform concordance. Of these, 502 genes (93%) were validated as upregulated in the full TCGA cohort, demonstrating high reproducibility. Functional enrichment analyses revealed dominant involvement in cell cycle regulation, chromosomal segregation, and DNA repair pathways. Subtype analysis showed a marked enrichment of these genes in Basal-like and HER2-enriched tumors, highlighting their association with more aggressive disease phenotypes. Survival analysis identified a subset of genes significantly associated with poor overall survival, while cell line validation confirmed expression in breast cancer models. Integration of these features enabled the prioritization of candidate genes with potential therapeutic relevance. Conclusion: This study demonstrates that integrating two independent RNA-seq analytical workflows (a STAR-counts pipeline applied to a balanced TCGA-BRCA discovery cohort and a Salmon-based pipeline applied to an independent SRA-derived dataset) enables the identification of robust and clinically meaningful gene signatures in breast cancer. The high concordance observed across these independent datasets underscores the reproducibility of the identified signals, while downstream analyses highlight their biological and translational relevance. This framework provides a systematic approach for prioritizing candidate biomarkers and therapeutic targets in cancer transcriptomics.

MIEN: a deep-learning model for pocket-conditioned glycan pose and conformation discrimination

Huang, X., Tian, Z.

Abstract: In protein-carbohydrate interaction, Molecular docking can generate many glycan poses that appear plausible, but the top-ranked pose may differ from the experimentally observed conformation. We introduce Minimal Interface Equivariant Network (MIEN), a structure-based equivariant graph network that scores the compatibility of a glycan pose with a fixed protein pocket. The model operates on a minimal-interface graph that contains only protein atoms forming polar or CH-pai contacts with the glycan; protein and sugar residue names are excluded. MIEN was trained on 5,241 experimental complexes and one unified set of contact-preserving rigid-body negatives grouped into three semantic-contact tiers. On a protein-disjoint benchmark of 260 held-out complexes, MIEN separated experimental poses from non-native poses and ranked experimental poses more highly within matched pockets. We then evaluated MIEN on AutoDock-GPU and GlycanDock candidate pools generated independently for the same complexes. Across three training seeds, MIEN selected lower-RMSD top-1 poses more often than either docking engine and increased the number of best poses below 1, 2 and 4 Angstrom. These results provide a practical route to more realistic protein-carbohydrate complex poses and a stronger basis for structural and mechanistic studies of protein-carbohydrate interactions.

Metabolite-centric Knowledge Graph Unveils Predictive and Mechanistic Insights through Self-supervised Multimodal Learning

Lu, Y., Guan, X., +4 authors, Wang, J.

Abstract: Metabolomics research is advancing toward a comprehensive view of metabolites and their biological functions, yet conventional, dictionary-like databases fall short of presenting these complexities. To overcome this limitation, we developed MetaKG, a metabolite-centric knowledge graph that integrates diverse chemical, biological, and semantic data, coupled with a global self-supervised multimodal learning framework that generates advanced embeddings (MetaKGE) for both predictive and mechanistic analyses. MetaKG outperforms existing models in standard representation learning metrics and across 12 external datasets. Furthermore, MetaKG refines existing database information by detecting misannotated labels and uncovering previously overlooked biochemical relationships. Its predictive capability naturally yields testable biochemical hypotheses, as demonstrated in studies of G6PD deficiency and colorectal cancer. By transforming passive, database-scale information into extractable knowledge and actionable insights, MetaKG paves the way for hypothesis-driven discovery in metabolomics and holds promise for informing future research in clinical biomarker identification and therapeutic development.

RDEmbed: A Sentence-Embedding Framework for Rare Disease Phenotype Representation and Phenotypic Similarity Analysis

Wang, X., Wang, J., +2 authors, Harmanci, A.

Abstract: Accurate phenotypic characterization of patients with rare diseases is essential for improving diagnosis, patient matchmaking, and gene discovery. Conventional ontology driven approaches based on the Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO) enable structured phenotype comparison but often limited by the incomplete annotations and reduced ability to capture the contextual information contained in free-text clinical narratives. We present RDEmbed, a sentence embedding framework that learns patient phenotype representations directly from rare disease case reports for similarity analysis. RDEmbed encodes narrative case reports into high-dimensional vectors to quantify phenotypic similarity. Unlike ontology-based approaches that rely on manually curated HPO annotations, RDEmbed encodes narrative case reports into dense semantic embeddings that capture complex phenotypic relationships from unstructured text. We further introduced retrieval-augmented embedding refinement (RAER), which enhances embedding representations by incorporating external domain knowledge at inference time without task-specific fine-tuning. RDEmbed was evaluated against HPO-based similarity methods (OWLSim, simGIC) using simulated datasets, published rare disease case reports and a curated case study dataset comprising 156 patient abstracts presenting six rare disease groups. Across the real world datasets and curated cohort. RDEmbed consistently outperformed ontology based approaches, improving AUROC and AUPRC by more than 5%. Embedding-based representations also produced more distinct disease-specific clustering in t-SNE visualizations and identified twice as many true positive rare disease pairs as ontology based methods (8 versus 4). Incorporating RAER further improved precision while maintaining high sensitivity compared with zero-shot embeddings. These results demonstrate that sentence embeddings provide an effective alternative to ontology-based phenotype representations by learning directly from free-text clinical narratives. RDEmbed enables more accurate phenotypic similarity analysis while reducing dependence on manually curated HPO annotations and offers a scalable framework for rare disease diagnosis, patient matchmaking, and gene discovery. RDEmbed is publicly available at https://github.com/xhwang2017/RDEmbed.

Proteome scale drug prioritization from AlphaFold 3 interaction confidence

Akabari, R., Mangione, W., +1 author, Samudrala, R.

Abstract: AlphaFold 3 (AF3) predicts protein ligand complexes, but the utility of its confidence scores for drug prioritization remains unclear. We benchmarked AF3 interaction scores against BANDOCK+, an experimentally informed approach, using approved drugs and human protein libraries. AF3 ipTM achieved similar aggregate disease ranking performance to BANDOCK+, with differences among individual indications. These results support the evaluation of AF3 interaction confidence as a source of information for proteome scale drug prioritization.

Emerging Role of AI in Single-Cell Sequencing Toward Biomarker Discovery, Therapeutic Target Identification and Precision Medicine in Lung Cancer.

P K Fasna, Reshma Krishnan, Ravindra Kumar

Abstract: Lung cancer, the most common malignancy worldwide, exists in numerous histological subtypes, rendering the diagnosis and prognosis of the disease a challenge to clinicians. The recent technological breakthroughs in single-cell sequencing (SCS) and Artificial Intelligence (AI) have expanded the existing knowledge of lung cancer biology and provided new opportunities in the area of precision medicine. SCS has the potential to expose the enormous complexity of tumors, showing heterogeneous cell populations and characterizing the tumor microenvironment, and thereby overcoming the inherent limitations of conventional bulk sequencing in resolving tumor heterogeneity. At the same time, AI's advanced machine learning (ML) and predictive modeling capabilities, when applied to SCS data, can facilitate the identification of subtle cellular states, dynamic cellular interactions, and complex molecular pathways often undetectable by traditional statistical methods. This review examines the emerging applications of SCS and AI in lung cancer, focusing on biomarker discovery, therapeutic target identification, and precision medicine. We outline how their integration may support the identification of new biomarkers and therapeutic targets, with implications for more personalized treatment approaches. Integration of single-cell biology knowledge with AI-based analytics may assist scientists and clinicians in addressing the shortcomings of traditional approaches, which may offer personalized therapies with the potential to improve patient care. The ultimate goal of this review is to address the challenges and future perspectives of this emerging trend by focusing on the potential of these technologies to fulfill the unmet needs in lung cancer diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment selection.
