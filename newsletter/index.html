European LD proxies lose tagging for 43% of GWAS leads in the African panel: a chromosome 22 analysis with SwiftLD, an 826-fold faster, byte-identical vcftools LD

Christensen, J.

Abstract: SwiftLD is a drop-in replacement for the pairwise linkage disequilibrium (LD) modes of vcftools 0.1.17, --hap-r2 and --geno-r2, which population-genetic pipelines use to compute r2, D and D' between nearby variants. On three regions of 1000 Genomes chromosome 22 with 503 to 2,504 samples it wrote the same output as vcftools, byte for byte, and was 826-fold faster (geometric mean at two threads on an AVX-512 CPU). It was also 7.6-fold faster than PLINK 2, the fastest alternative, from VCF input to LD output on all five regions tested; PLINK 2 writes a different file, although its r2 values agree with vcftools' within 10^-6. One megabase with 503 samples took vcftools 29 s and SwiftLD 0.049 s, and on an ordinary AVX2 laptop SwiftLD was 102-fold faster than vcftools at 12 threads. vcftools was slow because it repeatedly re-reads genotypes from a temporary file, counts haplotypes individually and flushes every output row. I built SwiftLD with correctness-gated coding agents (Claude Opus 5.5): every change had to keep the output byte-identical to vcftools. No output byte differed in 167,749 differential tests on three machines. On chromosome 22, the best European LD proxy of a GWAS lead kept r2 >= 0.8 for 57.4% of leads in the African panel and 85.6% to 92.7% in other panels (98.1% in a European split-half control). Code and a two-command reproduction: https://github.com/joe-b-christensen/swiftld.

Language-guided domain adaptation improves protein sequence rep-resentations for antimicrobial resistance analysis

Ye, R., Li, L., +1 author, Song, S.

Abstract: Antimicrobial resistance (AMR), particularly antibiotic resistance in bacterial pathogens, complicates infectious disease management. Because bacterial resistance is often mediated by protein functions, including antibiotic inactivation and efflux, protein sequence analysis provides a molecular basis for digital AMR surveillance and therapeutic intervention. We developed BacLLM, a language guided protein representation framework that integrates annotation-derived functional knowledge into protein language model representations for AMR analysis in WHO priority bacterial pathogens. Through sequence enhancement, semantic enrichment of UniProt annotations, and cross modal contrastive learning, BacLLM transfers LLM encoded annotation priors into the sequence encoder. Compared with baselines, BacLLM provided more informative representations for resistance-gene identification and mechanism prediction, captured resistance associated mutational signals at single-residue resolution, and preserved taxonomic relationships not reflected by phenotype based clinical groupings. Moreover, BacLLM recovered resistance associated functional organization within a TolC-centered protein association network, prioritizing proteins with low sequence identity to TolC but predicted structural similarity to TolC. Extending from AMR characterization to therapeutic prioritization, BacLLM also improved strain-level phage host matching for phage therapy candidate prioritization. Together, these findings establish BacLLM as a protein representation framework supporting AMR surveillance, functional characterization, and therapeutic candidate prioritization.

Protify: A Benchmarking Platform Revealing Task Dependence and Homology Leakage in Protein Language Model Evaluation

Rafailidis, N., Hallee, L. P., +3 authors, Gleghorn, J. P.

Abstract: Protein Language Models (pLMs) have become essential tools for computational protein analysis, and the rapid proliferation of architectures, training objectives, and pretraining corpora has produced a diverse and expanding model landscape. Evaluation practices, however, remain fragmented: studies use different datasets, preprocessing pipelines, and reporting conventions, so results rarely transfer between publications. Here we introduce Protify, an open-source platform that standardizes pLM benchmarking across zero-shot, supervised, and transferlearning settings through a unified, low-code interface. Protify currently supports 35 pretrained models and 61 curated datasets and it is easy to add custom versions of each. Leveraging Protify's high throughput pipelines, we benchmarked 23 models on ProteinGym, discovering that DPLM3B is currently the top-performing self-supervised encoder. Another large-scale supervised evaluation across 32 diverse tasks, spanning enzyme classification, gene ontology annotation, protein-protein interaction, subcellular localization, and other functional properties, found that no single pLM dominated: 14 different models took the top score on at least one dataset, and the margins between the best and second-best models fell within run-to-run variability. On enzyme optimal pH prediction, a general-purpose probe pipeline matched or exceeded EpHod, a specialized model selected from 11,550 training runs, after 50 hyperparameter trials (R2 = 0.482 versus 0.425). Under a homology-controlled split of the same data, every pooled-embedding probe collapsed to near-zero R2, and only a transformer probe over full residue embeddings recovered appreciable signal (R2 = 0.248). Model rankings therefore depend as much on evaluation design as on the models themselves. Protify makes those design choices explicit, controlled, and replayable, and is freely available at https://github.com/Gleghorn-Lab/Protify.

Dated public evidence and known genes for retrospective evaluation of gene prioritization

Huang, C.-H., Wang, Y.

Abstract: Retrospective evaluation of gene prioritization needs evidence that was public before the evaluated genes became known. Public resources are open and reproducible, but are distributed as current releases that also contain evidence produced after the genes became known. This Data Descriptor presents per-gene evidence tables for four areas (oncology, inflammatory bowel disease, metabolism and psoriasis) from public resources. Every feature carries a time axis, the date of its release in a calendar of 183 archived releases of 20 sources, and a leakage axis, a provenance class (A to E) with two risk grades that define five feature sets. Nineteen lists of known genes are dated by the first trial, study, release or classification that supports each gene. They cover targets of a kind of drug, targets of drugs tested in a disease, and genes associated with a disease. The oncology lists also carry the first day the trial registry showed the trial.

SPECHTacle: An Efficient Detection and Visualization of Cell Specific Regulation in Single Cell-Based Experiments Using a Novel Combinatorial Computational Tool

Specht, K. M., Schwierling, H. C., Robson, M. J.

Abstract: Herein, we describe SPECHTacle, a novel computational tool affording the ability to efficiently visualize and delineate the specific cellular population(s) impacted by an experimental paradigm utilizing heterogeneous cell-based samples. SPECHTacle can be employed to efficiently determine, in a cell specific manner, the populations preferentially impacted by treatments, drugs, injuries, disease states, and/or development within datasets continuously being generated across nearly countless subfields in molecular biology. SPECHTacle accomplishes this task by harnessing the power of Waddington optimal transport analysis and Shannon entropy in a combinatorial fashion to distinguish cell populations preferentially altered due to nearly any experimental paradigm and generates a visual representation of cellular entropy (i.e. change) across entire datasets. Ultimately, this allows for efficient determination of specific populations preferentially impacted by an experimental paradigm and identifies populations to target for downstream analyses employing quantitative measurements. We propose that SPECHTacle represents an immediately useful computational tool with the potential to be integrated into single cell-based workflows and greatly enhance the efficiency of both manual and machine-based learning workflows.

Provenance-aware handling of identical dengue genome records alters inferred envelope-protein variation in Bangladesh DENV-3

Begum, K. T., Rafea, K. A. G.

Abstract: The reliability of interpreting dengue virus (DENV) envelope (E) protein variations depends on whether identical public sequence records are kept as representatives or eliminated based on provenance reviews. We evaluated three record-handling strategies using a frozen Bangladesh-associated dataset of 426 E sequences: exact-sequence deduplication, a prespecified provenance-sensitive exclusion method, and an independently curated Nextstrain accession list. Within the 2021 DENV3 subset (96 accessions), 18 records dated August 2021 were identical across 10,707 nucleotides to the older Sri Lankan genome AY099336.1. There were 21 variable E positions within the original 96-accession DENV3 cohort. Whole-cohort deduplication (47 retained) and retaining one representative of the 18-record group (79 retained) preserved all 21 positions. Excluding the group (78 retained) resulted in eight variable positions: 13 of 21 (61.9%) were lost due to differences in state at those positions between the excluded group and retained cohort. Nextstrain reference-specific exclusions selected the same 18 records; applying its full saved list produced 77 accessions and the same eight variable positions. E270 and E383, which had pre-2021 antibody-related evidence, did not meet the sequence-variation criteria for follow-up; however, E223 and E224 remained variable. Antibody-position enrichment was not statistically significant. These results indicate that cohort eligibility decisions may impact dengue E-protein variant prioritization despite representative-retaining deduplication. Genome identity does not indicate specimen identity or an antigenic phenotype.

Classifying tumors from broad category to hierarchical molecular subtype: an interpretable hierarchical framework for precision oncology

Song, Q., Liu, F., Lai, X.

Abstract: Precise tumor subtype classification is important for guiding personalized treatment, characterizing tumor heterogeneity, and investigating molecular mechanisms. However, most existing multi-label classification models use flat architectures and do not fully exploit the inherent hierarchical relationships and biological constraints among tumor types, which limits their performance and interpretability in complex, fine-grained pan-cancer classification. Here, we present the cancer Hallmark-based Hierarchical Recursive Model (HHRM) for pan-cancer hierarchical multi-label classification. HHRM integrates hierarchical label structures with recursive neural networks, enabling simultaneous modeling of multi-level dependencies from broad cancer categories to specific subtypes. We trained the model on a comprehensive pan-cancer cohort of 11,476 samples spanning 26 primary cancer types and 405 subtypes across up to eight hierarchical levels. HHRM achieved an accuracy of 0.7924 for predicting hierarchical tumor subtypes and was further evaluated on a validation dataset of 887 samples. Compared with other hierarchical modeling frameworks, HHRM achieved higher accuracy in all three output modes. Ablation experiments showed that replacing the hallmark-based graph convolutional network reduced model performance. On an independent dataset of 1,608 breast cancer samples, HHRM also achieved an accuracy greater than 0.86. Finally, we combined SHAP with a network-based method to rank and analyze predictive genes at different levels of the tumor hierarchy, which identified both known and potentially novel biomarkers for cancer diagnosis. In conclusion, to our knowledge, HHRM is the first hierarchical model for multi-level tumor subtype classification. Its competitive performance, combined with post hoc biological interpretation, suggests potential value for precision cancer diagnosis.

TIGRdisco: a tool for the discovery of TIGR arrays and TIGR-associated Tas proteins from nucleotide sequences

Gu, S., Gong, Z., Mortimer, J.

Abstract: Tandem Interspaced Guide RNA (TIGR)-TIGR-associated protein (Tas) systems mediate DNA targeting through a hypercompact Tas effector, guided by dual spacers within a tigRNA that is processed from TIGR arrays. TIGR-Tas loci are composed of a Tas coding sequence associated with TIGR arrays of short units in either dual-repeat or stem-loop architecture. Unlike CRISPR (Clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats) arrays that comprise units of direct repeats interspersed with unique spacers, units within the two distinct TIGR architectures only conform through extremely short box motifs, complicating detection. Here, we present TIGRdisco, an integrated tool capable of discovering both the Tas protein and its associated TIGR arrays directly from unannotated nucleotide sequences. TIGRdisco implements independent engines to detect the two array architectures through a motif-agnostic approach. We benchmarked TIGRdisco against a reference dataset of TIGR-Tas loci, recovering more than 80% of array units within its search window. Furthermore, TIGRdisco recovered arrays for loci within entire clades of Tas where associated arrays were unannotated in the reference. We found that these arrays largely contain non-canonical box motifs or unit lengths. Lastly, as a case study, we utilized TIGRdisco and successfully uncovered TIGR-Tas loci across metagenomic and genomic datasets.

A protein language model for domain, functional site, and variant effect prediction

Wang, P., Ahsan, M. U., +1 author, Wang, K.

Abstract: Protein sequence databases are growing far faster than experimentally curated annotations, limiting functional interpretation of newly identified proteins and coding variants. We developed PALM, a protein annotation language model that jointly predicts domain and functional-site occurrence, while preserving overlapping and nested annotations through a soft-target objective. PALM integrates ESM2 with low-rank adaptation (LoRA), learned layer aggregation, and a bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) encoder. PALM was trained on ~15 million InterPro-annotated UniRef50 sequences spanning 17,951 domain and 1,383 site classes. Across 2.3 million held-out proteins, PALM achieved domain- and site-occurrence F1 scores of 0.941 and 0.877, respectively, and retained substantial performance in proteins with <20% sequence identity to the training and validation data. PALM also substantially outperformed ESM3 annotation baseline. Without training on variant-pathogenicity labels, PALM embedding disruption distinguished 151,823 ClinVar missense variants more accurately than ESM2 embedding disruption (AUROC 0.835 versus 0.710), and combining PALM embedding disruption with the ESM2 likelihood-ratio signal reached an AUROC of 0.877. The same combined zero-shot score outperformed AlphaMissense on a benchmark dataset on 128,161 missense variants, that were derived from literature but not present in ClinVar database. At the protein level, PALM embeddings showed stronger CATH-Gene3D superfamily organization than frozen ESM2 and ESM3 embeddings, and improved prediction of experimental activation and repression effects when combined with ESM2. In a downstream case study, we demonstrated the ability to use PALM-derived embedding as proxy of deep mutational scanning (DMS), showcasing the broad application of PALM. Together, PALM converts curated residue-level knowledge into transferable and mechanistically interpretable representations that connect protein sequence, function, and variant consequence.

Harnessing catalytic knowledge to guide enzyme discovery and evolution with AlphaEnzyme

Liu, Y., Zheng, S.

Abstract: Retrieving an enzyme for a target reaction provides a starting point for evolution, but predicting how to improve it remains difficult. This requires connecting evolutionary information with the structural and chemical context of catalysis. Here we present AlphaEnzyme, an enzyme foundation model built on a structure-grounded representation of an enzyme paired with its reaction, from which catalytic compatibility, kinetics and mutational response are read out together. On a time-separated benchmark, AlphaEnzyme achieves a top-1 catalyst retrieval success rate of 30.1%, against 17.2% for the strongest baseline. Michaelis constant predictions achieve the highest rank correlations among the compared methods, reaching 0.64 for enzymes sharing at most 40% identity with the kinetic training set. The model also enriches high-fitness TrpB variants at a budget of 16 candidates without target-specific fitness training. In prospective transaminase engineering, several prioritized variants exceed the parent, with the best design yielding a 30.4-fold increase in product signal in a lysate-based assay while retaining (R)-selectivity. Together, AlphaEnzyme provides a common molecular basis for selecting starting enzymes and prioritizing sequence changes for their improvement.

BNCT Triggers Immunogenic Cell Death in B16 Melanoma Without Accompanying T-Cell Infiltration--Implications for Combination Immunotherapy

Quan, R., Huang, H., +7 authors, Gu, L.

Abstract: Objective: Boron neutron capture therapy (BNCT) is a targeted radiotherapy with highly selective tumor killing; its potential to induce immunogenic cell death (ICD) and modulate the tumor immune microenvironment has attracted attention. However, whether BNCT-induced immune activation can overcome the T-cell exclusion barrier of immunologically "cold" tumors and translate into intratumoral effector T-cell infiltration remains unclear. Methods: A B16 melanoma model was established in C57BL/6 mice. Mice were assigned to CK, N10/N20 (neutron irradiation alone for 10 or 20 min), BPA, and BNCTN10/BNCTN20 groups (BPA 500 mg/kg plus 10 or 20 min irradiation) to evaluate tumor suppression and survival. In a separate cohort (CK, N20, and BNCTN20), the most efficacious regimen (BNCTN20) was compared with CK on days 3 and 7: H&E staining; immunohistochemistry/immunofluorescence for gamma-H2AX, HMGB1, cleaved caspase-3, PD-L1, CD8+, and granzyme B; ELISA for cytokines and chemokines; and ICP-OES for 10B distribution. Results: BNCT significantly suppressed tumor growth. When CK tumors reached the humane endpoint (~2000 mm3), N20 tumor volume was half that of CK (TGI ~ 49.8%), whereas BNCTN20 reached only one tenth (TGI ~ 88.9%, p < 0.001), with prolonged survival (p < 0.001). BNCT induced gamma-H2AX-associated DNA damage, HMGB1 release, and cleaved caspase-3-mediated apoptosis; upregulated PD-L1; induced a Th1-type response (elevated IFN-gamma/TNF-alpha, reduced IL-10); and enhanced CXCL9/10 signaling. However, intratumoral CD8+ T-cell infiltration and granzyme B expression did not increase, indicating that this activation was not translated into a local effector T-cell response. Conclusions: BNCT inhibited tumor growth and prolonged survival while inducing ICD and a systemic Th1 response; nevertheless, systemic activation failed to translate into local CD8+ T-cell infiltration, while PD-L1 was upregulated, a "strong direct killing but blocked immune translation" profile in the B16 cold tumor. PD-L1-associated adaptive immune resistance may limit this conversion, providing rationale for combining BNCT with immune checkpoint blockade.

Targeting NF-κB prevents Schwann cell reprogramming and plexiform neurofibroma initiation in mouse model of Nf1

Ravindran, R., Youn, E.-K., +10 authors, Ratner, N.

Abstract: Plexiform neurofibromas arise in approximately half of all individuals with Neurofibromatosis Type I (NF1). Genetic lesions apart from NF1 pathogenic variants are not present in NF1 mutant Schwann cells, suggesting that tumorigenesis may instead require cellular rewiring. To identify putatively re-wired tumor cells, we used multiplex imaging and flow cytometry to delineate markers that distinguish tumor Schwann cell from non-tumor Schwann cells. We identified tumor Schwann cells as CD44+;CD49f+ cells that show increased transcription of epithelial to mesenchymal plasticity (EMP)- associated genes. CD44+;CD49f+ expression was induced by treating Nf1-/- Schwann cells with stressors or by direct NF-{kappa}B pathway activation via IKK2; treated Schwann cells increased secretion of NF-{kappa}B targets and immune cell chemoattractants CCL2 and CCL3. In established tumors, MEK inhibition plus NF-{kappa}B blockade diminished tumor cell proliferation, tumor Schwann cells, immune cell populations, tumor cytokines and tumor number. Notably, transient single agent NF-{kappa}B pathway blockade was sufficient to significantly delay tumor formation in mice. Thus, Nf1 loss in Schwann cells is followed by NF-{kappa}B activation and cellular reprogramming that drives tumor initiation and targeting NF-{kappa}B activation can reduce NF1-deficient tumor formation.

Integrative miRNA-multi-omics analysis of brain-metastasis predisposition in non-small cell lung cancer prioritizes HDAC inhibitors and anti-miR-330-3p as hypothesis-generating therapeutic candidates.

Fahad Hassan Shah, Jung Sig Cho, +6 authors, Hyung-Joo Chung

Abstract: Brain metastasis (BM) is the leading cause of death in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), yet its molecular drivers remain poorly targeted. Here we present an integrative in-silico pipeline that moves from miRNA discovery to therapeutic hypothesis generation. Reprocessing the GSE23019 miRNA microarray cohort (7 BM+ versus 5 BM- primary tumors) recovered the published BM-associated miRNAs miR-328 and miR-330-3p and, through validated miRNA-target interactions, yielded a signature of 828 up-regulated and 177 down-regulated genes enriched for extracellular-matrix and vasculature programs. Resampling analyses show this discovery-cohort signal is directionally reproducible but statistically fragile (no probe passes FDR control; permutation P = 0.29), so we carry it forward only as a seed for independent testing. In 519 TCGA lung adenocarcinomas, multi-omics factor analysis linked the signature to a latent stromal factor whose association with worse overall survival persisted after adjustment for stage, age, sex and smoking (adjusted HR 1.26 per SD, 95% CI 1.07-1.48), and miRNA-target anti-correlation nominated miR-330-3p-> VEGFA as the strongest effector axis (Spearman rho = -0.37). Connectivity mapping against ~ 693,000 LINCS L1000 signatures identified histone deacetylase (HDAC) inhibition as the only enriched reversal mechanism (odds ratio 30.7, FDR = 0.0019). Blood-brain-barrier and lipophilicity machine-learning models ranked panobinostat first among 30 candidates, and anti-miR-330-3p is the prioritized miRNA therapeutic. Single-cell analysis of 208,506 lung-cancer transcriptomes and spatial co-localization across 13 NSCLC pucks mapped the program to cancer-associated fibroblasts, indicating the bulk signature tracks stromal composition. Knowledge-graph propagation recovered HDAC1 and HDAC2 among top-ranked genes, though sensitivity analyses show this convergence partly reflects hub structure. All nominations are hypothesis-generating and require experimental validation.

Integrative transcriptomic analysis identifies miR-642a-5p as a regulator of POFUT1 expression in colon cancer.

Oumaima Mazour, Mouna Ababou, +1 author, Agnès Germot

Abstract: Colorectal cancer (CRC) is one of the most common and deadly cancers worldwide, underscoring the urgent need for novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets. Protein O-fucosyltransferase 1 (POFUT1) is increasingly recognized as an oncogenic driver in CRC, but the upstream regulatory mechanisms responsible for its overexpression remain poorly understood. We implemented an integrative, multi-step bioinformatics framework that combined multiple linear regression with stepwise selection, penalized regression models (LASSO and Elastic Net), Random Forest analysis for non-linear feature evaluation and correlation analysis. Five miRNAs (miR-92b, miR-484, miR-574, miR-642a, miR-3940) showing a consistent inverse association with POFUT1 across these complementary methods, were retained as final candidates. Among them, miR-642a exhibited the strongest negative correlation with POFUT1 and was then chosen to test its impact on POFUT1 expression in colon cancer cell lines. Overexpression of miR-642a-5p, through mimic transfection, significantly reduced POFUT1 transcript levels by approximately 46 % in HCT 116, 50 % in SW480, and 22 % in SW620 cells relative to controls. Dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed direct binding of miR-642a-5p to two predicted sites within the POFUT1 3'UTR, resulting in a decrease of luciferase activity by 40 or 50 % according to the site. Our study combines bioinformatics screening based on multiple linear regression and machine learning models, with experimental validation, to identify a miRNA related to POFUT1 expression. This approach deserves to be extended to the other candidate miRNAs, which, combined with POFUT1, could serve as a basis for future diagnostic or therapeutic strategies in CRC.
