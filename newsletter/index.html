SpectroBird: Event-Level Vocalization Detection and Curation for Avian Ecology

Dreyer, E. G., Gokhale, P., +9 authors, Freeman, B.

Abstract: Passive acoustic monitoring often summarizes long recordings as fixed-window presence predictions, but many ecological questions require records of individual vocal events: how many occurred, when they occurred, whether they overlapped, how long they lasted, what frequency band they occupied, and whether each event was complete enough for structural measurement. We address this event-level measurement problem with SpectroBird, a spectrogram-based method for localizing and curating avian vocalizations in noisy field recordings. SpectroBird first applies a detection-localization model to identify candidate vocalizations as time-frequency boxes. An event-consolidation step merges repeated detections from overlapping windows while preserving overlapping vocalizations as separate events. A completeness-curation model then classifies each consolidated event as complete or incomplete before downstream event-level analysis. We demonstrate SpectroBird on breeding-season Hume's Leaf Warbler buzz vocalizations recorded in the Himalaya across a 3,200-3,800 m elevational gradient. Under shared window-level scoring, the detection-localization model remained competitive with AST and YOLOv11-cls baselines while retaining event timing and frequency bounds; event-level evaluation achieved AP > 0.999 for complete buzzes. Applied to two field seasons, the resulting event records showed that duration of complete buzzes declined with elevation, complete-buzz occurrence showed no simple elevation trend, and relative day-of-year occurrence patterns were strongly correlated between years. These results show how event-level detection and curation can support ecological analyses that depend on individual vocalizations rather than fixed-window presence alone.

SLAFIT: Viral fitness inference and structural variant detection from TGS data

Abdullah, S. M. U., Cai, D., +2 authors, Sun, Y.

Abstract: Evolution is driven by genetic variation at multiple scales, from single-nucleotide variants (SNVs) to structural variants (SVs). Within populations, nonrandom associations among SNVs, quantified by linkage disequilibrium (LD), must be considered when inferring fitness from sequencing data. Characterizing SVs, SNVs, and the LD between SNVs therefore provides complementary insights into the genetic basis of evolutionary change. Although third-generation sequencing technologies facilitate the characterization of genomic variation, finite read lengths often limit the resolution of LD across entire genomes. Here, we present SLAFIT, a pipeline for linkage-aware fitness inference and SV detection from third-generation sequencing data. The pipeline reconstructs genome-wide haplotypes and their frequencies from third-generation sequencing reads to infer SNVs and estimate LD. When time-series data are available, it also infers fitness. An established SV detection tool is incorporated into the same workflow. Simulations support accurate LD and fitness inference under tested conditions, with reduced computation time through parallelization. Applications to enterovirus C104 and herpes simplex virus 1 reveal selection signals in predicted epitopes and recover a known inversion, respectively. Together, these results demonstrate the utility of SLAFIT for studying viral evolution through complementary analyses of fitness and structural variation.

The (implicit) force is with you: an application of the implicit function theorem for automatic protein force fields reparametrization

tamby, s., Bujupaj, A., orlando, g.

Abstract: Molecular dynamics simulations are powerful tools for studying protein behavior, yet their accuracy remains limited by the approximations of molecular force fields and the difficulty of determining their parameters. Moreover, existing force fields are largely optimized for globular proteins and can perform poorly on structurally distinct systems. Developing a universal force field that accurately describes all existing protein structures may be beyond reach with current physical models. Instead, we propose shifting generality from the force field itself to the method used to assign its parameters, enabling systematic reparameterization for restricted, structurally homogeneous classes of proteins. We formulate this task as a bilevel optimization problem and solve it using the implicit function theorem. We demonstrate this approach by reparameterizing AMBER for amyloid fibrils, optimizing its parameters to improve agreement with experimentally determined structures and enhance fibril stability during molecular dynamics simulations.

Benchmarking Large Language Models for Assay-Aware Drug-Target Binding Affinity Prediction

Gupta, N., Verma, A., +1 author, Sethi, T.

Abstract: Machine learning models for molecular discovery often train on bioactivity data from heterogeneous assays, yet assay context is rarely explicitly represented. We investigate whether natural-language assay descriptions provide predictive information beyond molecular and protein representations. We develop ACL-DTI (Assay-aware Contrastive Learning-based Drug Target Interaction) and MAMMAL-AsAP, an assay-context extension of MAMMAL-DTI. Across five triplet-disjoint random splits, assay-aware models achieve competitive or improved performance over assay-agnostic baselines. Among the evaluated models, MAMMAL-AsAP achieves the highest (R^2) of 0.7198 +/- 0.0085. Masking experiments show that ACL-DTI is robust to incomplete assay descriptions, exhibiting the smallest reductions in R^2 among the evaluated models when masking the target name, assay technique, or both: 0.0228 +/- 0.0064, 0.0406 +/- 0.0113, and 0.0620 +/- 0.0190, respectively. These results indicate that assay descriptions contain predictive information not fully captured by molecular structures and protein sequences, and reveal architecture-dependent differences in how this information is exploited.

BioMINTREL expands biological problem solving through cross-disciplinary representations

Dong, J., Zhang, Y., +7 authors, Gao, X.

Abstract: Biological research agents often rely on familiar analytical methods, limiting the questions explored in existing data. We introduce BioMINTREL, an AI agent that finds methods from other disciplines and connects them to biological measurements. It can draw on relationships among datasets and complementary ideas from several methods to develop new questions and analyses. We evaluate it on BioMDC-Bench, which assesses 228 possible applications of computational methods to biological tasks. Compared with GPT-5.4 mini and Qwen3.6-35B, BioMINTREL improves F1 agreement with human experts by up to 45.3 percentage points and increases coverage of applications supported by studies published in subsequent years by up to 58.8 percentage points. Four case studies on real data go beyond previous analyses to reveal dose-specific variant effects, constraints on spatial cell communication and routes through perturbation-response states, and identify the sampling needed to test whether rare high-state cells predict later lineage size. These results highlight BioMINTREL's potential to expand questions in existing data and suggest directions for subsequent research.

ELiAH2: update of the atlas of E3 ligases in human tissues by integrating tissue-specificity and docking properties of ligands for targeted protein degradation

Paik, H., Seo, S.

Abstract: Targeted protein degradation (TPD), including proteolysis-targeting chimeras (PROTACs), requires coordinated selection of E3 ligases, target proteins, and ligands to achieve efficient and tissue-selective degradation. However, current TPD resources provide limited support for integrating tissue-specific transcriptomic evidence with structural compatibility during early candidate prioritization. Here, we present ELiAH2, an expanded database for transcriptome- and structure-guided prioritization of TPD candidates. ELiAH2 integrates GTEx RNA-seq profiles across 24 human tissues to characterize tissue-specific expression patterns of E3 ligases and target genes. Compared with the previous version, ELiAH, current update presented over 200% of expansion for the number of covered tissues and samples. Previously, E3 ligase-target gene associations were inferred using mutual information-based network analysis, enabling users to explore transcriptomic relationships. To further support structural evaluation, ELiAH2 provides large-scale docking datasets comprising 827,385 E3 ligase-target protein pairs and 7,800,521 protein-ligand docking models, including E3 ligase-ligand and target protein-ligand interactions. Docking scores, ranked interaction tables, gene and ligand identifiers, and downloadable PDB structures are incorporated into the web interface. By combining tissue specificity, E3-target association, protein-protein docking, and protein-ligand docking, ELiAH2 provides a comprehensive resource for narrowing the TPD search space and supporting rational degrader design. ELiAH2 is freely available at https://eliahdb.org.

MassBank Human: The annotated human metabolome

Matsuda, F., Hayasaka, R., +9 authors, Hirayama, A.

Abstract: Mass spectrometry-based metabolomics is essential for deciphering human metabolic functions and metabolite dark matter. However, the dark matter of the human metabolome is intricately hidden. Therefore, we developed MassBank Human, a curated library that distills 36,512,540 MS2 spectra from 76 public human datasets into 203,886 high-quality spectra via spectral averaging, recalibration, and filtering to achieve high mass accuracy. We further used three metabolite annotation tools and developed a Zipf-Bayesian rank aggregation framework to aggregate their rankings with false discovery rate control. This framework assigned molecular formulas, chemical classes, and planar structures to 55,564, 22,619, and 14,198 spectra, respectively. Approximately half of the annotated structures potentially originated from food, pollutants, or gut microbiota. In a metabolome analysis of colorectal cancer cells, MassBank Human doubled structural annotation coverage (10.6% to 21.2%), identifying acetylated polyamines as markers of metabolic rewiring. This community resource provides an annotated foundation for exploring human metabolomes.

The Evolving Bileome: A Pan-Repository Landscape of Expanded Bile Acid and Related Steroidal Signatures

Mannochio-Russo, H., Nelson, M. R., +18 authors, Dorrestein, P. C.

Abstract: Bile acids and related steroidal metabolites are among the most studied biomolecules, yet recent work has revealed far greater structural diversity than previously recognized, much of it shaped by the microbiome. We previously introduced a repository-scale bile acid MS/MS library built from queries targeting C24 bile acid fragmentation, limited to Orbitrap Q Exactive datasets within GNPS/MassIVE. Here we announce a major expansion of this library: a bile acid and steroidal MS/MS spectral library assembled across three major metabolomics repositories, now capturing C23, C24 (including alkamine), C26 (C-C bonded aceto derivatives), and C27 bile acids, as well as C28 bile alcohols. The expanded libraries comprise 640,141 unique MS/MS spectra, of which 120,293 (18.8%) match 2,529 reference spectra, leaving most of this steroidal chemical space structurally unassigned. Linked to harmonized pan-repository metadata, the library provides a starting point for classifying MS/MS spectra as likely bile acids or related steroids, with two example analyses illustrated here. This resource allows researchers to flag candidate bile acid and steroidal signatures in their own data for further characterization.

Mechanism-guided Discovery of Fluoroacetate Dehalogenase (FAcD) Variants for Enzymatic PFAS Defluorination

Ahmad, S., Forouzandeh, A., +3 authors, Nielsen, T. K.

Abstract: Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are persistent anthropogenic "forever chemicals" whose strong C-F bonds (~460-533 kJ mol-1) resist defluorination, and the recast EU Drinking Water Directive sets parametric values of 0.10 ug L-1 for the sum of 20 specified PFAS and 0.50 ug L-1 for total PFAS. Here, we present DeFluorX, a mechanism-first pipeline that uses Boltz-2 to co-fold 2,150 bacterial fluoroacetate dehalogenase (FAcD) variants with 26 PFAS and fluoroacetate, generating uniformly high model confidence (rho = +0.08) but poorly tracked catalytic competence. Thus, the complexes were ranked and tiered by SN2-defluorination geometry based on the known mechanism of control of FAcDs RPA1163 with fluoroacetate: Odelta...C-alpha distance and Odelta-C-alpha-F angle; relay; carboxylate clamp; F-stabiliser; F-cradle; and penalties for scissile bond strength, backside occlusion, beta-fluorination and bond multiplicity. The tiering identified eight complexes (med angle 173.2 degrees, distance 2.91 A) with a high probability of degradation, including Alphaproteobacterial enzymes, with mono-, di- and trifluoroacetate attacking at 172-178 degrees versus 163 degrees for the control. We performed WaterMap, restrained 1 us Desmond MD, MM-GBSA and scans of the three WaterMap-aware best MD frames per complex using B3LYP/6-31G** QM/MM. All 12 frames exhibited product-end C-F cleavage with barriers ranging from 1.1 to 11.8 kcal mol-1, and the departing fluoride carried -0.43 to -0.63 e. The Laboratory validation is ongoing.

Modulator Resolves Transcript-, Allele-, and Condition-associated RNA Modification Differences from Direct RNA Sequencing

Nelson, T. M., Goneos, M., +8 authors, Mason, C. E.

Abstract: Nanopore direct-RNA sequencing (DRS) reports RNA modifications on single molecules, but there is no comprehensive algorithm for processing, aligning, and differentiating base modifications across reads that are often 5'-truncated. Here we present Modulator, which assigns every read, truncated or not, to a fragmentform, defined by a shared transcript 3'-end and the splice-junctions upstream of it. Of note, Modulator builds fragmentforms from genomic alignments without a reference transcriptome, estimates per-base modification stoichiometry within each fragmentform, and classifies each stoichiometry difference as transcript-, allele-, or condition-dependent. In the control HG002 transcriptome, Modulator built 23,746 fragmentforms, with 15,620 candidate novel isoforms that carried poly(A) signals as often as previously annotated transcripts. Also, assigning 5'-truncated reads to fragmentforms nearly doubled the number of testable and differentially modified sites. m6A sites within 100 nucleotides (nt) of a splice junction or within 1 kb of a poly(A) site were less modified in fragmentforms that use that junction or poly(A) site, indicating that local transcript context shapes where m6A is deposited. From the same reads, Modulator called SNPs with 93% precision and no phasing errors against the Genome in a Bottle benchmark. It identified 131 SNPs that create or remove a modifiable nucleotide and 1,358 allele-specific modification differences, most involving SNPs more than 50 nt from the modified site. In cells infected with Zika virus, which remodels gene expression relative to uninfected cells, Modulator identified 1,253 transcript-independent modification stoichiometry changes. Of the m6A changes at sites measured by GLORI, 96% agreed in direction with single-nucleotide-resolution GLORI maps of the same samples. Thus, Modulator accurately separates changes in RNA modification from transcript-associated and allele-associated differences and can improve the utility of nanopore DRS. Availability and Implementation: Modulator is free and open source: https://github.com/Theo-Nelson/Modulator. It is written in Python and runs on Linux OS.
