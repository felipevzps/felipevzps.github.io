ARC-Impute: robust reference-guided spatial gene imputation through regularised cell-to-spot mapping

Han, E., Zhang, A., +1 author, Yang, J.

Abstract: Motivation: Reference-guided spatial imputation uses single-cell RNA sequencing references to predict genes that are unmeasured in spatial transcriptomics data. Limited shared-gene overlap can leave the mapping of reference cells to spatial locations ambiguous: different assignments may reconstruct observed genes similarly yet yield different imputed expression and cell-type estimates. This gives rise to local assignment ambiguity, source-target mismatch and reference-transfer instability. Results: We present ARC-Impute, which regularises cell-to-spot mapping through anchor-residual optimisation, target-guided reference curation and routing, and twin-panel consistency. Across 17 human and mouse datasets spanning spot-based and single-cell technologies, ARC-Impute ranked first for held-out Pearson correlation throughout and exceeded the dataset-specific baseline mean by 47.3% on average. It also achieved the strongest spot-level cell-type composition recovery across three pseudospot benchmarks and retained leading accuracy and consistency under reference and training-gene perturbations. Cross-validated predictions revealed systematic differences in gene imputability. Availability and implementation: Code and workflows at https://github.com/ethanhaan/arc-impute.

Proteome-scale inference of transcription factor binding motifs from predicted protein-DNA structures

Iovino, B., Bulaong, N., +8 authors, Royer, L. A.

Abstract: Transcription factors (TFs) control development and cell identity by binding sequence-specific DNA motifs, yet the binding specificities of most TFs remain unknown because experimental binding assays do not scale. In zebrafish, only one-third of TFs have assigned motifs, nearly all inferred by homology from human or mouse experimental data. Here we present a proteome-scale computational screen that infers DNA-binding specificity from predicted protein-DNA structures. We use AlphaFold3 to model zebrafish TFs with candidate motif sequences derived from accessible chromatin, then use NA-MPNN to infer the DNA motifs from the most confident predictions. Across 210 TFs with experimentally characterized orthologs, predicted motifs match the known motif for 44%. Propagated through sequence clusters, these predictions form ZebraMotif, a motif database for all 2,543 zebrafish TFs. ZebraMotif covers 2.8 times as many TFs as CisBP's zebrafish collection and links accessible regulatory elements to candidate upstream regulators genome-wide. Because the approach requires only a proteome and a set of candidate motifs, it is applicable to any species lacking experimental binding data.

Haplotype reconstruction in multi-generational diploid and tetraploid populations

Zheng, C., Resende, M. F. R., +1 author, Endelman, J. B.

Abstract: Previously, we developed a statistical framework and software (PolyOrigin1) to reconstruct parental haplotypes of each offspring in connected tetraploid F1 populations. A key application of the technology is quantitative trait loci (QTL) mapping, via regression of phenotypes on posterior probabilities of parental origins. In this work, we extend the methodology and software (PolyOrigin2) to multi-generational populations (both diploid and tetraploid), which are common in breeding programs. The extension links parental origins across generations to estimate posterior probabilities of founder origins. Compared to PolyOrigin1, the main challenge is to phase intermediate individuals that are both parents and offspring, which is overcome by the Bayesian update rule. In addition, genotypic error models are extended to account for sequencing errors such as allelic bias and overdispersion in genotyping-by-sequencing (GBS). Extensive simulations were used to compare PolyOrigin2 with previous methods and to study the effects of such variables as sequencing errors and the number of progeny per parent. We further evaluated PolyOrigin2 using three real datasets: diploid potato with 23 founders and 7 generations, tetraploid potato with 10 founders and 3 generations, and tetraploid blueberry with 10 founders and 4 generations. The results show that PolyOrigin2 is accurate for haplotype phasing and reconstruction in diploid and tetraploid pedigrees and opens new opportunities for haplotype-based QTL mapping.

Surrogate Verifiers Shape Decisions in Closed-Loop Experimental Design

Merzbacher, C., Goecke, J., +1 author, Patz, M.

Abstract: High throughput, automated screening has revolutionized biological experimentation and prompted predictions of AI-enabled "self-driving" labs. Deployed autonomous lab systems typically use a learned surrogate to predict new experimental candidates and rank which conditions deserve expensive follow-up. Determining whether a computational surrogate verifier can substitute for an experimental assay's ground truth requires an understanding of model accuracy, uncertainty, and extrapolation. In this work, we present surrogate verifiers trained on N=7,688 Lacticaseibacillus growth curves exploring a 10-dimensional media component experimental design space. We train surrogate models across six distinct families including polynomial, Gaussian-process, tree-ensemble, neural, and Monod-kinetic forms to predict maximum optical density (OD600). Surrogates ranked by the most commonly reported accuracy metrics (held-out R^2; MAE; RMSE) disagree sharply on which experiments to test next and the best achievable yield. We evaluate models in a Bayesian optimization loop and find that the model used as the active learning oracle matters more than the acquisition function or batch size. We apply conformal calibration to build a model-agnostic assessment criterion of uncertainty and model coverage. We end by evaluating model accuracy and calibration under transfer to an unseen related bacterial strain, and confirm on a prospective validation plate that the transferred verifier's intervals fail in the direction and region our retrospective analysis predicts. This work explores the surrogate verifier within the loop as a previously underexplored axis of closed-loop design and points towards best calibration practices.

AIO Part I: Interpretable ADMET Prediction for Cyclic Peptides and Macrocycles

Wen, S., Wang, S., Qian, Y.

Abstract: Macrocycles and cyclic peptides are increasingly important in beyond-rule-of-five (bRo5) drug discovery, yet their absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) properties remain difficult to predict owing to sparse data, complex topology, and environment-dependent conformations. Here, we present AIO (All-in-One), an interpretable multimodal ADMET framework combining topology-preserving molecular segmentation with solvent-aware contrastive pretraining. Across repeated cross-validation, AIO performed strongly across permeability, solubility, unbound fraction, clearance, half-life, bioavailability, and toxicity endpoints, achieving Pearson R = 0.87 for PAMPA permeability and AUROC = 0.91 for bioavailability. Case studies of recently approved macrocyclic and cyclic-peptide drugs showed agreement between predictions and reported pharmacokinetic profiles, while segment-level attribution highlighted molecular regions associated with predicted ADMET bottlenecks. Despite pretraining exclusively on macrocycles, AIO also transferred competitively to the small-molecule PharmaBench dataset. These results establish a promising foundation for interpretable ADMET prediction across diverse molecular modalities in drug discovery.

MitoArc: A Reproducible Short-Read Workflow for Human Mitochondrial Genome Analysis with Circular-Coordinate and NUMT-Aware Evaluation

Galgale, S., Vats, I., Haldar, A.

Abstract: Human mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) analysis from short-read sequencing is complicated by nuclear mitochondrial DNA segments (NUMTs), circular-reference coordinates, sequencing depth, and variant-caller configuration. We developed MitoArc, a Nextflow workflow that accepts paired-end FASTQ files or pre-aligned BAM and CRAM files, retains available nuclear mapping context, and performs standard and shifted-coordinate processing. It runs Genome Analysis Toolkit (GATK) Mutect2 and mutserve2 independently and reports mitochondrial quality metrics, NUMT evidence, haplogroups, contamination estimates, functional annotations, consensus sequences, and reproducibility outputs. We evaluated MitoArc using 18 controlled datasets, spanning three technical replicates and six sequencing depths (100x-5000x), with 648 allele-level truth observations. Requiring confident primary mitochondrial mapping eliminated 33 observable synthetic NUMT-associated false-positive calls but reduced recall from 0.894 to 0.796. In a matched evaluation comprising 108 truth observations, standard-only and standard-plus-shifted GATK processing produced identical variant recovery and allele-fraction estimates. GATK recall increased from 0.741 at 100x to 0.917 at 5000x, while temporary working storage increased approximately 20-fold. Against the complete truth set, GATK recovered 548 observations and mutserve2 recovered 311 under the evaluated configurations; mutserve2 insertion and deletion modes were not enabled. The benchmark used synthetic data without a background sequencing-error model. A reduced HG002 dataset demonstrated end-to-end operation of the pre-aligned workflow, subject to incomplete nuclear context. MitoArc preserves caller-specific evidence and the analytical context needed to interpret mitochondrial variants. The software is publicly available at https://github.com/sarvezh1/mitoarc.

Causal regulators of human T-cell and macrophage activation from perturbation screens, and a replicated coupling of T-cell exhaustion markers with macrophage M1 activation in patients under checkpoint blockade

Cupaioli, F. A., Merelli, I.

Abstract: Engineering anti-tumour immunity requires knowing which genes control T-cell and macrophage programs, and how the two compartments relate to each other in patients. We built ImmunoTwin, an analysis framework that addresses both questions with public data and with every test, baseline and criterion fixed before the result was computed. From a genome-scale CRISPR-interference Perturb-seq in primary human CD4+ T cells (22 million cells, four donors) we derive a map of regulators of the cytotoxic effector program that is calibrated, reverses direction under CRISPR activation (rho = -0.53), and tracks interferon-gamma production among the hits of independent sorting-based screens (rho = 0.69). From a Perturb-seq in primary human macrophages we recover the TLR4-NF-kappaB pathway as regulator of the lipopolysaccharide response (pathway-level P = 0.0004) and find that transcriptional effects track TNF protein measured in independent donors (rho = 0.58). In both cell types, however, a generic measure of perturbation strength recovers the hits of functional screens better than the program-specific effect (area under the ROC curve 0.96 versus 0.73 in T cells; 0.87 versus 0.72 in macrophages). In 90 patients from seven cohorts with tumour sampled before and during checkpoint blockade, T-cell and macrophage programs shifted together, most strongly T-cell exhaustion markers and the macrophage M1 program (r = 0.43). Fixed as a hypothesis and tested once in 86 further patients from six cohorts, this association replicated (r = 0.51, permutation P = 0.0001) and was specific against expression-matched random gene sets (P = 0.004). The baseline state of one compartment did not improve the forecast of the other, so the criterion we had set for calling the framework a patient-level digital twin was not met. ImmunoTwin provides two validated perturbation maps, a replicated cross-compartment association in patients, and a caution on how such maps are benchmarked.

Prediction and Dissection of Nitrogenase Activity Based on Protein Sequences using Dual Descriptor

Zhao, Y.-J., Wu, G., +3 authors, Ma, B.-G.

Abstract: Nitrogenase reduces N2 to ammonia at ambient temperature and pressure, but its activity differs markedly among natural sequences, and conventional wet-laboratory screening suffers from low throughput and long turnaround times. Existing computational methods have partially alleviated this bottleneck, yet most of them rely on protein embeddings obtained from protein language models together with features such as gene expression, and their interpretability analyses mostly stop at the level of feature contribution. Here we present CarmnaDD, a purely sequence-driven framework built on the dual descriptor (DD): two learnable components, the composition weight map (CWM) and the position weight function (PWF), jointly generate a position-resolved description N(k), so that activity can be predicted without any prior knowledge beyond the sequence. On the same dataset used by Carmna, CarmnaDD attains R2 = 0.60 for regression and AUC = 0.94 and F1 = 0.86 for classification, better than our previous Carmna, which integrates multiple features. Interpretability analysis shows that the amplitude of the periodic terms of the PWF decreases as the rank increases, indicating that the positional encoding strategy adapts to the rank; the high-weight 3-mer fragments in the CWM are significantly enriched in polar uncharged residues, which corroborates the SHAP-based conclusion of Carmna; sliding-window scanning identifies fragments significantly associated with activity (FDR < 0.05), and the strongest window displays a D-X-E pattern with conserved flanks and a polymorphic center, sequences carrying R at the central position showing the highest activity; conservation is significantly negatively correlated ({rho} = -0.30) with description intensity. Mapping the key windows onto the protein structure shows that four highly correlated windows converge on the nucleotide-binding interface and the conformational-transmission/protein-docking interface of NifH. These results provide a purely sequence-based framework for nitrogenase activity prediction that balances performance and interpretability.

Localizing Unsampled Regions in Partially Sampled Pathogen Phylogenies Using Machine Learning

Khodaei, M., Hupert, N., RoyChoudhury, A.

Abstract: Motivation: Pathogen phylogenies are useful for monitoring the evolution of pathogens and for detecting new variants, as it provides information that can aid public health decisions and outbreak response. Since not all infections are sequenced, certain pathogen lineages may be missing or only poorly represented in reconstructed phylogenies. Identifying these gaps could help to determine where additional data collection is required. Results: We developed a computational method to locate regions of missing samples from the observed tree alone, without sequences from those samples. A machine learning model predicts which branches lead toward the missing region. Beam search then identifies three representative paths and estimates a candidate most recent common ancestor (MRCA) of the missing region for each path. We tested this framework on 5,000 simulated phylogenies where we controlled which tips were deleted. On an independent test set, the best candidate recovered an average of 89.9% of deleted tips, increasing to 90.7% when all three candidates were combined. Predicted and true MRCAs lay on the same ancestral-descendant lineage in 91.3% of cases. We further evaluated the approach on an empirical SARS-CoV-2 phylogeny with introduced missingness. By identifying potentially underrepresented lineages, this framework could help real-time identification of missing infections and improve outbreak response.

Glioblastoma exhibits bidirectional adaptation to treatment through JARID2-associated developmental plasticity

Thomas, M., Wang, B., +15 authors, Stead, L. F.

Abstract: Glioblastoma (GBM) exhibits bidirectional transcriptional reprogramming following standard-of-care (SoC) treatment, yet the direction cannot be predicted from primary tumours. Using independent longitudinal bulk and single-cell datasets, we show that this response is captured by a framework, the JARID2 Binding Site (JBS) axis. Tumours and cells can be positioned along this axis according to expression of JBS genes, with responder subtypes defined by movement along the axis from primary to recurrent states. The JBS axis aligns with established GBM ecosystems and their constituent cell populations, and recurrent tumours polarise towards opposite ends. Changes in tumour microenvironment composition strongly influence this polarisation and drive adaptive transcriptional responses more profoundly than chemoradiation alone. Mechanistically, JBS genes are enriched for promoters marked by persistent bivalent chromatin and neurodevelopmental regulatory programmes. These findings suggest that JARID2-associated Polycomb regulation maintains a reservoir of developmental plasticity that enables treatment-driven ecosystem adaptation and represents a potential therapeutic vulnerability.

Connexin43 drives mitochondrial biogenesis and organelle stress in BRAF-mutant melanoma cells

Guitian-Caamano, A., Fernandez-Bejarano, E., +7 authors, Mayan, M. D.

Abstract: Metabolic flexibility helps cancer cells survive targeted therapy, and BRAF-mutant melanoma rewires mitochondrial metabolism in response to BRAF/MEK inhibitors (BRAF/MEKi). We previously showed that connexin43 (Cx43) acts as a tumour suppressor in BRAF-mutant melanoma and enhances the response to BRAF/MEKi. Here, we investigated whether mitochondrial remodelling contributes to these effects. Cx43 upregulation promoted mitochondrial biogenesis including selective increase of CI and CV, enhanced oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), and increased reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, alongside DRP1-associated mitochondrial fragmentation. Despite enhanced mitochondrial biogenesis and OXPHOS, Cx43 upregulation was associated with respiratory chain remodelling, characterised by loss of complex IV, increased ER and mitochondrial stress, and enhanced mTORC1-associated signalling. These changes were accompanied by an adaptive unfolded protein response that did not engage the pro-apoptotic ATF4-CHOP axis. Notably, BRAF/MEK inhibition largely attenuated the mitochondrial biogenesis phenotype and most of the stress markers associated with Cx43 upregulation, while promoting mitochondrial network elongation and markedly increasing mitochondrial ROS. Despite this impact, Cx43-expressing tumour cells retained higher membrane potential, ROS levels and survival under glucose-limiting conditions. Collectively, these findings reveal a dissociation between the mitochondrial effects of Cx43 and its enhancement of the response to BRAF/MEKi, highlighting the complexity of Cx43-mediated metabolic regulation in BRAF-mutant melanoma. By distinguishing mitochondrial remodelling from therapeutic sensitisation, this study provides new insights into the response to BRAF/MEK inhibition and highlights mTORC1-associated signalling and mitochondrial oxidative stress as potential therapeutic vulnerabilities for further investigation.

Radiation survival selects clonally restricted NSCLC survivors with impaired metastatic capacity and distinct bioenergetic states

Martinez-Cruz, M., Darbalaei, M., +8 authors, Grüner, B. M.

Abstract: Radiation resistance and recurrence remain major challenges in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Whether radiation survival enriches for more aggressive, metastasis-competent cells remains unresolved. Using longitudinal cellular barcoding, functional assays, and multi-omics profiling in diverse NSCLC models, we show that fractionated irradiation imposes a strong clonal bottleneck, but recovery follows either stochastic or deterministic trajectories. Despite this diversity, all radiation-surviving populations exhibit increased radiosensitivity and profoundly impaired metastatic capacity, including near-complete failure in lung colonization in vivo. Transcriptomic and metabolic analyses reveal three distinct bioenergetic states, hypermetabolic, energy-conserving, and metabolically suppressed, each supported by unique transcriptional programs. Crucially, clonal dominance does not predict molecular or functional state, indicating that clonal selection and post-irradiation reprogramming are decoupled. These findings challenge the assumption that radiation selects for aggressive clones and reveal that radiation survival may instead create a state of post-irradiation vulnerability. This has important implications for therapy: targeting adaptive pathways, not just resistant clones, may improve outcomes.
