Arena3D 3.0: a web server for exploring, analyzing, annotating and sharing multilayered networks in 3D

Karatzas, E., Baltoumas, F., +9 authors, Pavlopoulos, G. A.

Abstract: Multilayer networks describe biological entities linked by several kinds of relation or measured under several conditions. Arena3D 3.0, a complete reimplementation of Arena3Dweb, gives each layer its own place in an interactive 3D scene. Networks are uploaded or imported from STRING, IntAct, OmniPath, NDEx, Reactome and the Gene Ontology. Twelve layouts, seven in 3D, are available, and node copies can be aligned across layers. Layers are drawn as floors or 3D containers in thirteen arrangements, including 3D hive plots. Communities and annotated groups can be laid out apart and outlined as hulls or fog clouds, and edges can be bundled. Analyses cover node and multilayer metrics, distributions and perturbation, with chord, Sankey, heatmap, UpSet and UMAP views. Networks go to Cytoscape, Gephi Lite, NORMA and JBrowse 2, gene lists to Flame, and a REST API gives programmatic access. Arena3D 3.0 is free at www.arena3d.org without login.

IG-28 enables identification and quantification of high-confidence isoforms from large bulk and single-cell full-length cDNA datasets

Calicchio, A., Li, A. O., +2 authors, Vollmers, C.

Abstract: The technology to sequence full-length transcriptomes from bulk and single-cell samples has rapidly advanced. As a result, full-length cDNA datasets now reach billions of reads in size which is beyond the capability of most established isoform identification tools. Here, we introduce IG-28 which addresses this challenge with its capability to process and analyze large fl-cDNA datasets fast and using minimal computational resources. In contrast to its predecessor Mandalorion, IG-28 is not limited to bulk fl-cDNA but is capable of parsing, processing, and analyzing single-cell fl-cDNA datasets. It does so in a lightweight, flexible manner, integrating seamlessly with established scRNA-seq analysis workflows. We benchmarked IG-28 against a variety of established and new isoform identification tools using several bulk and a complex single-cell fl-cDNA dataset and found that IG-28 consistently outperformed the other tools by identifying high-confidence public isoforms.

Interpreting Graph Neural Networks for Antibacterial Drug Repurposing against Acinetobacter baumannii

Romano, S., Grassia, M., +3 authors, Taccioli, C.

Abstract: Antimicrobial resistance in Acinetobacter baumannii, whose carbapenem-resistant strains are classified by the World Health Organization as a critical priority, has motivated machine-learning screens for new antibacterials, but translating predictions into chemical hypotheses requires evaluating the molecular features on which a model relies. A graph neural network ensemble achieves competitive predictive performance against a descriptor-augmented reference model on the same antibacterial screen while using only atom features and connectivity, allowing interpretation to focus on the molecular graph without a separate input of handcrafted descriptors. The atom-level explanations identify features supporting the model's activity predictions under perturbation and restoration, while comparisons with equally sized perturbations within each molecule distinguish piperazine as the most consistently supported recurring contributor to predicted activity. Prospective screening of the Drug Repurposing Hub followed by developability and safety assessment yields a ranked shortlist for experimental follow-up and leads to testing of CE3F4, which reduced A. baumannii growth by 57% at 0.25 mM in a preliminary assay with a non-monotonic concentration--response profile.

A Synthetic Virology Platform for Rapid Prototyping of Influenza A Virus mutants

Murti, J. R., Schön, K., Angeletti, D.

Abstract: Influenza A virus (IAV) remains a significant public health threat, with the need for effective vaccines and antiviral therapies remaining a high priority. In vitro testing is paramount in studying novel IAV viruses and their immunogenicity. To address this need, we developed a synthetic virology approach to rapidly prototype and test IAV mutants with altered antigenic sites. By strategically inserting restriction sites within an ambisense plasmid, we generated a tool where we can seamlessly replace either 1) the full hemagglutinin (HA), 2) the HA globular head domain to generate head/stalk chimeras or 3) the five canonical antigenic sites. The cloning inserts can be computationally designed and artificially synthesized, enabling rescue of synthetic Influenza A viruses. Using this platform we rescued IAV H1N1, H3N2 & H5N1, and went on to prototype their respective antigenic site mutants & chimeric IAVs using viral genome sequence alone. This platform could be useful for generating vaccine seed strains or to study anti-viral immunity.

Yeast surface display reveals structure-dependent β-glucan association with the plant immune receptor AtIGP1

Menn, B., Molina, A., +2 authors, Ramirez, V.

Abstract: Cell wall-derived oligosaccharides can function as immune-active signals in plants and trigger pattern-triggered immunity (PTI) through pattern recognition receptors (PRRs). In Arabidopsis thaliana, the leucine-rich repeat malectin (LRR-MAL) receptor kinase IGP1 (impaired in glycan perception 1) contributes to immune responses induced by cellulose- and mixed-linkage {beta}-1,3/1,4-glucan (MLG)-derived oligosaccharides, although whether MLG-derived oligosaccharides are directly recognized by AtIGP1 needs further clarification. We established a Saccharomyces cerevisiae surface-display system in which the AtIGP1 extracellular domain (ECD) is presented at the yeast cell surface, allowing oligosaccharide recognition to be examined separately from downstream plant signaling and without receptor purification. Previously, cellulose-derived oligosaccharides have been shown to elicit plant defense responses via AtIGP1. Here, surface-displayed AtIGP1 retained the ability to recognize fluorescently labeled cellotriose (CEL3) and cellopentaose (CEL5) ligands, supporting functional presentation of the receptor for investigating glycan recognition in a heterologous context. Using this platform, we investigated the association of AtIGP1 with fluorescently labeled MLG-derived oligosaccharides differing in degree of polymerization (DP) and glycosidic linkage arrangement. This analysis revealed a specific, competitively displaceable association with MLG DP2 (G3G; laminaribiose), whereas the DP3 (G4G3G) and DP4 (G4G4G3G) structures tested showed no detectable association. To test whether this association is relevant in planta, we treated A. thaliana seedlings with unlabeled MLG DP2, which induced a cytosolic Ca2+ response, an early readout of immune activation. This response was markedly reduced in the igp1-3 mutant. Together, these results identify laminaribiose as an additional candidate ligand of AtIGP1. The established yeast surface-display platform thus provides an approach for testing candidate oligosaccharides for association with the extracellular domains of plant PRRs.

Engineered probiotics target granulomas to treat mycobacterial infection

Chen, Y.-Y., Harimoto, T., +10 authors, Danino, T.

Abstract: Granulomas are a pathological niche for mycobacteria that remain difficult to access with conventional therapies. Here we show that probiotic Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 (EcN) infiltrates and colonizes granulomas and can be engineered as a delivery system that senses the local microenvironment and releases antimycobacterial payloads. Specifically, we leveraged the hypoxic microenvironment of necrotic granulomas to restrict bacterial growth, and developed a dual-mode lysis circuit combining intracellular and extracellular killing for sustained payload release with limited evolutionary escape. We further engineered EcN to express a mycobacteriophage-derived enzyme, generating a strain that suppressed drug-sensitive and drug-resistant mycobacteria in vitro and potentiated conventional antibiotic activity. Engineered probiotics also reduced mycobacterial burden in vivo, and in a murine model of rifampicin-resistant infection, combination treatment with pretomanid achieved greater bacterial reduction than either monotherapy. Together, these results establish a synthetic biology framework for targeting granulomatous infections with programmable living microbes.

Agentic Discovery Transfer Enables Cross-Cancer Discovery at Scale

Cheng, L., Shireman, J., +6 authors, Kendziorski, C.

Abstract: Cancer research generates tens of thousands of studies each year, yet whether a published finding holds in another cancer type or patient population is rarely tested, because doing so is labor-intensive and difficult to scale. We define this task as discovery transfer and present SMART, a multi-agent large language model framework for discovery transfer in single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) studies. Given a source manuscript and a target scRNA-seq dataset, SMART extracts major clinical and mechanistic findings, generates and executes analyses for the testable findings, and reports which findings transfer and suggests follow-up. Applied to 121 studies across six cancer types, SMART identified findings that transfer across cancers, including a lung cancer prognostic signature that we confirmed to be associated with survival in liver cancer, pancreatic cancer and pleural mesothelioma. SMART also generated new mechanistic hypotheses, demonstrating how agentic discovery transfer can extend published findings beyond their original setting.

Expanding the therapeutic window of bacteria for cancer therapy

Im, J., Komaranchath, M., +13 authors, Danino, T.

Abstract: Engineered bacteria enable self-amplifying therapeutic delivery and immune activation, yet these same features drive systemic toxicity and off-target growth. Here, we create a "tumorobe" that overcomes these limitations by integrating reduced immunogenicity, tumour-restricted growth, and enhanced payload release. Specifically, we identify lipopolysaccharide modifications that reduce immunogenicity while preserving fitness, restrict growth to tumours using a hypoxia-lactate "AND" gate, and enhance extracellular payload release via lipoprotein deletion. This strain expands the maximum tolerated dose in a route-dependent manner by up to 1,000-fold. Across multiple murine models, including colorectal cancer and glioblastoma, the strain drives robust antitumour efficacy as both a standalone therapeutic and an immunotherapeutic delivery platform, achieving durable complete responses in up to 75% of colorectal tumours when armed with a cytokine payload. Together, these results establish a broadly applicable platform for safe and effective bacterial cancer therapy.

NanoPro - a Framework for Probiotically-Guided Nanovector Therapy

Michels, A., Haseki, A., +8 authors, Danino, T.

Abstract: The 'antigen problem', i.e. the disconnect between the broad spectrum of therapeutic modes enabled by the range of viral and synthetic nanoparticles for which workable receptor-targeting architectures exist and the relative dearth of addressable target receptors on most tumors, is a long-standing challenge in targeted tumor nanotherapy. Here we provide in vitro-proof-of-principle for NanoPro, a platform for guiding nanoscale therapeutics to otherwise antigenically indistinct tumors via probiotically-released tumor cell-binding tags, ProTags. These consist of an orthogonal antigen (GFP or ALFA) fused to the heparin-binding domain of placenta growth factor-2 and serve as attachment receptors for ProTag-targeted nanovectors. ProTag release from Escherichia coli Nissle 1917 was facilitated by deletion of the membrane-tethering lpp gene, which increased ProTag supernatant concentrations 16.5-fold compared to the parental strain without impacting viability. HBD-dependent decoration with ProTag in bacterial supernatant followed by treatment with receptor-targeted aGFP- or aALFA-lentiviral vectors resulted in majority transduction of MC-38 and MDA-MB-468 tumor cell lines with antigen specificities of 96.5% and 98.5%, respectively, depending on the choice of nanobody. This ProTag-mediated interaction was notably robust, with selective transduction still observed at 625-fold supernatant dilution, warranting further exploration of the utility of the NanoPro system for tumor-restricted delivery of nanotherapeutics.

Transcriptomic similarity guides source selection beyond tissue lineage

Jiang, L., Pang, J., +3 authors, Zhang, H.

Abstract: Perturbational and pharmacogenomic maps remain incomplete across cellular contexts, leaving a practical source-selection question before response prediction: which measured context should inform an unmeasured target? We tested whether baseline transcriptomic similarity ranks candidate sources for held-out targets, separating source ranking from aggregation and transfer-uncertainty decisions. Similarity tracked matched-response concordance in LINCS and GSE306429; selected-source utility exceeded random expectation in GSE306429 ({Delta}U = 0.034; P = 0.002) and in exact-time-matched MIX-Seq tasks ({Delta}U = 0.046). In the full GDSC2 analysis, the drug-balanced ranking effect was 0.359 (95% CI, 0.337-0.383). In a lineage-restricted analysis of the same eligible subset, within-tissue ranking remained positive at 0.130 (95% CI, 0.119-0.141), retaining 35.8% of the same-subset global effect (0.363; 95% CI, 0.342-0.386). Global transcriptomic top-1 selection exceeded tissue-matched random selection by 0.154 (95% CI, 0.143-0.164). Thus, baseline similarity adds source-ranking information after tissue matching in the evaluated setting, while ranking alone does not resolve whether to aggregate sources or how to quantify transfer uncertainty.

Myogenic and neural transcriptional circuitry determines PAX3::FOXO1 driven initiation of rhabdomyosarcoma

Stevens, B. T., Zhang, Y., +17 authors, Hatley, M. E.

Abstract: Alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma (ARMS) is largely characterized by the PAX3::FOXO1 (P3F) fusion oncoprotein, yet the mechanisms by which P3F initiates tumorigenesis and cell identity remain incompletely understood. Here, we describe a doxycycline inducible, degradable PAX3::FOXO1 iPSC derived ARMS model (ddP3F) to interrogate the temporal requirements of P3F for ARMS cell identity and proliferation. We show that, while P3F is essential for initiating ARMS transformation from precursor cells, continued expression of P3F is not required for maintaining proliferation, viability, or RMS associated transcriptional programs. Integration of CUT&RUN, bulk RNA seq, and 10X multiome profiling during endothelial-directed differentiation revealed that P3F rapidly pioneers a novel enhancer landscape within two days of expression, activating transcriptional circuits anchored by both specific myogenic and neural transcription factors (TFs), including MYOD1, PRDM12, and POU4F1 (BRN3A). Our single-cell analysis shows that these TFs are specifically co-expressed with P3F from the earliest stages of transformation. Knockout of MYOD1 or POU4F1 impairs ARMS identity acquisition without preventing transformation, whereas PRDM12 loss enhances P3F expression programs and myogenic differentiation. Together, these findings underscore a previously unappreciated interplay between myogenic and neural transcriptional programs during ARMS initiation. Furthermore, these data demonstrate that P3F establishes a stable oncogenic state that persists even after removal, thus providing a framework for evaluating P3F dependent and P3F independent therapeutic vulnerabilities.

Implementation and Evaluation of Marker Rescue and qPCR Assays for Detection of Replication-Competent Retrovirus in Gene Therapy Products

Ebrahimiyan, H., Sayadmanesh, A., +1 author, Basiri, M.

Abstract: Quality control of retroviral gene therapy vectors requires reliable detection of replication-competent retrovirus (RCR) particles. Cell-based assays remain the standard for detecting RCR, while PCR-based assays serve as rapid, complementary methods. Despite their widespread use, few studies provide comprehensive protocols and performance data to guide assay setup. Here we provide a detailed description and performance assessment of a cell-based firefly luciferase marker rescue assay for RCR testing. Additionally, we describe the setup and assessment of a qPCR-based RCR assay targeting retroviral pol gene sequence. Different viral copy numbers of MuLV were used to determine the final sensitivity of the marker rescue assay with and without initial amplification. Different q-PCR strategies were investigated for RCR testing using different types of primers, including env and pol genes. The marker rescue assay detected 73.2 RCR genome particles without amplification, while the amplification phase enabled detection of functional RCR particles and increased sensitivity, with the amplification step increasing viral particles by up to 5.23x103 fold. The PCR-based RCR assay using retroviral pol gene could detect approximately 7.32x103 viral particles. This study offers a resource for establishing RCR testing procedures for clinical-grade retroviral vector products.

Persistent Malignant Stem Cells Drive Relapse After Transplantation in the Myelodysplastic Neoplasms

Kroger, B., Tamari, R., +25 authors, Chung, S. S.

Abstract: The myelodysplastic neoplasms (MDS) are initiated in hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs). Allogeneic transplantation is the only curative treatment, but relapse remains common. To detect rare MDS HSCs, we developed an ultra-low-input DNA sequencing assay and applied it to a multicenter post-transplant cohort. HSC mutation detection preceded relapse by a mean of 98 days and strongly predicted relapse (95.7% vs. 30.3%; hazard ratio, 8.8; P=1.5e-7). Mutations detected only in unfractionated marrow did not predict relapse. We developed methods to measure donor-host HSC chimerism, improving relapse prediction sensitivity. Single-cell protein, DNA, and RNA analyses with mitochondrial lineage tracing confirmed MDS cell enrichment within immunophenotypically- and transcriptionally-defined HSCs, identifying shared ribosomal gene downregulation and candidate mechanisms of venetoclax sensitivity. Venetoclax/azacytidine depleted detectable MDS HSCs in longitudinally evaluated patients. These findings establish that HSC mutation detection predicts post-transplant relapse, provide a rationale for preemptive intervention, and define shared biological features of transplant-resistant MDS HSCs.

Targeting the sinusoidal-to-capillarized transition to normalize vessels in breast cancer liver metastasis

Huang, H.-Y., Zhao, C., +2 authors, Tian, L.

Abstract: Liver metastases exhibit marked resistance to anti-VEGF anti-angiogenic therapies, yet the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. By combining single-cell RNA sequencing of human breast cancer liver metastases with a liver sinusoidal endothelial cell (LSEC) lineage-tracing mouse model, we show that liver metastasis-associated vessels originate from Lyve1+ sinusoidal endothelial cells but undergo a sinusoidal-to-capillarized transition as they grow into the metastatic nodule, characterized by enhanced inflammatory signaling, loss of sinusoidal markers, and acquisition of capillarized markers. We functionally perturbed each of these abnormalities individually. Unexpectedly, ectopic Lyve1 overexpression paradoxically enhanced hyaluronan and other extracellular matrix deposition in the tumor microenvironment, suggesting a compensatory mechanism. Similarly, targeting TNF signaling, a hallmark inflammatory pathway in liver metastasis, dampened the inflammatory state of metastasis-associated vessels but promoted metastatic outgrowth, indicating that TNF blockade is not a viable strategy for metastasis treatment. Strikingly, inhibition of the capillary tip cell marker Esm1 improved vessel perfusion and enhanced chemotherapeutic drug delivery, two defining features of vascular normalization. Pan-cancer analysis further revealed that Esm1 expression is restricted to tip cells and correlates with poor patient prognosis, positioning Esm1 as a candidate target for promoting vascular normalization. Collectively, our findings reveal the vascular plasticity of liver metastasis and, although not all abnormal features are actionable, we propose targeting Esm1 to inhibit sprouting angiogenesis and to normalize dysfunctional liver metastasis vessels, offering a promising therapeutic avenue for liver malignancies.

Customizable bead-based platform for EV surface protein immunocapture and detection in plasma

Ilamathi, H. S., Mesa, M. C., +8 authors, Wiklander, O. P. B.

Abstract: Extracellular vesicles (EVs) are increasingly recognized as valuable biomarkers for liquid biopsy because they carry molecular signatures that reflect their cells of origin. However, EV isolation remains a challenge, and existing EV immunocapture platforms are constrained by nonspecific binding, limited flexibility, and insufficient sensitivity. Here, we present a customizable bead-based platform for the isolation and detection of specific EV populations directly from plasma. The platform combines bead-immobilized capture antibodies with fluorescent detection antibodies, enabling sensitive and multiplexed EV profiling. Optimization of bead conjugation chemistry improved antibody immobilization and reduced nonspecific background signals, enhancing assay specificity and performance. Using breast cancer-derived EVs expressing fluorescent protein, we demonstrate the detection of tumor-derived EVs in plasma at concentrations as low as 1E9 EVs/ml. Furthermore, the assay distinguished HER2- and TROP2-high from low EV populations, demonstrating its ability to detect clinically relevant differences in biomarker expression. The workflow is compatible with direct EV analysis in the unprocessed plasma samples, thereby reducing sample preparation requirements and assay complexity. These findings establish a sensitive, flexible, and scalable platform for EV biomarker analysis. The approach overcomes several limitations of current commercial bead-based technologies and may facilitate the development of customizable multiplexed EV-based assays for cancer detection, patient stratification, and disease monitoring.

Gene context shapes spatial organization and transcriptional heterogeneity of extrachromosomal DNA in cancer cells

Frank, L., Marinopoulou, G., Medema, R. H.

Abstract: Extrachromosomal DNA (ecDNA) promotes tumor adaptability by making oncogenic driver genes variably abundant, spatially mobile, and heterogeneously expressed. Transcriptional hyperactivation and spatial mobility of ecDNA-amplified genes is associated with aberrant regulatory states that could create targetable vulnerabilities in ecDNA-driven cancers. However, how gene context shapes their nuclear positioning and aberrant transcription remains unclear. To explore spatio-transcriptional behavior across ecDNA-amplified genes, we developed FLAGx, a multimodal imaging method that quantitatively resolves transcriptional activity and nuclear position of individual ecDNA genes in situ. Surprisingly, we find that gene context affects subnuclear distribution and transcriptional deregulation of ecDNA. Further, ecDNA clustering, cell cycle regulation, and drug responses depend on gene context, while transcriptional shutdown in mitosis persists across ecDNA species, arguing against pervasive mitotic transcription as a common feature. These findings show that driver gene context shapes ecDNA behavior and establish FLAGx as a useful approach to separate gene-specific from shared ecDNA features.

Cancer cell adaptations along a proliferation-robustness axis drive TME pro-tumor phenotypes

Pour, M., Zhao, G., +10 authors, Yanai, I.

Abstract: Tumors are highly complex dynamic ecosystems with causal relationships between cancer cells and their tumor microenvironment (TME). To disentangle these interactions, we performed long-term co-culture experiments with cancer cells evolving alongside continuously replenished fibroblasts or macrophages. This allowed us to distinguish between adaptive and physiological responses in specific cancer-TME interactions, which we then also recapitulated in patient tumors. We found that co-culture with fibroblasts induced the epithelial-mesenchymal transition in cancer cells and resulted in their increased drug tolerance, while co-culture with macrophages promoted stress, hypoxia responses and enhanced tumorigenicity. Adaptations were recurrent and partially reversible, but could not be explained by clonal selection, suggesting a dynamic non-mutational process. Moreover, adapted cancer cells elicited cell state changes in the fibroblasts and macrophages consistent with an extended phenotype mode of cancer evolution. Our findings reframe cancer-TME interactions by a systematic dissection according to sets of specific adaptations and programmed responses.

Deep learning-based multiomics integration identifies AI-defined tissue regions associated with cancer-associated fibroblast enrichment and chemoresistance in muscle-invasive bladder cancer.

Chao Hu, Fan Wang, +8 authors, Xiongbing Lu

Abstract: Muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) frequently develops resistance to platinum-based chemotherapy, and tools for characterizing treatment-associated tumor microenvironmental features remain limited. We developed an artificial intelligence (AI)-based framework integrating pathological imaging, multiomics data, single-cell RNA sequencing, and ex vivo drug-sensitivity testing. Three convolutional neural network models trained using hematoxylin-and-eosin-stained whole-slide images from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) classified tumor, necrosis, and lymphocyte-enriched regions and were evaluated in an independent tissue microarray cohort. Patches negative for all three classifiers were designated AI-defined "Other" regions; this residual category was not trained as a cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF) classifier. Higher Other% was associated with shorter overall survival in univariable analyses, but the association was not significant after multivariable adjustment in TCGA. Multiomics and tissue-origin analyses linked Other% to stromal content. Scissor analysis of eight single-cell RNA-sequencing datasets identified Other-associated Scissor+ cells across epithelial and fibroblast compartments, while ACTA2 expression and extracellular matrix-remodeling pathways were enriched. In 48 MIBC specimens assessed by the hydrogel-embedded histoculture drug sensitivity test, Other% was higher in the gemcitabine-cisplatin-resistant group. Multiplex immunofluorescence further showed enrichment of alpha-smooth muscle actin signal in AI-defined Other regions. Erdafitinib showed ex vivo activity in a subset of specimens, but FGFR alteration status was unavailable and clinical efficacy was not evaluated. These findings support an association between AI-defined Other regions, CAF-related stromal features, and chemoresistance, while prospective validation is required before clinical use.

From forward-backward sweep to backpropagation for optimal control in tumor-immune dynamics.

Wenjing Zhang, Wandi Ding, Huaiping Zhu

Abstract: Optimal control problems in mathematical biology are commonly solved by the forward-backward sweep on the Pontryagin's Maximum Principle necessary conditions. The sweep has known stability issues at constraint-saturated optima. We frame the same problem within the universal differential equation framework. A neural network represents the control. The cost functional serves as the training loss. We prove that any stationary point of the training loss satisfies the continuous first-order necessary conditions to a discretisation-controlled accuracy. On a grid of bifurcation-parameter and initial-condition scenarios drawn from a tumor-immune dynamics model, the standard sweep fails on most scenarios in the nominal regime. Neural network training reaches a stable optimum on every scenario, with a lower final cost than the converged damped sweep iterate. The pipeline accommodates cost functional variants by changing only the loss function. A Sobol sensitivity analysis confirms robustness of the trained schedule to perturbations of the cost weights.
