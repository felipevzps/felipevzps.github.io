Hierarchical distribution matching enables comprehensive characterization of common and condition-specific cell niches in spatial omics data

Liu, Y., Xiao, J., +5 authors, Yang, C.

Abstract: Deciphering cell niches in complex tissues is essential for understanding tissue structure and disease. Recent advances in spatial omics have enabled subcellular resolution and accurate cell identity mapping. However, robust delineation of cell niches and disease-associated spatial patterns remains difficult. We introduce Harmonics, a novel computational framework that systematically identifies both common and condition-specific cell niches from spatial omics data through hierarchical distribution matching. Harmonics also includes a suite of downstream modules that facilitate comprehensive niche characterization. We demonstrate its scalability, accuracy and generalizability across datasets spanning diverse species, tissues, diseases, spatial modalities, and technological platforms. For condition-agnostic datasets, Harmonics outperforms baseline methods in both accuracy and robustness, and further demonstrates the capability to resolve niche structures at finer granularity. Across diverse diseases including pulmonary fibrosis, triple-negative breast cancer, and colorectal cancer, Harmonics enables precise identification of condition-specific niches and reveals disease-associated dynamics, subtype-specific spatial patterns, and structured immune architectures. We envision Harmonics as a practical and versatile tool for spatial niche analysis that can be applied across a wide range of biological contexts and seamlessly integrated with existing spatial omics workflows.

An integrated database of ion channels

Gaikwad, K. B., Parthasarathi, K. T. S., +2 authors, Sharma, J.

Abstract: Ion channels play critical roles in a wide range of physiological processes, including the propagation of nerve impulses, insulin secretion and regulation of heart rate. Alterations in ion-channels function or expression can contribute to the development of various diseases, including cancers, genetic disorders, and infectious diseases. Ion channels represent the second-largest class of membrane proteins considered potential targets for therapeutic intervention. However, a comprehensive data resource integrating information on human ion channels and their associations with diseases has been limited. Therefore, the Integrated Database of Ion Channels (IDIC) was developed, comprising information on 493 human ion channels. The database contains a broad range of ion-channel annotations, including gene and protein information, structural details, protein signature sequences, protein domains, nuclear localization signals, and signal peptides. It also provides cross-references to several established biological resources, including the Human Genome Organization Gene Nomenclature Committee (HGNC), National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) Gene, Vertebrate Genome Annotation (VEGA), Ensembl, and the Protein Data Bank (PDB). Furthermore, IDIC incorporates genomic variations identified in various tumors and disease associations involving ion channels, which have been systematically curated from published literature. Through concise search methods and user-friendly browsing functionalities, IDIC serves as an integrated resource for exploring human ion channels and investigating their structural, functional, genetic, and disease-related characteristics. Thus, the database can facilitate a better understanding of ion channels and their potential involvement in diverse human diseases.

Distance-AF Multimer: A Multi-Step Approach for Protein Complex Structure Modeling with User-Defined Distance Constraints

Ling, K., Zhang, Y., +2 authors, Kihara, D.

Abstract: The three-dimensional structure of proteins provides essential insight into the molecular mechanisms underlying their function. Many biological activities are mediated through protein interactions, making accurate structural determination of protein complexes important. Despite significant advances in computational protein structure prediction, there are still many cases where protein complexes are not correctly assembled. In particular, there are cases where experimental data, such as distances between residue pairs in interacting proteins are available, yet existing prediction methods fail to produce models consistent with these data. Here, we present Distance-AF Multimer, a method that guides and refines AlphaFold-Multimer docking predictions using user-provided residue pair distance information. This approach extends our previously successful data-guided single-protein modeling method, Distance-AF. Distance-AF Multimer operates in a two-step process, starting from multiple initial conformations and iteratively refining them to satisfy the provided distance constraints. We evaluated Distance-AF Multimer on protein-complex benchmarks spanning different chain counts and docking errors, demonstrating competitive or superior performance compared to other existing deep-learning-based structure prediction methods. In addition to dimer cases, we also demonstrated the potential of Distance-AF Multimer for large-scale protein assemblies containing multiple chains, including those with more than 5 chains.

Metalink: Joint taxonomic and functional profiling of metagenomes and metatranscriptomes across resolution scales

Li, L., Wang, Y.

Abstract: Metagenomic and metatranscriptomic analyses remain largely fragmented: taxonomic and functional profiling are performed separately, often at a single resolution (reads, contigs, or MAGs) yielding separate species or function lists rather than mechanistic insights. Here, we developed Metalink, a tool that unifies taxonomic and functional profiling into coupled outputs across all three resolution levels. Metalink is built on three innovations. First, we constructed a Last Common Function-based functional Hierarchy (LCFH) from ~424 million proteins, organizing KEGG Orthologs into a tree structure that enables LCA-like functional assignment. Second, a unified framework integrates the taxonomy of Genome Taxonomy Database (GTDB), the International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses (ICTV), and NCBI for consistent classification across reads, contigs, and MAGs. Third, a multi-level integration strategy with overlap-based deduplication balances accuracy and completeness. At the read level, Metalink exceeds both HUMAnN3 and eggNOG-Mapper in functional assignment accuracy while running ~30-fold faster and 1,000-fold faster, respectively; at the contig level, its functional profiles remain highly consistent with eggNOG-Mapper. Multi-level integration further improves taxonomic resolution over single-level analyses and outperforms leading taxonomic profilers on the CAMI II benchmarks. Metalink's joint outputs move the analysis of metagenomes and metatranscriptomes from cataloging species and functions toward mechanistic inference of species-function-environment triadic associations. By enabling these integrated analyses across resolution scales, Metalink provides a general framework for linking community composition to its functional potential across diverse ecosystems.

RiboBridge couples RNA backbone generation to sequence design through a learned interaction topology

Ma, Z., Zheng, Y.

Abstract: Designing ribonucleic acid (RNA) molecules with prescribed structures and functions is central to RNA engineering. Backbone-first approaches generate three-dimensional geometry before assigning a nucleotide sequence, yet leave open how that geometry should be represented for downstream sequence design. Here we introduce RiboBridge, which infers a probabilistic residue-pair state from backbone geometry, recycles binary pair probabilities through a second pass and transfers binary and typed signals as soft attention biases for sequence decoding. On 550 generated backbones, pair-type decoding achieved joint refolding validity of 0.451 versus 0.335 for an architecture-matched topology-free decoder. On a matched 110-backbone intervention cohort, replacing aligned pair states with exact-length donors matched on pair-state statistics reduced joint validity from 0.464 to 0.214, showing backbone alignment matters. Pair-confidence ranking enriched the joint-validity rate from 0.451 overall to 0.596 among the top 248 before external refolding, with the same ranking signal transferring to independently generated RFDpoly and RNA-FrameFlow backbones. On 53 experimentally determined backbones, pair-coupled decoding improved secondary-structure agreement relative to residue-wise argmax. These results support a learned relational interface between sequence-free backbone generation and sequence design.

DeepD enables efficient genome modeling and controllable long-context sequence generation across domains of life

Lai, J., Xin, B., +14 authors, Zhao, H.

Abstract: Genome foundation models hold great potential for advancing biological research, but modeling diverse complex genomes and generating controllable genome-scale sequences remain challenging. We present DeepD, a phylogeny-informed sparse mixture-of-experts model operating at single-nucleotide resolution over a 1-million-token context while reducing active computation. Pretrained on unselected whole-genome sequences across all domains of life, DeepD learns genic and intergenic sequence features and supports broad sequence-to-function prediction across species. DeepD performs well in human protein fitness and disease variants effect prediction, such as BRCA1 and BRCA2, with particular strength in regulatory regions, such as promoter and transcription-factor binding sites. Expert routing captures exon-intron boundaries, transposable elements, and distal cis-regulatory elements. DeepD also generates experimentally validated core promoters with specified transcriptional strengths. Finally, the Context-Anchor-Body strategy enables generation of complete yeast chromosome sequences and megabase-scale Arabidopsis segments, preserving gene structure and order while introducing variations within natural ranges. Together, DeepD is an efficient genome foundation model that unifies accurate sequence-to-function prediction with controllable genome-scale generation, advancing predictive and generative design of complex genomes.

DIMPLE: A Tree-Guided Divide-and-Conquer Framework for ILS-Aware Phylogenetic Network Inference on Large-Scale Data

Guang, Z., Sapoval, N., +2 authors, Nakhleh, L.

Abstract: Inferring evolutionary histories of species provides a foundation for comparative genomics and evolutionary analyses. Extensive evidence of reticulate evolution motivates the inference of phylogenetic networks, which represent relationships that species trees cannot capture. However, accurate reconstruction of large phylogenetic networks remains a major challenge because of the vast space of possible network topologies. Here, we propose DIMPLE, a divide-and-conquer framework for phylogenetic network inference without an explicit restriction on network level. DIMPLE uses a tree-of-blobs (TOB), which represents the tree-like relationships between regions of potential reticulation while leaving relationships within those regions unresolved. Within each unresolved region, DIMPLE partitions the set of taxa into pairwise-disjoint subsets, each containing at most a user-specified number of taxa. Multiple division runs are allowed, and subsets from different runs may overlap. DIMPLE infers a subnetwork on each subset, merges the subnetworks within each region, and uses the TOB to assemble the resulting networks into a network on the full taxon set. Experiments with true inputs demonstrate accurate merging, with improved reticulation recovery over existing methods on higher-level networks. With estimated inputs, DIMPLE achieves competitive topological accuracy and more consistently recovers hybrid ancestry than competing methods across varying levels of network complexity. Its computational requirements support analyses of hundreds of taxa. Applications to fern and gymnosperm datasets recover documented hybridization and previously reported phylogenetic conflict.

Relation-Aware Graph-Hypergraph Networks for Antibody Sequence-Structure Co-Design

Wei, J., Zhang, S., +2 authors, GUO, J.

Abstract: Motivation: The design of antibody complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) is pivotal for therapeutic development, yet remains challenged by the complexity of the antibody-antigen interface. Existing methods, which largely rely on pairwise relationships, struggle to capture intrinsic multi-body interactions and global structural constraints. Results: We propose the Relation-Aware Graph-Hypergraph Network (RA-GHN), a framework that unifies graph-based pairwise modeling with biophysical hypergraphs to capture high-order cooperative effects. Its heterogeneous hypergraph uses epitope-paratope interaction patches, spatial structural clusters, and high-degree hub structures. A dual-pathway architecture integrates a Relation-Aware Equivariant Graph Network with an Equivariant Hypergraph Encoder while preserving SE(3)-equivariance. We also introduce the Structure-Sequence Decoupling Index, which balances structural reliability with sequence novelty. Experiments show that RA-GHN outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across standard benchmarks and affinity optimization tasks.

Determinants of cysteine coverage by chemoproteomics

Riziotis, I. G., Wilders, H., +16 authors, Bush, J. T.

Abstract: Covalent chemoproteomics has become a powerful route to expanding the druggable proteome, enabling proteome-wide screening of compounds that covalently modify nucleophilic residues, most commonly cysteine. Yet typical experiments detect only 5-18% of the ~250,000 cysteines encoded by the human proteome, and the determinants of this limited coverage remain poorlydefined. Here we combine cysteine featurisation, explainable machine learning (ML) and robust statistical filtering to establish what governs cysteine detectability in iodoacetamide- desthiobiotin (IA-DTB) chemoproteomics. Using a scalable feature-generation pipeline, we computed physicochemical and structural properties for cysteines and the corresponding tryptic peptides and trained models that predict detectability with high accuracy. Shapley analysis identifies protein abundance and intrinsic peptide properties governing LC-MS behaviour as the dominant determinants, whereas cysteine structural descriptors contribute comparatively little, indicating that detection is constrained downstream of probe reactivity. We further introduce a distance-based statistical filter that improves reproducibility across replicates, and show that cell line selection, subcellular fractionation and alternative electrophiles together expand coverage by >30%. More broadly, this work moves optimisation of chemoproteomics workflows from empirical trial-and-error towards rational, data-driven design.
