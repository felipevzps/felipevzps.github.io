Even more space efficient genome sketching with Lash

Zhao, J., Wang, C., +3 authors, Knight, R.

Abstract: Advances in sequencing technology have driven an exponential growth in microbial genomic data, making exact pairwise distance computation increasingly impractical at scale. As a result, computationally efficient genomic distance estimation algorithms have become an essential alternative, e.g., sketching algorithms such as HyperLogLog and MinHash. Here, we present Lash, a software that provides UltraLogLog and ExaLogLog (more space-efficient variants of the HyperLogLog structure) and HyperMinHash (a more space-efficient variant of a MinHash structure) as methods to approximate genomic distance. UltraLogLog and ExaLogLog were demonstrated to be around 28% and 43% more space/memory-efficient respectively than HyperLogLog, and HyperMinHash was 4-5 times more space/memory-efficient than MinHash for various genome comparison tasks.

EditFinder: A Sequence-Based Model for A-to-I RNA Editing Identification and Characterization of Genetic Variant Effects

Zeng, Y., Liu, Q., +2 authors, Wu, S.

Abstract: A-to-I RNA editing is a widespread post-transcriptional modification that contributes to transcriptomic diversity and has been implicated in various diseases. However, incomplete reference resources for RNA editing and limited research into its genetic regulation hinder investigations into RNA editing-mediated disease mechanisms. To address these limitations, EditFinder, a sequence-based model for identifying A-to-I RNA editing sites and assessing the effects of genetic variants on RNA editing, is developed. EditFinder demonstrates superior performance on both an independent reference validation dataset and a read-based sequencing dataset. Its genome-wide application generates an expanded resource of candidate A-to-I RNA editing sites, providing a foundation for comprehensive exploration and functional annotation of RNA editing. Beyond site identification, EditFinder enables systematic assessment of the position- and nucleotide-dependent effects of genetic variations on predicted editing probabilities, highlighting the particularly strong influence of the 3'-adjacent nucleotide. Analysis of REDIportal-documented editing sites further suggests that genetic variations in flanking sequences may alter the predicted editing status of approximately 10% of the entries. Moreover, changes in EditFinder-predicted editing probabilities may reflect trends in SNP-associated variation in RNA editing frequency. Collectively, EditFinder provides a valuable resource for investigating RNA editing and its genetic regulation related to human diseases.

Multi-layer colocalization prioritizes adipose RNA regulatory features beyond gene expression at type 2 diabetes loci

CHEN, Y., LEI, T., +7 authors, JIN, N.

Abstract: Type 2 diabetes (T2D) genetic signals reside predominantly in non-coding regions, and linking them to molecular mechanisms requires resolving the RNA phenotypes through which they act in disease-relevant tissues. Adipose tissue is central to systemic glucose homeostasis, yet most colocalization studies have relied on total gene expression, which captures only one dimension of RNA regulation. Here, we integrated 338 fine-mapped T2D signals from DIAMANTE with five GTEx RNA measurements-gene expression, exon expression, transcript usage, transcriptional-event usage, and splice-junction usage-across subcutaneous and visceral adipose tissue from the eQTL Catalogue. Enforcing bilateral posterior coverage and a cumulative posterior selection rule across 13,216 admissible comparisons expanded testable T2D signals from 253 with gene expression alone to 293 and identified 77 colocalized pairs defining 38 signal-gene relationships across 30 T2D signals, with 87.0% of retained pairs involving non-gene-expression features. At loci with direct same-gene comparators, including BDNF-AS and PGM1, specific RNA features strongly colocalized with T2D while bulk gene expression favored distinct causal variants, demonstrating that gene-level analysis alone can miss or misattribute the primary regulatory signal. Locus-resolved fine-mapping linked a BDNF-AS splice junction to rs6265 in both depots and narrowed PGM1 and RCCD1 to shared or nested candidate-variant sets around defined transcript anchors. External adipose cohorts provided complementary support, single-nucleus and cell-class eQTL data defined cellular contexts, and metabolic-trait and multi-tissue analyses distinguished insulin-resistance (IRS1), fat-distribution (CCDC92), and cross-tissue adiposity (BDNF-AS) profiles. Together, these results provide a multi-layer adipose regulatory atlas that converts T2D signals into specific RNA features, candidate variants, cellular settings, and measurement-led routes for functional validation.

PTMlink: a database resource for co-variation of glycosylation and phosphorylation in human tumors

Wei, E., Liu, H., +3 authors, Zhang, H.

Abstract: Protein glycosylation and phosphorylation can regulate each other, but the two protein modifications are usually studied separately, and their modification-specific co-variation across tumor types remains poorly characterized. PTMlink is a database of site-resolved co-variation between glycosylation and phosphorylation in the matched glycoproteomes and phosphoproteomes of 1,211 tumor samples from cohorts of the Clinical Proteomic Tumor Analysis Consortium, complemented by studies that perturbed glycosylation in human cells to examine its crosstalk with, and functional effects on, phosphorylation. Eligible glycosite- phosphosite pairs are scored within each tumor cohort by a covariate-adjusted partial rank correlation and combined across cohorts by meta-analysis, where specific glycoforms are scored in the same way and gene pairs are summarized. The strongest associations reproduce in held-out tumor types and show concordance in normal adjacent tissue, and the strongest pairs are enriched for known protein interactions and shared pathways; perturbation evidence recovers literature-documented relations that co-variation misses. Available through a website, an R package and a Model Context Protocol server, PTMlink is, to our knowledge, the first resource to provide a queryable score for site-specific relations between glycosylation and phosphorylation across tumor cohorts, with a framework that can extend to other protein modifications as matched measurements become available.

ELLA-mix: Detecting Cell-to-Cell Heterogeneity in Subcellular RNALocalization

Wang, J., Bandreddi, A., Bruce, S. A.

Abstract: Subcellular RNA localization can differ between cells expressing the same gene, yet most analyses summarize transcript positions with a single gene-level pattern. We propose ELLA-mix, a finite mixture model for identifying cell subpopulations with distinct radial localization patterns from imaging-based spatial transcriptomics data. Transcript positions are normalized relative to the cell boundary and modeled using flexible spline densities within a count-conditioned nonhomogeneous Poisson process framework. Conditioning on each cell's transcript count separates differences in localization from differences in abundance. We estimate component densities and cell memberships using an expectation-maximization algorithm and select the number of patterns using the Bayesian information criterion, restricting consideration to models with stable cell assignments and sufficiently distinct components. Simulation studies evaluated the type I error and power of the test for nonuniform localization, as well as the accuracy of selecting the number of mixture components and estimating their radial distributions. ELLA-mix correctly selected the number of components in most simulated settings. In seqFISH+ mouse fibroblasts, ELLA-mix identified distinct cell subpopulations for genes previously assigned a single localization class; genes showing such variation were enriched for endoplasmic reticulum and extracellular matrix functions. In Xenium ovarian cancer tissue, more peripheral localization patterns were associated with higher proportions of proliferating cells, particularly in S phase. These results show how modeling cell-to-cell variation can reveal localization patterns and biological associations obscured by gene-level summaries.

Unipept in 2026: A Unified Web Platform for Comparative Metaproteomics Analysis

Vande Moortele, T., Van de Vyver, S., +5 authors, Mesuere, B.

Abstract: Metaproteomics studies the collective proteome of microbial communities to characterize their taxonomic composition and functional activity. Unipept has long supported such analyses, but organizing samples into projects, building targeted reference databases, and comparing samples across a study were only possible in a standalone desktop application. Here we present a major update that moves these workflows into the web application, so a complete multi-sample study can be assembled, analyzed, and shared without local installation. Samples can be compared both taxonomically and functionally, the latter by mapping enzymes onto metabolic pathways with the integrated PathwayPilot. The analyses also became more informative. The Peptonizer2000, now accelerated by the new NORI inference engine, adds confidence scores to taxonomic and functional results, and an optional filter removes common contaminants before analysis. The Unipept API itself was also reworked, with an optimized index and flexible memory loading that roughly doubled query throughput and lowered the memory needed for deployment. We demonstrate these capabilities on six datasets of a defined microbial community.

APEs: Agentic Prediction of Epitopes to Guide Antibody-Antigen Structure Prediction

Kang, M., Yoon, M. S., +2 authors, Baek, M.

Abstract: Antibody-antigen complex prediction remains difficult because the surface recognized by a specific antibody is often uncertain, and model confidence may not distinguish alternative poses. We developed APEs (Agentic Prediction of Epitopes for Structure Prediction), an agentic system that retrieves public evidence, interprets predicted antibody and antigen structures, and produces ranked residue-level epitope hypotheses. For retrospective evaluation, web retrieval was restricted to sources released before each CASP target entry date. Across eight CASP16 and six released CASP17 targets, the top-ranked patch reached residue precision of at least 0.5 for nine of 14 targets. We then used the predicted epitopes to steer additional Protenix-v1 sampling and to compare steered finalists with the highest ipSAE unsteered model. The retrospective best selection across three independent router calls increased mean DockQ from 0.306 to 0.375; DockQ increased from 0.089 to 0.679 for H2321 and from 0.005 to 0.379 for H2346. These results show that publicly available evidence specific to the target antibody-antigen complex can serve as an inference-time signal for epitope localization, guided complex sampling, and model selection. We also release predictions for CASP17 targets whose experimental structures had not been released when the predictions were finalized, providing a prospective test of APEs (https://github.com/CSSB-SNU/ape).

In silico pipeline for identification of targets of plastic-derived chemicals

Halatian, A. G., Sawicki, L. R., +2 authors, Velez Rueda, A. J.

Abstract: Plastic pollution is a growing global health concern, yet the specific human protein targets mediating the toxic effects of plastic-derived chemicals (PDCs) remain largely unknown. Here, we present an integrative in silico pipeline that combines transcriptomic data with structural bioinformatics to identify putative human plastic-derived targets. Through comparative analysis with their canonical ligands, we identified 27 potential targets showing global binding energies comparable to endogenous ligands (-5.26 vs -5.60 kcal/mol), and six cases in which plastic-derived compounds performed better. Chemical similarity analysis revealed very low structural resemblance between PDCs and canonical ligands, indicating that PDCs do not act as structural mimetics but instead exploit different interactions within the same binding cavities. In a proof-of-concept case, we showed that Deoxyribonuclease I (DNase I) binds BP2 with higher affinity than its canonical ligand PO-3 (median -5.85 vs -2.55 kcal/mol). It also forms hydrogen bonds with Asn192 and {pi}-stacking interactions with the catalytic histidine His274, a pattern conserved in bovine DNase I. Experimental validation confirmed that BP2 at 10- M inhibits DNA hydrolysis by 21.5% in bovine DNase I (p-value < 0.01) and 23.2% in recombinant human DNase I (p-value < 0.001). Our work establishes a robust, transferable computational framework for identifying protein targets of environmental contaminants and reveals that PDCs engage their targets through novel non-mimetic binding modes, with structural patterns that can be conserved across species. These findings may have significant implications for the safety assessment of plastic additives.
