Targeting TMPRSS2-ERG Fusion-Driven Prostate Cancer using Novel HBS-α-Helicomimics

Kundu, S., Ganguly, P., +3 authors, Ateeq, B.

Abstract: Gene fusions between the androgen receptor (AR)-responsive gene TMPRSS2 and ERG occur in approximately 50% of prostate cancers (PCa), thereby promoting tumorigenesis through aberrant ERG expression. Therapeutic targeting of transcription factor-DNA interfaces remains a formidable challenge, largely owing to the entropic penalty associated with peptide folding during target engagement. Here, we describe the structure-guided design and preclinical evaluation of hydrogen-bond surrogate (HBS)-constrained alpha-helicomimics targeting the ERG-DNA recognition interface. Structural analysis of ERG-DNA complex revealed that DNA binding induces elongation of recognition helix H4 from approximately 2.5 to 4.0 helical turns. To stabilize the bioactive conformation, we incorporated an N-terminal HBS macrocycle, introduced D363E and S366E mutations to enhance DNA binding and structural stability, and added dual NLS-TAT tags to facilitate cellular delivery. Biochemical and cellular assays demonstrated that the lead compound, ERG helicomimics, competitively inhibited ERG binding to ETS motifs, disrupted ERG association with AR and other cofactors, leading to suppressed ERG transcriptional activity. Chromatin immunoprecipitation-qPCR confirmed reduced ERG occupancy at regulatory regions of canonical target genes, accompanied by downregulation of ERG-driven transcriptional programs. ERG helicomimics also reduced proliferation, migration, and invasion in ERG-positive PCa cells. In a murine xenograft model of TMPRSS2-ERG-positive PCa, systemic ERG helicomimics treatment suppressed tumor growth without overt toxicity and reduced Ki-67 expression compared with the designated control peptide. Collectively, our findings support HBS-constrained ERG helicomimics as a rational strategy for targeting ERG-driven PCa and warrant further therapeutic optimization.

An integrated data exploration engine of knowledge, expression and genetic association for human transposable elements

Jiang, X., Fang, Y., +5 authors, Chen, M.

Abstract: Transposable elements (TEs) constitute a substantial fraction of the human genome and contribute to genomic and epigenomic regulation. However, TE-related knowledge remains fragmented across the literature and databases, with inconsistent identifiers hindering integration and reuse. Here, we present TE-Dee (Transposable Element Data Exploration Engine), a curated resource that harmonizes literature-derived TE relationships with TE annotations, tissue-specific expression, and GTEx v11 eQTL associations. TE-Dee contains 10,387 entities and 9,583 curated relationships from 2,308 publications, 1,505 leaf-level TE classification records, and 11,334,060 instance-level observations generated by intersecting 640,456 reference TE intervals with significant variant-gene associations across 50 GTEx tissue categories. By linking molecular knowledge to genomic, transcriptomic and genetic evidence, TE-Dee provides reusable data for investigating TE biology, gene-regulatory and disease-related mechanisms, and a structured corpus for training and benchmarking data-driven and artificial intelligence models. TE-Dee is available at https://bis.zju.edu.cn/tedee/.

Non-exonic read content reorganises lncRNA co-expression networks in renal and other cancers

Youssef, G., Han, N.

Abstract: Co-expression networks are used to find prognostic lncRNA programmes, but lncRNA quantification depends on unprocessed RNA in the library. In renal cancer (1,062 patients), the leading lncRNA axis tracked the non-exonic (non-feature) read fraction, a routine alignment metric, at Spearman rho 0.90. Without adjustment, most of the lncRNA network merged into one module. Adjustment preserved agreement with single-cell tumour-normal differences. Under rules fixed with the renal results known, 9 of 25 TCGA cancer types had a lncRNA-specific axis and 4 had network collapse (8 and 2 of 23 non-renal types). Intronic lncRNAs tracked the metric more than intergenic ones in all 25, and the metric was not consistently prognostic. All five renal modules that survived adjustment kept their direction in an external cohort, two nominally significant and none at a 5% false discovery rate. Three localised to malignant or epithelial cells. Together they added no discrimination over TNM and grade.

Hidden introns and repeated gains shape the heavily interrupted tRNA genes of Asgard archaea

Goswami, S., Ghosh, A. K., Sinha, K.

Abstract: Transfer RNA (tRNA) genes are usually compact, yet in some Asgard archaea-the closest known prokaryotic relatives of eukaryotes-most are interrupted by introns. Studying this has been hampered by circularity: archaeal introns are catalogued by software that finds them partly through the splicing motif later used to explain where they persist. We built a motif-free census that detects intervening sequences by comparing each tRNA gene with compact relatives, and applied it to 90 Asgard genomes and 18 reference archaea. The standard annotator, tRNAscan-SE, had missed 30% of intervening sequences in the three focal families, mostly in the D-arm, but almost none in reference archaea; six independent checks supported the census. After correction, focal tRNA genes carry 2.2 intervening sequences on average, and in 30 genomes chosen before analysis the two focal families ranked first and second of 21 (exact P = 1/210). A reconstruction based on the census rather than on annotated introns placed 129 high-confidence gains at 65 loci on 37 branches of the species tree, 71 of them terminal; the two intron-rich classes are not sister lineages and carry mostly different intron positions, consistent with independent accumulation. Intervening sequences sat at canonical bulge-helix-bulge splicing motifs six to ten times more often than the average of three matched null models, including those the annotator missed and the reconstructed gains. Tests of copying between tRNA genes, mobile elements and reverse transcription found no general route of intron arrival. Asgard tRNA introns were thus gained repeatedly and sit preferentially at sites predicted to allow splicing; how they arrive remains open.

Deciphering Spatial Cellular Organization through RNA-Anchored Multi-Omics Deconvolution

Bai, Y., Guan, X., +7 authors, Dong, Q., liu, w.

Abstract: Spatial technologies that jointly profile RNA and additional molecular layers provide a comprehensive view of tissue organization, yet their limited resolution results in measurements that reflect mixtures of multiple cell types. While RNA-based deconvolution methods are well established, incorporating co-profiled non-transcriptomic modalities without compromising cell-type identifiability remains an open challenge, as reliable references are largely restricted to transcriptomic data. Here we present SMODER, an RNA-anchored framework for spatial multi-omics deconvolution that treats transcriptomes as the primary determinant of cell identity while incorporating complementary molecular modalities in a constrained manner. By adopting an asymmetric integration strategy, SMODER preserves RNA-driven cell-type resolution and enables robust estimation of cell-type compositions across spatial locations. Across simulations and diverse spatial multi-omics datasets spanning protein, chromatin accessibility and histone modification assays, SMODER consistently outperforms existing methods in both accuracy and spatial coherence. Beyond deconvolution, SMODER denoises multi-omic signals and enables cell-type-aware analysis of regulatory programs. Application to spatial CUT&Tag--RNA-seq and {RNA--ATAC-seq} data reveals spatially resolved epigenetic repression and cis-regulatory relationships linked to gene expression.

Dual-graph deconvolution of enzyme fitness: an interpretable framework to identify and explain catalytic modulators

Garcia Busto, S., Eschbach, E., +3 authors, Marks, D. S.

Abstract: Models trained on protein sequences can predict mutational effects, but their outputs represent a convolution of different phenotypes. Isolating stability-independent activity effects is essential for rational enzyme design, allosteric drug design and functional annotation, yet existing pLM-based deconvolution methods lack interpretability. Here, we introduce a hybrid framework that pairs stability predictors with probabilistic graphical models of coevolution. Benchmarked on all enzymes with available multidimensional deep mutational scanning data, our framework isolates stability-independent activity effects with a fidelity that matches analogous pLM-based approaches. Crucially, we reveal biophysically plausible communication pathways that align with previous knowledge and that we validate with experimental data across a diverse set of targets. This hybrid approach merges statistical and geometric deep learning into a zero-shot, explainable tool for targeted engineering and mechanistic discovery.

Systematic evaluation of transcript normalization methods and quantification pipelines across long-read sequencing platforms

Paniagua, A., Jetzinger, F., +1 author, Conesa, A.

Abstract: Long-read RNA sequencing (lrRNA-seq) has transformed transcriptomics by enabling full-length isoform resolution, yet transcript quantification remains confounded by platform-dependent length biases and tool choices. While a quantification normalization step is common in short-read RNA-seq, this has not yet been extended to lrRNA-seq and the field lacks a systematic analysis of normalization strategies applied to long reads data. Here, we systematically benchmarked quantification pipelines and downstream normalization strategies across Pacific Biosciences (PacBio) and Oxford Nanopore Technologies (ONT) platforms. Using synthetic spike-in controls (SIRVs and ERCC) alongside matched Illumina short-read benchmarks across mouse tissues and human cell models, we evaluated five normalization methods (CPM, TPM, CQN, EDASeq, and Scaled Median Ratio) across five quantification workflows (Bambu, FLAIR, IsoQuant, Oarfish, and Kallisto). Our framework assessed PacBio Iso-Seq and Kinnex protocols together with ONT cDNA and direct RNA (dRNA) sequencing datasets. We demonstrate that standard Transcripts Per Million (TPM) scaling counteracts short-molecule depletion in PacBio datasets, but severely distorts Nanopore expression estimates. For Nanopore libraries, covariate-aware normalizations (CQN and EDASeq) mitigate length bias without TPM's over-correction penalty. Conversely, relative median ratio normalization (SMR) degrades accuracy and introduces noise in low-variability biological samples. Across computational tools, FLAIR minimized absolute error on short SIRVs, while reference-restricted quantifiers (Oarfish and Kallisto) consistently outperformed transcript reconstruction tools in short-read validation. These findings show that quantification normalization should be applied to lrRNA-seq data and establish platform-specific guidelines for long-read transcriptomics.

Programmable design of functional RNAs through multimodal generative learning

Ma, J., Zhu, J., +11 authors, Zhang, Q. C.

Abstract: RNA engineering is bottlenecked by the tight coupling among sequence, structure, and function, leaving the field reliant on heuristics or screening. We introduce RNAverse, a multimodal generative model that learns a conditionable design grammar from RNA sequences, experimental structural probing profiles, and functional annotations. Beyond setting a new state of the art across 16 predictive modeling tasks, RNAverse inverts into generative design across four distinct RNA classes through three regimes, requiring experimental testing of no more than 20 candidates per-task. Zero-shot in-painting generates snoRNAs that promote premature termination codon (PTC) readthrough, and chimeric aptazymes for ligand-controlled gene regulation, exceeding rational design or large-scale screens. In both settings, RNAverse outperforms family-adapted sequence-only baselines. Fine-tuned in-painting extends to an unseen functional class, yielding IGFBP3-binding aptamers with picomolar affinity that outperform SELEX winners. Iterative optimization engineers 5' UTRs that outperform clinical benchmarks, enhancing protein production in cell-based assays for both the Zolgensma gene therapy and the BNT162b2 COVID-19 vaccine, and boosting antibody responses to BNT162b2 in mice. Together, these results establish multimodal generative learning as a route from predictive RNA modeling to programmable specification of functional RNAs.

Longitudinal treatment selection under irreversible discontinuation: History-aware prediction and trajectory-aware learning in peanut-allergy prevention

Taub, R., Wasserman, T., Savir, Y.

Abstract: Clinical decision-support systems that guide longitudinal treatment must determine not only which treatment is preferred, but also when the available evidence is sufficient to change course. This challenge is especially important when discontinuation is irreversible, because stopping too early prevents later information from informing care. Food allergy is a growing public-health concern that can cause severe reactions and imposes substantial medical, dietary, and psychosocial burdens. Prevention is therefore a major clinical priority. In peanut-allergy prevention, this longitudinal decision is particularly consequential because resuming peanut consumption after discontinuation may be unsafe without renewed clinical assessment. In this work, we introduce a framework for preferred-treatment prediction and evaluate it using data from 628 children assigned to early peanut consumption or avoidance in the Learning Early About Peanut Allergy trial. We first introduced visit-wise and history-aware models that update recommendations as clinical and immunological measurements accumulate. Preferred treatment was poorly discriminated at baseline but became substantially more predictable during follow-up, with history-aware models achieving area-under-the-curve values of about 0.9 at later visits. However, imposing irreversible discontinuation after training significantly reduce the performance of these models. We therefore developed TreatNet, which jointly optimizes visit-specific predictors through a trajectory-level objective representing the downstream consequences of early discontinuation. Under the same irreversible constraint, TreatNet achieved accuracies of 0.805-0.839 and area-under-the-curve values of 0.819-0.869 across follow-up visits and two alternative preferred-treatment definitions. TreatNet generally preserved consumption while baseline evidence was limited and selectively recommended discontinuation after more informative measurements became available. These findings show that longitudinal information is most useful when model training reflects how current actions restrict future options. Although evaluated in peanut-allergy prevention, TreatNet provides a general framework for decision-support problems involving irreversible or difficult-to-reverse treatment changes.

SMARTFlexDB: a paired apo-holo RNA structure database and analysis platform for RNA conformational flexibility and small-molecule recognition

Jiang, S., Wei, J., +5 authors, Li, Y.

Abstract: RNA structures can undergo substantial conformational changes upon small-molecule binding, yet existing structural resources provide only static RNA-ligand snapshots without connecting ligand-bound (holo) RNA structures to corresponding ligand-free (apo) conformations. SMARTFlexDB is a curated database and analysis platform that uses paired apo-holo RNA structures to characterize conformational differences and RNA-small-molecule recognition. Candidate pairs were identified from the Protein Data Bank by matching RNA chains with at least 90% sequence identity and coverage, and fully conserved binding pocket nucleotides. The current release contains 434 binding pockets from 266 holo structures, linked to 186 apo structures to yield 1,187 pocket-level apo-holo pairs organized into 734 database entries, spanning 150 small molecules and 23 RNA functional classes. Each entry integrates RNA sequence and secondary-structure annotations, apo and holo three-dimensional structures, RNA-ligand atomic interactions, and quantitative descriptors of global and local conformational changes. An interactive web interface enables users to explore sequence alignments and structural overlays, compare secondary structures and RNA motifs, and upload paired apo-holo structures for customized analysis. By linking ligand-bound and ligand-free RNA structures, SMARTFlexDB provides a robust foundation for investigating RNA structural flexibility and supporting RNA-targeted small-molecule drug discovery. The database and analysis platform are available at https://aidd.rc.ufl.edu/app/smartflexdb/.

Optical biosensors for cancer diagnosis and monitoring.

Heru Agung Saputra, Md Ali Zaber Sahin, Yeni Wahyuni Hartati

Abstract: Cancer is driven by complex genetic and epigenetic alterations that promote uncontrolled cellular proliferation, invasion, and metastasis. Despite substantial advances in oncology, conventional diagnostic approaches remain constrained by invasiveness, cost, limited sensitivity, and limited capacity to detect malignancies at early stages. Optical biosensors have emerged as promising platforms for sensitive, rapid, and non- or minimally invasive cancer biomarker detection, offering opportunities for real-time, label-free analysis of biofluids. These systems integrate highly selective biorecognition elements with optical transducers that interrogate molecular interactions. Recent advances in nanomaterials and nanophotonic architectures have further improved analytical sensitivity, while artificial intelligence (AI) and machine-learning approaches enable multiplexed biomarker profiling, pattern recognition, and increasingly precise diagnostic interpretation. This review critically presents the fundamental principles, sensor configurations, and applications of optical biosensors for cancer detection and monitoring, with particular emphasis on technological advances that underpin sensitivity, specificity, multiplexing, and clinical translation. The review also discusses key barriers to widespread implementation, including reproducibility, scalability, biofluid complexity, and standardization. Emerging directions, including quantum-enhanced sensing, advanced nanophotonics, and AI-driven analytics, are evaluated in the context of next-generation precision diagnostics. By integrating photonics, molecular biology, nanotechnology, and bioengineering, optical biosensors have strong potential to enable earlier, non-invasive or minimally invasive, patient-centric cancer detection and contribute to more effective precision oncology.

OM-sqPCR enables one-step multiplex quantification of circulating small-RNA subclasses for colorectal cancer classification.

Danping Guan, Xiang Li, +16 authors, Yanbo Wang

Abstract: Accurate multiplex quantification of circulating small RNAs (sRNAs) enables comprehensive evaluation of their diagnostic potential. Here, we develop OM-sqPCR, a closed-tube, one-step multiplex RT-qPCR platform that simultaneously quantifies microRNAs, transfer RNA-derived sRNAs (tsRNAs), and ribosomal RNA-derived sRNAs (rsRNAs). By integrating optimized reaction chemistry for short RNA templates with computational primer-probe design, OM-sqPCR enables coordinated amplification of multiple sRNA subclasses in a single reaction. Applied to a multicenter cohort of 651 participants, OM-sqPCR identified a four-sRNA classifier that discriminated colorectal cancer from controls with an area under the ROC curve of 0.97, 85.9% sensitivity, and 93.1% specificity, with sustained performance in early-stage disease. The platform also supported measurement of previously reported sRNA panels for renal and thyroid cancer. These findings establish OM-sqPCR as a practical approach for multiplex circulating sRNA analysis and clinical biomarker evaluation.

Artificial intelligence for brain cancer management: multimodal digital workflows for diagnosis, treatment planning, and longitudinal monitoring.

Ganesan Saibaba, Olawumi Giwa, +14 authors, Milana Frenkel-Morgenstern

Abstract: Brain cancers, especially glioblastoma, remain among the deadliest adult cancers, with outcomes largely unchanged despite multimodal treatments. This review summarizes cutting-edge artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning (ML) advances transforming neuro-oncology in diagnostics, molecular profiling, treatment planning, and monitoring. Key findings show AI-driven radiomics and deep learning (DL) reaching over 90% accuracy in tumour segmentation and grading from MRI and whole-slide images, non-invasive IDH/MGMT prediction through liquid biopsy (LB) analysis, and augmented reality-guided resection that maximizes tumour removal while safeguarding expressive cortex. Treatment planning benefits from hybrid Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN)-Transformer models for immunotherapy stratification and blood-brain barrier penetrant drug repurposing, while real-time progression detection via multimodal integration helps differentiate true progression from pseudoprogression. Despite these advances, significant challenges remain, including data scarcity and imbalance in rare subtypes, domain shift due to imaging variability, black-box model behaviour eroding trust, regulatory requirements for prospective validation, and workflow fragmentation. Emerging solutions include federated and transfer learning for scalable model development, explainable AI (such as SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and attention interpretation for vision transformers) to foster clinician-AI collaboration, and foundation models pretrained on large-scale neuro-oncology datasets to facilitate personalization. Achieving this potential will depend on harmonized multi-omics registries, strong ethical and regulatory governance, and deliberate human-AI collaboration frameworks to integrate these tools into precision neuro-oncology.
