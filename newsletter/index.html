Cross-Species Dynamics of Transposable Elements in Aging Female Visceral Adipose Tissues

Ali, S. S., Gaire, A., +8 authors, Wang, J.

Abstract: Transposable elements (TEs) make up nearly half of mammalian genomes and are increasingly implicated in genomic instability, cellular stress responses, aging, and age-related disease. Recent past research has highlighted their significant involvement in disease models such as cancer and Alzheimer's disease (AD), though much remains to be unraveled regarding these genomic insertions. In this study, we explore the involvement of TEs in aging female mouse and human visceral adipose tissue (VAT) using 10x single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) data analyzed with the computational tool SoloTE. By assessing the TE-annotated snRNA-seq count matrix using Seurat, we found that retrotransposons-specifically short interspersed nuclear elements (SINEs) and long interspersed nuclear elements (LINEs) on chromosomes 1 and 19-are significantly upregulated in aging female human VAT and in potential senescent cells (CDKN1A^+). Although changes in mouse VAT were less pronounced, long terminal repeats (LTRs) were predominant in aging mouse cells. Furthermore, analysis of upregulated features in aging and CDKN1A^+ female human VAT cells revealed that LINE and SINE TEs embedded within specific gene loci-most notably CUX1, SOD2, C3, NAMPT, ALDH1A3, IL6, and the non-coding RNA LOC105375913-exhibit a positive regulatory relationship with their host genes. Specifically, higher TE expression may directly upregulate host gene expression (e.g., C3 TEs driving C3 upregulation). Consistently ranking at the top of differentially expressed feature lists, these TEs act as potential drivers of aging and cellular senescence in female human VAT and can be plausible targets without risk of disruption of multiple biological systems.

BEguided: Systematic guide and base editor evaluation for precision single-nucleotide editing

Schmitt, L. T., Wang, P., +3 authors, Sayed, S.

Abstract: Base editing enables precise nucleotide substitutions without introducing DNA double-strand breaks, but identifying optimal editor-guide combinations remains challenging because PAM compatibility, editing-window positioning, bystander modifications, and off-target effects must be considered simultaneously. Here, we introduce BEguided, an integrated computational platform for variant-specific guide RNA design and systematic comparison across 38 predefined base-editor profiles, with support for user-defined editors. BEguided incorporates single-nucleotide variants directly into target sequences, eliminating the need for customized reference genomes, while its companion tool, BEguidedOFF, provides base-editing-aware off-target prediction. To demonstrate the utility of this approach, we systematically assessed the accessibility of the 1,000 most frequent ABE-editable cancer mutations to adenine base editing. Under stringent guide-quality, bystander-editing, and predicted off-target criteria, only 7 mutations could be targeted using SpCas9-ABE8e. Expanding the analysis to additional SpCas9-compatible adenine base editors increased this number to 30, while inclusion of SaCas9, relaxed-PAM Cas9-NG/SpG variants, and PAM-less SpRY further expanded the number of potentially targetable mutations to 100, 156, and 238, respectively. These findings highlight the substantial impact of editor selection on mutation accessibility and establish BEguided as a versatile framework for identifying and prioritizing base-editing strategies for disease-associated variants. BEguided is available at https://beguided.buchholzlab.org.

Sequence-Derived Geometric Priors Enable Controllable Generation of Intrinsically Disordered Protein Ensembles

Jang, W., Shin, W.-H.

Abstract: Predicting heterogeneous conformational ensembles from sequence remains a central challenge for intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs). Sequence-to-ensemble models often entangle geometric inference with coordinate generation, obscuring error sources and limiting control over ensemble properties. Here we introduce MANTA, a C-level framework that separates these stages. An encoder derives geometric priors from protein language-model representations, and a graph-Laplacian decoder converts these priors into conformational ensembles. On a homology-held-out test set, replacing predicted priors with reference-derived priors under the same decoder reduces the median absolute relative radius-of-gyration error from 8.78% to 1.89%, identifying geometric-prior estimation as the dominant bottleneck in MANTA. Across an external benchmark of 27 IDPs, MANTA generates broad, locally valid ensembles. Decoder controls modulate mean compactness and conformational breadth along partially separable axes, while compactness modulation alters motif exposure in tau and amyloid-{beta}. Meanwhile, -synuclein variants shift local exposure populations at nearly unchanged global size. MANTA provides a diagnostic and controllable framework for ensemble generation that captures both global dimensions and local conformational organization.

SIREN: Predicting RNA-Small Molecule Interaction using Language Models without Requiring Structure

Jang, W., Kim, Y., +2 authors, Shin, W.-H.

Abstract: RNA-targeted drug discovery is constrained by the scarcity of resolved RNA tertiary structures and the conformational flexibility of RNA, which limit the applicability of structure-based virtual screening. Here, SIREN (Small molecule Interaction with RNA via sEquence-based predictioN) is presented, a sequence-based framework that ranks small molecules for a given RNA target directly from its primary sequence without requiring tertiary structure. SIREN integrates a pretrained RNA language model with molecular language model embeddings and physicochemical descriptors through an interaction network that outputs a target-specific compound-ranking score. On four held-out ROBIN riboswitch targets, SIREN outperforms the structure-based methods RNAmigos2, RNAmigos++, and rDock in both enrichment and overall discrimination, and a cross-target derangement analysis confirms that its rankings depend on RNA identity rather than general ligand propensity alone. On an independent HIV-1 TAR benchmark, SIREN achieves strong discrimination without an experimentally derived RNA conformational ensemble. Prospective screening against IRES-derived RNA hairpins from cMyc and XIAP mRNAs further identifies multiple experimentally validated, micromolar-affinity binders from a diverse chemical library. These results demonstrate that SIREN generalizes to unseen RNA targets, transfers across independent benchmarks, and provides a scalable, structure-free framework for RNA-targeted compound prioritization that complements existing structure-based screening approaches.

Period-Specific Remodeling of Luminal Lineages and the Mammary Microenvironment in Dairy Goats

Ni, M., He, Z., +8 authors, Li, C.

Abstract: Milk production requires coordinated remodeling of mammary epithelial lineages and their stromal and immune niches, but this process is incompletely resolved in dairy goats. We aim to define period specific cell states and epithelial niche interactions across the dry to lactation transition. We profile mammary tissue from three peak-lactation and three dry-period Saanen goats using 10x Genomics single-cell RNA sequencing and map one peak-lactation goat with Stereo-seq. Among 44,120 cells, we identify15 populations. Peak lactation is characterized by expansion of luminal alveolar cells and increased {beta}-casein and -lactalbumin expression, whereas luminal progenitors, hormone-sensing cells, fibroblasts, and B cells are enriched in the dry period. Trajectory inference place luminal progenitors near branches toward alveolar and hormone-sensing states, with sustained ELF5-associated activity along the alveolar branch. Myoepithelial, macrophage, natural killer T, and T cell states are also remodeled. SPP1 and MIF signaling networks become more connected and change their cellular targets. These data establish a period resolved atlas of dairy goat mammary remodeling and identify coordinated epithelial, stromal, immune, and communication programs associated with active lactation.

RR3DD 2.0: an expanded database for RNA three-dimensional structure classification

Li, D., Liu, X., +1 author, Zhu, Y., liu, s.

Abstract: RNA performs diverse biological functions by folding into specific 3D structures, which are often more conserved than their sequences. Systematic classification of RNA 3D structures is fundamental to understanding structure-function relationships. We previously established RR3DD, the first classification database based on global 3D structural similarity. However, its version 1.0 contained data only up to July 2021 and can no longer reflect the rapidly growing RNA structure space since then. Here, we present RR3DD 2.0. Starting from the 7,466 PDB-format chains in 1.0, we incorporated 8,298 newly released or updated chains, expanding the dataset to 15,764 chains. Pairwise structural similarity (RMscore) was calculated using RMalign, and all chains were re-clustered to yield 1,042 clusters. Compared with 1.0, 2.0 not only increases sampling density but also identifies 342 new clusters. Notably, Cluster-891 and Cluster-899 correspond to the ROOL RNA and GOLLD RNA families, marking the first inclusion of these large structured bacterial ncRNAs into a global classification framework. Mapping the clusters to Rfam annotated 6,388 chains (40.5%) across 154 families. A single cluster can correspond to one or several Rfam families, indicating that global structure classification and sequence family classification are complementary. RR3DD 2.0 provides an updated web interface.

Replicate libraries set the noise floor for cell-state claims in cross-sectional single-cell data: an empirical audit in drug-tolerant EGFR-mutant PC9 cells

Raza, W., Mehmood, S., +1 author, Barbosa da Silva, F. A.

Abstract: Claims that drug-tolerant cancer cells occupy stable states rest on single-cell snapshots with one sequencing library per condition, where a difference between conditions cannot be separated from a difference between libraries. No standard says how far a contrast must exceed replicate-library variation. We contribute a replicate-referenced framework: a distance ratio expressing a contrast as a multiple of the distance between replicate libraries of one condition, and a library-level permutation test giving exact p values by relabelling whole libraries. In simulations, single-library tests and a split-half reference rejected a true null in 99 to 100% of runs, and Harmony followed by a cell-level test did so in 12 to 32% with structured library effects. With four libraries per arm, the library-level test held false positives at 3 to 5% in every setting. In public osimertinib-treated PC9 data with two libraries per condition (GSE150949), halves of one library were indistinguishable, replicate libraries separated at 59 to 93% accuracy, and contrasts between times in drug and sort gates exceeded replicate distance 4.5- to 51-fold. In Drop-seq erlotinib data with one library per condition (GSE149383), two libraries of the same condition separated more readily (93.3%) than withdrawn and re-treated cells (88.5%). Cross-sectional contrasts should be reported as multiples of replicate-library distance and, with four or more libraries per arm, tested by library-level permutation. Cells moved away from the untreated profile and stayed away, but the data do not support attractor-style inference, nor do they show that tolerant states are not bistable.

MDQ: A DockQ-Inspired Scoring Function for Assessing Interface Similarity and Clustering in Molecular Dynamics

Ehles, J., Barbault, F., MICLOT, T.

Abstract: Assessing the similarity between protein-protein interfaces is essential for evaluating docking models, yet widely used metrics such as DockQ rely on a single native reference structure and yield asymmetric scores, making them unsuitable for molecular dynamics (MD) trajectories where no reference exists and frame-to-frame comparisons are required. Here, we introduce the MDQ, a fully symmetric reformulation of DockQ designed for MD trajectory analysis. MDQ replaces the fraction of native contacts with the Fraction of Shared Contacts (FSC), and redefines interface and ligand RMSD as weighted variants that penalize low atomic overlap between compared frames. The score can be used to follow structure/contacts convergence of an interface along a trajectory. And it is inverted to produce a true distance metric ranging from 0 (identical interfaces) to 1 (distinct interfaces), directly compatible with distance-based clustering. Applied to two benchmark complexes, Ail-Vitronectin and TGFBR1-FKBP12, MDQ heatmaps reveal global interface stability alongside subtle conformational rearrangements, while DBSCAN and hierarchical clustering of the MDQ distance matrix identifies representative conformational states. Then non-bonding interaction found in clusters were identified by MICLOT software. MDQ thus provides a robust and frame-to-frame symmetric framework for monitoring protein-protein interface conformational and interaction heterogeneity throughout MD simulations.

Combinatorial perturbation structure enables graded inference of signaling activity by CIRCE

Hutchins, N. T., Maffa, S., +1 author, Li, P.

Abstract: Cells decode external inputs into intracellular activities of varying magnitude, and inferring both input identity and strength is crucial for cell-fate engineering. However, perturbation-response models typically treat all cells exposed to a given input as a discrete class, ignoring cell-to-cell variation in response magnitude. Here, we introduce a structured-label framework that infers both input identity and strength from combinatorial perturbation screens without dose-resolved training labels. We represent each perturbation combination in a binary combinatorial space and replace one-hot supervision with a Boltzmann distribution that decays with the Hamming distance between combinations. This approach preserves classification accuracy while improving calibration and resolving continuous pathway activities across individual cells. Importantly, intermediate scores primarily reflect ambiguity in cellular response states rather than insufficient model knowledge. Applied to engineered and natural tissues, the calibrated model successfully recovered graded activities. These results establish combinatorial perturbation screens as a self-calibrating framework for decoding graded cellular responses.

Pangenome-guided interpretation of k-mer GWAS inCapsicum

Papastolopoulou, C., Flege, P., +4 authors, Smit, S.

Abstract: Agronomic trait diversity in Capsicum is shaped by sequence variation that it is not always captured by SNPs called against a single reference genome. Reference-free k-mer GWAS can detect variants beyond SNPs, but significant k-mers are difficult to interpret as they lack genomic coordinates and annotation. In this study, we developed a pangenome-guided workflow to localize significant k-mers in a graph-based Capsicum pangenome. The workflow connects significant k-mers, identified through k-mer GWAS, to genomic positions, their annotations and homology groups. We applied this approach to a metabolite dataset of 343 C. annuum accessions, focusing on the cases of capsaicinoids and specific aroma compounds. For capsaicinoid content, our workflow provided insight in the haplotype composition of the sequenced accessions in the Pun1 locus and the genomic variations contributing to pungency. In the case of dehydroxylinalool oxide, the analysis identified that the haplotype associated to high phenotypes contained an extra terpene synthase (TPS). And last, the production of -ylangene seems to be influenced by a 2 Mb inversion on chromosome 9, right next to a terpene synthase gene. Integrating k-mer GWAS with graph-pangenome localization improves the interpretation of k-mer association signals in complex plant genomes. This workflow can help identify structural variation, haplotypes and candidate loci relevant for trait/metabolic diversity and future breeding applications.

Spatial metabolic ecosystems shape the architecture of IDH-mutant glioma

Moffet, J. J., Kriel, J., +20 authors, Best, S. A.

Abstract: The principles governing tissue organization in IDH-mutant glioma remain poorly understood, particularly in the absence of hypoxia and necrosis that define IDH-wildtype glioblastoma. Here, we integrated spatial transcriptomics, metabolomics and histology across co-registered serial tissue sections, generating a multidimensional atlas of IDH-mutant glioma. Integration with an independent spatial transcriptomics cohort and a single cell atlas (n=101 samples), enabled resolution of metabolic and cellular heterogeneity underlying tissue organization. We identified twelve conserved spatial niches with distinct metabolic identities that could not be explained by cellular composition or histology alone. Beyond these ecosystems, we discovered a transcriptionally and epigenetically intermediate malignant cell state enriched in WHO grade 2/3 oligodendroglioma, and spatially segregated myeloid programs. Importantly, progression following chemoradiotherapy was associated with expansion of a glutathione-rich, NRF2-associated antioxidant niche. Together, our findings establish metabolic specialization as a fundamental dimension of glioma organization, providing new insight into tumor heterogeneity, progression and treatment-associated adaptation.

Targeting the YAP1/TAZ-TEAD Transcriptional Axis Reveals a Therapeutic Vulnerability in Androgen Receptor Signaling Inhibitor (ARSI)-Resistant Prostate Cancer

Mohan, V., Lamichhane, S., +4 authors, Koul, H. K.

Abstract: Therapeutic resistance remains a major barrier in advanced prostate cancer (PCa), particularly after prolonged treatment with second-generation androgen receptor signaling inhibitors (ARSIs) such as enzalutamide. Tumors can evade these drugs by escaping androgen receptor (AR) dependence through cellular plasticity and lineage transitions, but alternative resistance networks remain poorly understood. Enzalutamide-resistant (ENZ-R) PCa cells showed marked activation and nuclear enrichment of the YAP-1/TAZ-TEAD complex. Disrupting this transcriptional interface with CA3 shows strong anti-metastatic effects, inhibiting migration, invasion, and colony formation across AR-dependent and AR-independent PCa cell lines, suggesting efficacy regardless of cellular lineage or AR status. Similar effects with GNE-7883, K-975, and siRNA knockdown supported a class effect. In vivo, CA3 treatment substantially reduces tumor burden in PC3 and DU145 xenograft models, supporting the systemic efficacy of disrupting the YAP-1/TAZ-TEAD axis in aggressive, AR-independent disease. Mechanistically, this anti-tumor response is driven by potent cytotoxicity: CA3 induces substantial DNA damage and apoptosis in both in vitro and in vivo settings. Moreover, we identified increased YAP-1/WWTR1(Taz) expression as a negative predictor of therapeutic response in PCa patients. These findings indicate that enzalutamide resistance creates a targetable dependence on functional Hippo transcription. Small-molecule disruption of YAP-1/TAZ-TEAD may offer a rational strategy to counter treatment resistance and lineage escape in advanced PCa.

TERRA engages MDA5 to shape the immune landscape in ALT brain tumors

Pecchini, I., Bontempi, V., +9 authors, Mione, M.

Abstract: In cancer, telomere maintenance is mediated by telomerase reactivation or by the alternative lengthening of telomere (ALT) pathway. Both mechanisms contribute to cancer cell replicative immortality, but ALT, generates telomere instability and promotes inflammation, thus contributing to cancer progression also through non-cell autonomous mechanisms. How ALT telomeres modulate inflammatory signals in cancer is not fully understood, mostly because of the lack of organismal models of ALT+ cancers. Here we used a zebrafish model of ALT+ brain tumor to demonstrate that ALT telomeres promote inflammation through increased transcription of TERRA, which engages RNA-sensing pathways through MDA5. Analysis of ALT+ brain tumors shows that MDA5-MAVS signaling results in the production of interferon responsive genes and cytokines that induce a pro-tumoral immune microenvironment, which can be prevented by abrogating TERRA signaling through in vivo antisense technology, or by CRISPR/Cas9 mediated knock-out of MDA5 or MAVS. Single-cell transcriptomic analysis reveals that abrogating TERRA signaling using ASOs affects both cancer cells and immune cell compartments in a long-lasting manner. These findings reveal a previously unrecognized role of TERRA in modulating immune responses in ALT+ brain tumors and highlight the potential of TERRA-targeting ASOs as a novel therapeutic strategy for ALT tumors.

DriverDBv5: a multi-omics integration database for cancer driver gene research.

Yo-Liang Lai, Yu-De Wang, +11 authors, Wei-Chung Cheng

Abstract: Cancer is driven by alterations across multiple molecular layers, but whether multi-omics drivers carry prognostic weight has received little systematic attention. DriverDBv5 (https://driverdb.bioinfomics.org/) extends DriverDB from driver identification to prognostic evaluation. It has been expanded to 187 cohorts, 43 862 patients, and 129 655 samples, with 154 cohorts from non-TCGA (The Cancer Genome Atlas) repositories reconciled by a generalized multi-omics barcode. Driver genes can be evaluated with Cox and mixture cure models, with machine learning on the full multi-omics feature matrix, and for the joint prognostic effect of alterations in two omics layers. The Customized Analysis module has grown from 4 to 10 tools for user-defined gene sets, subgroups, and covariates, and a human body map offers an anatomical entry point. DriverDBv5 thus reveals not only which alterations drive a tumor, but also which of them relate to patient outcome.
