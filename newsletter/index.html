Somatic Structural Variant Discovery from Tumor Specific Strings Computed Against a Draft Composite Germline Reference

Monti, D. C., Ciccolella, S., +4 authors, Hormozdiari, F.

Abstract: Somatic structural variants (SVs) are among the most clinically relevant genomic alterations in cancer. However, their accurate detection remains challenging due to complex genomic rearrangements, repetitive genomic regions, difficulties in distinguishing somatic variants from germline variation and sequencing artifacts, and limited sequencing support for low-frequency events. Existing mapping-based somatic SV callers are susceptible to alignment ambiguities in repetitive regions and near complex SV breakpoints, while approaches relying on comparisons of independently generated tumor and normal variant callsets may propagate errors from both callsets. Building on the concept of specific strings, previously applied to germline SV discovery, we introduce VESPA-SS, a novel approach for detecting somatic SVs from paired tumor-normal long-read whole-genome sequencing data. VESPA-SS directly extracts somatic SV signals using specific strings computed from tumor reads against a Draft Composite Germline Reference, without requiring initial read mapping or a high-quality germline assembly. These signals are subsequently clustered and refined through consensus sequence generation and alignment-based procedures to identify somatic SVs. Evaluation on real PacBio HiFi datasets demonstrates that VESPA-SS outperforms state-of-the-art somatic SV callers, achieving F1 scores exceeding 0.92 and 0.90 on the well-characterized HG008 and COLO829 cancer cell lines, respectively, compared with the highest F1 scores of 0.86 and 0.85 achieved by the evaluated methods. These results demonstrate the effectiveness of specific string based signal extraction for accurate somatic SV discovery and establish a new computational paradigm for identifying complex genomic alterations in cancer. VESPA-SS is freely available at https://github.com/dcmonti/VESPA.

LeadWeaver: Topology-Aware Masked-Lead Modeling for Robust 12-Lead ECG Diagnosis

Schneider, F., Collins, N., Weber, L. A.

Abstract: Clinical electrocardiography is organized as a structured set of correlated leads, yet most neural ECG classifiers assume that all twelve leads are present, correctly ordered, and acquired under the same device conditions at training and deployment. We propose LeadWeaver, a topology-aware ECG representation model designed for diagnosis under missing, corrupted, or device-shifted leads. LeadWeaver represents each lead as a temporal token stream, couples leads through a fixed-plus-learned physiological lead graph, and performs masked-lead latent completion rather than waveform synthesis. A reliability router estimates which observed leads should dominate each diagnostic decision, while a consistency objective aligns complete-lead and incomplete-lead predictions. We evaluate the model on PTB-XL and CPSC-style settings using complete-input diagnosis, arbitrary missing-lead subsets, lead corruption, cross-dataset transfer, calibration, and selective prediction. The central hypothesis is that robust ECG diagnosis requires explicitly modeling both inter-lead topology and uncertainty about unavailable evidence, rather than treating missing channels as zero-valued inputs.

MOCHI enables systematic identification of candidate independent intronic transcriptional signals from short-read RNA-seq data

Gurer-Er, D. C., Karakulah, G., +1 author, Akgul, B.

Abstract: Standard short-read RNA-seq analysis pipelines quantify expression over annotated exons and disregard signals mapped to introns, thereby missing independently transcribed intronic RNAs. Existing strategies depend on annotation, biologically crude whole-intron quantification, or subjective, low-throughput manual genome-browser inspection. We present MOCHI (Mapping of Cryptic Hidden Intronic Transcripts), a framework that couples reference-independent, k-mer-based signal discovery with reference-guided genomic interpretation and a custom multi-step filtering module to systematically curate potential independent intronic transcription units from strand-specific short-read RNA-seq data. On two simulated datasets, MOCHI recovered annotated sense-intronic long non-coding RNAs removed from the reference and distinguished them from intronic signal confounders. Across paired ribosomal RNA-depleted and poly(A)-selected libraries from ten cancer cell lines, MOCHI curated candidates whose host introns are strongly enriched for a transcription-initiation signal. Candidate expression is largely uncoupled from host-gene exons, and their genomic features resemble those of annotated long non-coding RNAs. In ten matched cohorts from The Cancer Genome Atlas, candidates were detected in tumor samples, and a subset was associated with overall survival after adjustment for age and host-gene expression. Consequently, MOCHI extends the transcriptomic landscape that can be mined from existing short-read RNA-seq data and provides a curated resource of candidate intronic transcripts for downstream analyses.

scCircaSim: A Circadian Simulator for Single-Cell and Spatial Transcriptomics

Li, C., Zhang, L., +7 authors, Sun, P.

Abstract: Motivation: Simulated single-cell and spatial transcriptomic datasets that capture circadian dynamics while providing known ground truth remain scarce, limiting systematic evaluation of circadian analysis methods. Results: We developed scCircaSim to generate circadian single-cell and spatial transcriptomic data from a single-time-point reference. The framework combines phase progression with reference-derived network propagation of anchor gene rhythms and incorporates neighborhood effects for spatial simulation. Outputs retain reference heterogeneity and provide known cell or spot phases, rhythmicity labels, and gene-level mesor, amplitude, and phase values. Validation showed retention of reference structure and recovery of specified temporal patterns. Benchmarks of four cell phase inference methods and three rhythmic gene analysis methods revealed differences in phase accuracy, rhythmicity detection, and parameter recovery. scCircaSim supports reproducible method evaluation and preliminary experimental planning. Availability and Implementation: scCircaSim is implemented in R and available at https://github.com/Liwei-DynamicsLab/scCircaSim.

Deducing splicing events from transcript sets with splicelogic

Campillo, B., Love, M. I.

Abstract: Long-read RNA sequencing captures a greater proportion of full-length transcripts, enabling more accurate isoform identification, discovery of novel isoforms, and more precise differential transcript usage analysis. However, comparing transcripts, whether between conditions or between sets such as novel and annotated isoforms, yields complex isoform changes that are hard to manipulate, summarize, and visualize. This is compounded by vertical integration of some tools, where downstream analysis depends on the specific output of a particular upstream tool. To address this gap, we have developed splicelogic, an R/Bioconductor package that makes transcript-set comparisons biologically interpretable by turning them into discrete splicing events, such as exon skipping, alternative splice sites, intron retention, and alternative transcription start and end sites. We designed splicelogic to integrate easily with existing RNA-seq workflows and tools. The splicelogic functions are modular, readable, and extensible, making it easy for users to adapt core logic to their own custom scientific questions or novel splicing definitions. By obtaining interpretable splicing events from transcript changes, splicelogic enables downstream analyses, including annotation of RNA-binding protein recognition sites, alongside summary statistics and visualization. The splicelogic package is publicly available and distributed through Bioconductor.

Joint protein-DNA language modeling enables prediction and engineering of DNA recognition specificity

Xiong, Y., Ren, J., +3 authors, Wei, W.

Abstract: Protein-DNA recognition specificity underlies transcriptional regulation, DNA replication, chromatin remodeling and genome editing. Protein-DNA recognition depends on both interacting sequences, yet language models that learn this cross-molecular dependence explicitly during pretraining remain limited. Here, we developed the Protein-DNA Language Models (PDLMs), interaction-aware language models that jointly represent protein and DNA sequences. PDLMs were built by continued pretraining from ESM2 with masked language modeling on paired protein-DNA sequences. A shared encoder places amino acids and nucleotides in the same molecular context, enabling conditional sequence scoring and transferable pair representations. We further introduced a domain-aware masking strategy that increased masking probability within DNA-binding domains to strengthen learning of interaction-relevant regions. Across downstream tasks, PDLMs improved protein-DNA pair classification relative to independent-encoder baselines and supported prediction of mutation-associated binding changes. Zero-shot scoring recovered known changes in DNA-recognition specificity and prioritized variants with higher editing activity. Learned representations also supported cross-study prediction of base editor activity. After fine-tuning on transcription factor binding data, PDLMs predicted DNA motifs directly from protein sequences. Together, PDLMs provide a framework for interaction-aware biomolecular language modeling, with potential applications in protein engineering and genome editing.

Development of a Pan-Proteome Multi-Epitope Vaccine Candidate Against Nipah Virus Using Reverse Vaccinology and Immunoinformatics Approaches

Nishan, R. K., Nahian, M., +9 authors, Islam, M. S.

Abstract: Nipah virus (NiV) is a high-consequence zoonotic pathogen for which no licensed human vaccine is currently available. This study applied a consensus-guided pan-proteome immunoinformatics framework to design and prioritize a multi-epitope vaccine candidate using sequence information from all nine NiV proteins. A total of 901 NiV protein sequences were retrieved, of which 842 passed quality filtering and were processed through clustering, multiple-sequence alignment, and protein-specific consensus generation. Consensus-derived fragments were screened for cytotoxic T-lymphocyte, helper T-lymphocyte, and B-cell epitopes, followed by multi-criterion filtering based on antigenicity, allergenicity, toxicity, conservancy, class-specific immune-response properties, and human-proteome similarity. Ten epitope-selection strategies were combined with two adjuvant candidates and three construct architectures to generate 60 vaccine constructs. Shortlisted candidates were subsequently evaluated for physicochemical properties, HLA population coverage, structural characteristics, conformational B-cell epitopes, TLR3 docking, molecular dynamics, immune-response simulation, and expression-oriented optimization. VC033_V6_BD_L03 construct was prioritized as the lead candidate and showed combined predicted population coverage of 99.78% worldwide and 99.96% in South Asia. Docking against human TLR3 structure 1ZIW yielded a ClusPro score of -1624.8. During a 100-ns molecular-dynamics simulation, the complex underwent early conformational reorganization followed by lower late-phase structural variation and persistent interchain hydrogen bonding. MM/GBSA analysis yielded a mean interaction-energy estimate of -20.52, 15.42 kcal/mol. C-ImmSim predicted coordinated humoral and cellular responses, while codon optimization generated a 1,539-bp expression sequence compatible with virtual cloning into pET-28a(+). These analyses prioritize VC033_V6_BD_L03 as a consensus-derived, pan-proteome NiV multi-epitope vaccine candidate for experimental evaluation of expression, immunogenicity, safety, and protective efficacy.

Bioprocess Modeling Made Easy With Hybrax

Libiseller-Egger, J., Gotsmy, M., +1 author, Zanghellini, J.

Abstract: Hybrid models that embed neural networks in ordinary differential equations are increasingly popular in bioprocess modeling, but they are challenging to set up. Much of the effort goes into the code around the model: wet-lab data must be imported and pre-processed, feeds and sampling must be accounted for in the balance equations, and a training loop with cross-validation must be set up. Researchers usually write this code from scratch for each project. In addition, no widely adopted format for bioprocess data exists, and datasets are mostly published in bespoke spreadsheets or not at all. As a result, models and datasets from the literature are difficult to reproduce, reuse, or compare.We present Hybrax, an open-source Python framework that addresses these problems. It combines a flexible format for bioprocess data with a customizable end-to-end training pipeline for mechanistic and hybrid models. Hybrax derives the transport terms in the process dynamics automatically and provides the utilities needed for training, so that researchers can focus on developing the model itself. It also facilitates model sharing and reproducibility: each training checkpoint contains the model parameters, code, configuration, and training data required to run the model or retrain it from scratch. We demonstrate Hybrax by re-implementing two models from the literature, one trained on real-world E. coli data and one on synthetic HEK293 data, with performance comparable to the original models. Hybrax is available at https://github.com/hybrax/hybrax.
