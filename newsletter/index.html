MoHuTS: Developability-Aware Antibody Humanization Using Multi-Objective Tree Search

Yoshimura, K., Fujii, S., +1 author, Terayama, K.

Abstract: Antibody humanization aims to reduce the immunogenicity risk associated with non-human antibodies while preserving antigen binding and other properties important for therapeutic development. Identifying suitable framework substitutions often requires repeated rounds of variant construction and experimental testing, making humanization costly. Recently, machine-learning methods, including language models trained on antibody sequences, have been proposed to identify human-like framework substitutions. Although prior humanization methods have considered antigen binding and biophysical properties, it remains challenging to balance humanness across both chains with developability-related properties such as thermal stability and aggregation propensity while retaining favorable parental properties. Here, we present Multi-objective Humanization Tree Search (MoHuTS), which combines an antibody language model with reward-guided tree search to explore combinations of framework substitutions efficiently. Starting from the parental heavy- and light-chain sequences, the language model proposes successive framework substitutions. A combined score for humanness, melting temperature, a structural proxy for binding retention, and aggregation propensity guides the search. Across five computational case studies, MoHuTS generated candidates that showed favorable humanness scores and developability-related properties with relatively few framework substitutions from the parental sequences. These results suggest that MoHuTS can help prioritize humanization candidates for experimental evaluation.

Target-specifc machine-learning scoring functions boost structure-based virtual screening for PfDHODH

Caba, K., Ballester, P.

Abstract: Plasmodium falciparum dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (PfDHODH) is a validated antimalarial target for which no inhibitor has yet been approved, and no target-specific machine learning scoring functions (ML SFs) have been reported for it. Here, we developed PfDHODH-specific ML SFs for structure-based virtual screening from a small dataset of 73 experimentally confirmed actives and 86 inactives, supplemented with property-matched (PM) and property-unmatched (PU) decoys. We compared five algorithms, several protein-ligand interaction featurisation schemes and classification and regression formulations under two evaluation designs: a Butina clustering split and a UMAP-based split in which each cluster of actives was held out in turn. The best model, a random forest regressor trained on PLEC fingerprints combined with Morgan fingerprints, was the strongest under both designs, reaching a normalised enrichment factor at 1% (NEF1%) of 0.617 on the Butina test set, compared with 0.323 for Smina, the best-performing generic SF, and an average NEF1% of 0.656 across the UMAP clusters (Smina: 0.362). Regression generally outperformed classification, and deep learning models were unstable with the limited training data. Adding PU decoys to the training set substantially improved enrichment for most held-out clusters, whereas the cluster most dissimilar to the training actives remained poorly predicted. These results show that target-specific ML SFs can be developed for a target with scarce bioactivity data, provided that algorithms, featurisation and evaluation design are tested comprehensively.

Deep Learning-Guided Design of Candidate Synthetic Cis-Regulatory Elements for a TCF7+ Progenitor-Exhausted-like CD8+ T-Cell State

Buhn, N., Lee, M., +1 author, Gill, S.

Abstract: Progenitor-exhausted CD8+ T cells (Tex-prog) are essential for sustaining T-cell-mediated immunity during chronic infection and cancer and therefore offer a promising immunotherapy target. Synthetic cis-regulatory elements (CREs) could enable selective transgene expression in Tex-prog cells, but whether they can distinguish this population from closely related CD8+ T-cell exhaustion states remains unclear. Here, we integrated publicly available paired single-cell ATAC-seq and single-cell RNA-seq datasets from mouse infection and tumor models and identified an ATAC-defined Tex-prog-like population (C1). We used the CREsted framework to fine-tune a pretrained Borzoi model to predict chromatin accessibility across C1 and ten comparator populations. We then used CREsted to design candidate synthetic CREs with predicted preferential accessibility in C1. The fine-tuned model achieved an average precision of 0.242 for identifying C1-selective regions on chromosomes held out from fine-tuning, approximately tenfold higher than the random-ranking baseline. Sequence optimization generated 196 1,000-bp designs with at least twofold higher predicted accessibility in C1 relative to the strongest competing population across eight DNA backgrounds. Motif enrichment and sequence-attribution analyses identified sequence patterns associated with AP-1, NFAT, POU, EGR, and TCF/LEF transcription factor families. Together, these findings identify candidate synthetic CREs with predicted preferential accessibility in a Tex-prog-like CD8+ T-cell population and provide a foundation for future experimental evaluation of their functional enhancer activity and cell-state specificity.

LAMPdecomposer: A generalisable framework for predicting and identifying loop-mediated isothermal amplification products using nanopore sequencing

dos Santos, C. A., McMahon, A., Robb, N. C.

Abstract: Loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) is widely used for rapid molecular diagnostics, yet its complex concatemeric products remain poorly characterised, limiting assay validation and contamination detection. Here, we introduce LAMPdecomposer, a computational framework that combines a generalisable model of LAMP product formation with nanopore read decomposition to resolve individual amplification products. We predict 48 product sequences arising through four molecular mechanisms and validate their occurrence across 420,797 reads from 11 primer sets. Nearly all predicted products were recovered, revealing conserved patterns of product abundance and preferential amplification pathways. The library explained 52.2% of reads within its defined length range, with limited additional coverage from substantially longer reads. LAMPdecomposer also identified products originating from other primer sets at single-read resolution, revealing cross-contamination in positive samples and negative controls. By linking predicted amplification mechanisms to experimentally observed products, LAMPdecomposer provides a framework for investigating LAMP amplification and improving molecular diagnostic quality control.

Extracting generator-specific information from irregular brain field potentials: a comparative study of feature-based and deep learning approaches.

Cosmin-Toader, N., Ramirez-Frias, A., +5 authors, Herreras, O.

Abstract: Brain field potentials (FPs) reflect the combined activity of multiple neural sources, challenging the accuracy of their temporal series and their spatial origin difficult to establish from waveform inspection alone. However, FP generators obtained by independent component analysis exhibit characteristic temporal patterns and unique spatial profiles that allow their identification, even during apparently irregular activity. Here, we compared feature-based machine learning (ML) and end-to-end deep learning (DL) approaches to assess how effectively this information can be extracted from short segments and whether learning directly from raw signals offers advantages over predefined signal descriptors. We analyzed irregular FP-generator activity from multiple hippocampal generators in a dataset pooled across experimenters, recording devices, and electrode types. Feature-based approaches used predefined spectral and nonlinear descriptors to construct multidimensional representations, which were classified using seven ML algorithms: bagged decision trees (BAG), light gradient boosting machine (LGBM), quadratic discriminant analysis (QDA), random forest (RF), support vector machines (SVM), extreme gradient boosting (XGB) and multilayer perceptron (MLP). In parallel, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and transformer architectures learned discriminative representations directly from raw signals. Performance was evaluated using animal-independent data partitions to assess generalization to previously unseen animals. Both approaches discriminated among FP generators from short segments of irregular activity. Raw-signal models generally achieved higher classification performance than feature-based approaches, although the latter also captured substantial generator-specific information. This suggests that discriminative information extends beyond the predefined descriptors and includes temporal patterns that can be exploited through direct signal learning. Classification across animals further supports the reproducibility of generator-related information despite variability in experimental conditions and recording procedures. These findings provide a scalable quantitative approach to identifying FP generators from irregular activity, opening new possibilities for investigating electrophysiological signals across physiological and pathological conditions.

RecurSieve: A Python Package to Detect Subtle Gene Expression Differences Across Single-Cell RNA-seq Comparisons Using Iterative Machine Learning

Mittal, A., Chin, G., +2 authors, Saunders, A. B., pavan, k.

Abstract: Most existing methods for performing differential expression testing using single-cell RNA-seq data evaluate genes independently and primarily detect shifts in mean RNA levels. These methods may thus miss RNAs that distinguish experimental groups jointly or through changes in the shape of genes' expression distributions. To address this limitation, we developed RecurSieve, an iterative ensemble learning method that combines shared expression classification with unsupervised clustering to recursively identify gene sets with group-based expression differences. Because experimental labels apply to heterogeneous populations of single cells within each comparative group, RecurSieve allows dynamic label reassignment during iterative training to detect subtle, non-linear expression patterns. In benchmarking experiments using simulated data with staged distributional expression differences, RecurSieve recovered more genes (2.3-3.9 of 10 staged genes) across groups as compared to standard methods (Wilcoxon, t-test, and logistic regression; 0.0-0.7 staged genes). When applied to a single nuclei RNA-seq analysis containing low and high-pathology stages of Alzheimer's disease (46 human donor brains), RecurSieve extended results from standard approaches by identifying genes including WWOX, DPP10, KCNIP4, HS6ST3, and CNTN5 which tend to exhibit stage-specific distributional expression differences accompanied by only minor changes in average expression. A subset of the genes exclusively detected in RecurSieve analysis had been previously associated with Alzheimer's disease, underscoring the potential importance resulting from the unique sensitivity of our method. These simulated and empirical single-cell RNA-seq results demonstrate how the RecurSieve framework complements standard methods for identifying genes with unique group-specific expression differences independent of changes in average expression.

Integrated single-cell and bulk transcriptomic analyses of lysine isonicotinylation-related gene expression in diabetic foot ulcer healing

Ye, Z., Wu, Y., +3 authors, Chen, Y.

Abstract: Diabetic foot ulcers have heterogeneous healing outcomes. We integrated public single-cell and bulk RNA-sequencing datasets to identify cell-type-specific lysine isonicotinylation (Kinic)-related transcriptional patterns and exploratory candidates associated with healing. The atlas comprised 56,568 cells across 14 cell types, with Kinic-related signature scores differing by cell type and healing group. Intersecting bulk differentially expressed genes, single-cell high-versus-low-score differentially expressed genes, and coexpression module genes prioritized LDHB, ISG15, and HAUS1 as exploratory candidates. Seven subtypes were identified among healing-enriched fibroblasts. ISG15-associated enrichment highlighted interferon and inflammatory responses. Simulated deletion of each candidate reduced mean similarity to a non-Healer reference state. Docking identified LDHB ZINC250K 0191530 as the lowest-scoring evaluated pair, while a single 100-ns simulation showed displacement of the ligand from its initial pose. These analyses prioritize LDHB, ISG15, and HAUS1 as exploratory candidates and characterize their cellular context in diabetic foot ulcer healing. Their expression patterns within healing-enriched fibroblasts provide clues to the cellular and molecular differences associated with healing outcomes.

Input Construction Accounts for Most PoseBusters Clash Failures on RNA-Ligand Complexes

Soultana, M., Odesanya, O. I., +1 author, Richman, G.

Abstract: PoseBusters, a widely used test of the physical validity of ligand poses, was calibrated on protein-ligand crystal structures, and whether its checks transfer to RNA has not been measured. Applied without filtering the input to 2,970 deposited RNA-ligand poses, the receptor-clash check failed 527 of 2,931 (18.0%), and all but 7 of the failures were in covalently bonded nucleotides or in entries that contain protein, were solved by cryo-electron microscopy or were resolved at worse than 2.0 [A], so most failures are associated with which structures and ligand records entered the input set. On a high-resolution set constructed after these failures were attributed, the check failed 3 of 149 (2.01%; 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.00-4.58%) against 0.32% for the protein benchmark, a difference this set cannot resolve, and the PoseBusters calibration rule selected a threshold of 0.735 against the shipped 0.75. We give a seven-step dataset construction protocol and a tool, rnaprep, that implements its first four steps. On 139 redocking targets, with accuracy measured in the crystal frame without superposition, requiring a valid pose cost NucleoDock 25.2 points (69.06% to 43.88% within 2 [A]) and AutoDock Vina none, and cut the lead of NucleoDock from 38.9 points (95% CI 26.5-50.0) to 13.7 points, a lead not clearly distinguishable from zero (95% CI 1.3-27.5; cluster permutation P = 0.053). For these two programs in one redocking regime, the result supports reporting validity alongside RMSD in RNA docking benchmarks.

Distance-stratified benchmarking of variant-effect predictors on β-lactamase deep mutational scanning data

Dhakal, A., Acharya, S., Chand, G.

Abstract: Mutations far from the active site of antibiotic-resistance enzymes can change resistance, but it is unclear what information predicts their effects. We asked how the performance of a protein language model (ESM2), a structure-based stability predictor (ThermoMPNN) and simple structural descriptors changes with distance from the catalytic residues, using deep mutational scanning data for the {beta}-lactamases TEM-1 (four assays) and VIM-2 (one assay). We measured Spearman correlation and AUROC within distance bins, with bootstrap intervals over positions. In TEM-1, ESM2 accuracy fell with distance (Stiffler assay: {rho} = 0.72 below 8 [A] and 0.42 beyond 20 [A]), while the stability score rose ({rho} = 0.41 to 0.70). Adding stability to a model of ESM2, solvent accessibility and neighbour count improved cross-validated {rho} by 0.02 below 8 [A] and 0.10 beyond 20 [A], and the combined model did not decline with distance. The pattern held across binning schemes, in a continuous-distance model, and when any one of five contiguous distal stretches was removed. Two TEM-1 assays are nearly duplicates, and the Deng assay was too noisy to be informative. VIM-2 has only seven positions beyond 20 [A] and showed no detectable trend within 20 [A], so it neither supports nor contradicts the TEM-1 pattern. Nine distal TEM-1 positions remained more sensitive than the models predicted. In TEM-1, predicted stability was more informative than the language model far from the catalytic site, and the language model was stronger near it. Distance-stratified reporting could add to variant-effect benchmarks. The study is exploratory and uses one structure and one phenotype per assay.

Germ-cell restricted antigens are presented on HLA-I in premalignant colorectal lesions

Gupta, S., Karumo, S., +15 authors, Snyder, M.

Abstract: Tumour-restricted antigens displayed by HLA class I proteins are potential targets for T cell recognition, but whether they are present before a lesion becomes invasive is unknown. Here we applied high-sensitivity HLA-I immunopeptidomics to 105 individual colorectal lesions and normal mucosa from 24 donors with hereditary polyposis. We identify peptides from germ-cell-restricted genes presented in premalignant lesions, confirmed against synthetic standards, and absent from reference immunopeptidomes of benign colon. These source genes are hypomethylated in polyps relative to normal mucosa. The same pattern is seen in colorectal tumours, and demethylating agents induce their expression in cell lines. Peptides were presented from transcripts at abundances well below conventional thresholds for target nomination, demonstrating the relevance of direct immunopeptidome readout with mass spectrometry. Several source genes were silent in medullary thymic epithelium, and dextramer-sorted T cells against one such peptide were dominated by a small number of clonotypes. An antigenic landscape therefore emerges in colorectal tissue before malignant transformation.

Stromal mitochondrial reprogramming and transfer reveal a chemotherapy-associated tumor state in ovarian cancer

Baruwal, R., Matusiak, P., +13 authors, Coffman, L.

Abstract: Mitochondrial transfer from stromal cells promotes ovarian cancer progression, yet the functional state of donor mitochondria remains poorly understood. Here we define a stress-adapted mitochondrial state in carcinoma-associated mesenchymal stem cells (CA-MSCs), characterized by increased respiratory and glycolytic activity despite increased mitochondrial proton leak and inefficient coupling of respiration to ATP production, oxidative stress, and elevated mitochondrial DNA variant burden. We identify WT1 as a regulator of mitochondrial reprogramming and PDK4 as a metabolic adaptation critical for CA-MSC growth. We further show that CA-MSCs preferentially transfer highly oxidized mitochondria to ovarian cancer cells. In a freshly-dissociated primary high grade serous ovarian cancer patient tumor, using mitochondrial variant tracking, we identify a transcriptionally distinct tumor population sharing mitochondrial variant patterns with stroma, in line with stromal-tumor mitochondrial transfer. This transcriptional signature is enriched in residual tumors following chemotherapy across four independent patient cohorts. Together, these findings establish stromal mitochondrial reprogramming and the preferential transfer of oxidatively-stressed mitochondria as important features of ovarian cancer heterogeneity and persistence following chemotherapy.
