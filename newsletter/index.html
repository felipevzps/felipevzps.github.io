Substrate rigidity suppresses the NRF2 antioxidant program in breast cancer cells

Khan, S. U., Javed, M. D., +2 authors, Stupack, D. G.

Abstract: Matrix stiffness rises as breast tumors progress, but whether rigidity raises or lowers activity of the KEAP1-NRF2 antioxidant pathway is contested: work in breast cancer cells places the antioxidant response on soft matrix, while work in liver cancer places response on stiff. We used publicly available transcriptome data to ask the question across laboratories rather than within one system. Of eleven series identified, four dataset arms passed quality criteria fixed before any biological analysis. Across five contrasts spanning three cell lines and two matrix formats, one program responded in the same direction every time: the NRF2 antioxidant battery was lower on the more rigid substrate in all five. In contrast, SLC7A11, the cystine transporter supporting glutathione synthesis, decreased in all five, by up to 6.5-fold. YAP and TEAD targets did not track rigidity, rising on hydrogels and glass but falling on cell-derived matrix. NFE2L2 and its regulators were unchanged, while targets fell in proportion to their dependence on the antioxidant response element, pointing to control after transcription. An unsupervised partition made without reference to any pathway recovered the same program and survived removal of proliferation genes. In 1,097 TCGA and 1,980 METABRIC breast tumors, the matrix proxy was unrelated to the full battery until immune and hypoxic composition were accounted for, after which the same negative direction appeared in both; and a tumor-intrinsic score excluding macrophage-expressed members showed it more directly. Single-cell and spatial tests produced the predicted direction but could not resolve it, due to limited sample size and because the genes carrying the effect are largely undetectable at spot depth. Reduced antioxidant capacity on rigid substrate predicts greater sensitivity to agents killing through lipid peroxidation, a prediction we set out for experimental test.

Cell-type label errors inflate neighborhood effects and can reverse niche claims in spatial transcriptomics

Elgart, V., Loscalzo, J.

Abstract: Niche analyses ask how a cell's neighbors shape its expression beyond its type. The type comes from a label, and labels are imperfect. Here we show that label errors inflate neighborhood effects and can reverse niche claims. A mislabeled cell still expresses its true type, and because cells of a type sit near one another, the neighborhood is credited with what the label misses. Standard practice, which calls labels on the modeled counts and selects cells by them, hid, exaggerated, or reversed this inflation. Labeling one half of each cell's counts and modeling the other revealed it in nearly every population of six datasets. On semi-synthetic colon data, a labeler that over-called one fibroblast subtype reversed claims of the niche method Niche-DE; new draws of the counts and errors spread at random over subtypes almost never did. Our software, nichepeek, reports the direction of this bias in a user's data.

gPlace: fast, memory-bounded phylogenetic placement with results identical to pplacer

Chaumeil, P.-A., Parks, D. H.

Abstract: Phylogenetic placement methods such as pplacer attach query sequences to a fixed reference tree, thereby establishing their evolutionary relationships to the reference sequences. These relationships can then be used to infer taxonomic classifications or likely metabolic functions. gPlace is a drop-in replacement for pplacer that produces identical results while reducing memory requirements by >3-fold and computation time by >30-fold under typical usage. These improvements are achieved by storing less data per node and streamlining the calculations used to position each query sequence. gPlace can also run within a user-specified memory limit by processing only a portion of the reference tree at a time. Consequently, it can efficiently place sequences on large reference trees that would be impractical for pplacer to process. We demonstrate the importance of these improvements by evaluating gPlace as a replacement for pplacer in GTDB-Tk and CheckM, two tools widely used in genome-resolved metagenomic studies. gPlace is released under the GNU General Public License v3.0 and is available at https://github.com/cmc-aau/gPlace.

RIPPLE: learning nucleotide relations in an RNA foundation model

Hao, Y., Guo, X., +5 authors, Li, F.

Abstract: RNA function depends on interactions between nucleotides that can be widely separated in sequence, yet capturing these relationships remains challenging for RNA language models. Existing approaches primarily learn nucleotide relationships implicitly from individual nucleotide reconstruction or rely on external structural information to model nucleotide interactions. Here we introduce RIPPLE, an RNA foundation model that learns to identify and represent sparse, predictively useful nucleotide relationships directly from sequence. Pretrained on 32.4 million RNA sequences, RIPPLE uses pairwise predictive utility to guide relational learning, transferring training-time supervision into a sequence-only model without requiring structural inputs at inference. RIPPLE improves overall performance across five downstream tasks spanning RNA family classification, ribosome loading prediction, secondary-structure prediction, three-dimensional contact prediction, and splice-site recognition. Mechanistic analyses further show that utility-guided learning improves the recovery of informative interactions and enhances the transferability of learned RNA representations.

Natural and synthetic polyamines as organic counterions for multi-crossover DNA nanostructures

Madhanagopal, B. R., Gallagher, G., +4 authors, Chandrasekaran, A. R.

Abstract: Assembly of DNA nanostructures requires positive counterions to shield the negative charge repulsion between the strands in the assembly. While several counterions have been explored for DNA nanostructure assembly, polyamines are particularly attractive candidates because they could support intracellular assembly of DNA nanostructures. However, a systematic analysis of natural and synthetic polyamines for DNA nanostructure assembly is currently lacking. In this study, we demonstrate the assembly of three model DNA nanostructures of the same size but with different number and topology of crossovers using three naturally occurring and two synthetic polyamines. The model DNA nanostructures are a double crossover motif with two antiparallel crossovers (DX-A), a double crossover motif with two parallel crossovers (DX-P), and a paranemic crossover with six parallel crossovers (PX). We successfully assembled all nanostructures, and relative assembly yields depended on the concentration and the type of polyamine and the type of DNA nanostructure. The structures exhibited comparable conformations but displayed slightly lower thermal stabilities compared to their Mg2+ assembled counterparts. We also demonstrate hierarchical assembly of DX tiles into periodic 2D lattices using the polyamines. Our results shed light on how polyamines differentially impact DNA nanostructures with distinct topological features and different crossover densities. Polyamines can serve as organic biocompatible counterions for the assembly of simple DNA motifs as well as larger arrays with implications for intracellular assembly and biological applications of DNA nanostructures.

Engineering acidophilic non-conventional yeasts as low-pH cell factories for organic acid production

Swidah, R., Visinoni, F., +4 authors, Delneri, D.

Abstract: Acidophilic non-conventional yeasts (NCYs) thrive at low pH and are promising hosts for organic acid production, but their use is limited by the availability of genetic engineering tools. Here, we optimised a chemical transformation protocol for efficient DNA delivery into six NCYs, including M. bulderi, S. bombicola, S. batistae, K. aerobia, K. naganishii and Y. lipolytica. We also developed Pan/ARS-containing expression vectors compatible with YeastFab for one-pot assembly of standardised promoters, open reading frames and terminators. As a proof of concept, we engineered M. bulderi, S. bombicola and S. batistae to produce lactic acid. We combined codon-optimised ldh genes from B. megaterium and L. helveticus with regulatory elements from S. cerevisiae or M. bulderi and introduced the resulting constructs into the hosts on episomal vectors. Lactic acid titres varied substantially across hosts and constructs. The highest titre, approximately 6.4 g/L, was achieved in S. batistae expressing the B. megaterium ldh gene under the control of S. cerevisiae regulatory elements. For targeted genome engineering, we developed a CREEPY-derived CRISPR-Cas9 system that supports multiplex gRNA assembly and achieved over 94% gene deletion efficiency in S. bombicola. In Starmerella species, Aro10p contributes to the conversion of pyruvate to acetaldehyde, potentially diverting carbon away from lactic acid production. Deleting ARO10 in S. bombicola increased lactic acid production through both endogenous and exogenous ldh pathways, with titres reaching up to 6 g/L. S. bombicola was unable to grow on lactate as the sole carbon source, whereas S. batistae utilised both D-lactate and L-lactate. This suggests that S. bombicola may offer a production advantage by limiting lactate reassimilation. Together, these genetic tools and metabolic engineering strategies expand the engineering capabilities of acidophilic NCYs and lay the groundwork for developing these yeasts as low-pH cell factories for the sustainable production of organic acids and other bio-based chemicals.

Engineering dynamic interkingdom communication between synthetic organelles and mammalian systems

Kulkarni, S. H., Rennells, T. S., +7 authors, Contag, C. H.

Abstract: Engineering intracellular microbes to function as synthetic organelles, or quasiorganelles, opens new avenues for controlling mammalian cell fates and function through programmable and quantitative interkingdom communication. Here, we develop a bioluminescent reporter platform to quantify the magnitude, kinetics, persistence, and spatial distribution of transcriptional regulation in mammalian host cells directed by quasiorganelles. Using an attenuated, flavin-auxotrophic Listeria monocytogenes chassis (LINQ1) engineered to deliver synthetic transcription factors, we demonstrate approximately 100-fold activation of mammalian reporter genes, with distinct kinetics and dynamics for the tetracycline transactivator and Gal4 systems. In transgenic reporter mice, LINQ1 induced approximately 25-fold reporter activation across multiple organs, with activity persisting beyond three weeks in some animals. In mice bearing triple-negative breast tumors, LINQ1 activated intratumoral transcription and, incidentally, transiently suppressed tumor growth, although responses diminished with repeated administration. These findings establish a quantitative foundation for engineering interkingdom communication in vivo to advance a new paradigm in which programmable microbial systems function as synthetic organelles to direct mammalian cell fates and function for therapeutic benefit.

Uncovering mechanisms of cell-free protein synthesis using tailored coarse-grained models

Wagner, C. S., Laohakunakorn, N., Weisse, A. Y.

Abstract: Cell-free protein synthesis has revolutionised molecular biology research, and it continues to be a fundamental methodology in synthetic biology today. Yet, many of its mechanistic principles remain poorly understood, limiting our ability to predict reaction behaviour. In this work, we introduce a pipeline for uncovering these principles that combines computational models with experimental validation in a design-build-test-learn cycle. First, we develop a coarse-grained ordinary differential equation model describing key processes in the reaction. Next, we adapt the model to predict system behaviour under competing mechanistic hypotheses and compare model predictions to our experimental data from batch reactions using Escherichia coli extract. Our data and analysis indicate that gene length determines the rate-limiting step in transcription; it identifies transcription/translation-independent depletion of chemical energy as the primary cause for reaction termination; and finally it highlights translation as the most variable process between batches of cellular extract. Together, these results point to strategies for improving the yield and reproducibility of cell-free protein synthesis, and provide a framework for rational, model-guided design of cell-free systems.

Alpha-Interferons and CD40: complementary pathways revealed by mGEDO in distinct transcripto-methylomic profiles of patients with Sjögren's disease

Bezier, C., Rolland, J., +9 authors, Devauchelle-Pensec, V.

Abstract: Objectives: Sjogren's disease (SjD) exhibits substantial clinical and molecular heterogeneity, challenging patient stratification and targeted therapies. We aimed to develop multi-omic signatures of interferon-alpha (IFN) and CD40 pathways using mGEDO, and characterize their activity across SjD subgroups and healthy controls. Methods: We developed mGEDO to integrate whole-blood transcriptomic and DNA methylation data from PRECISESADS using BloodGen3 modules, generating continuous two-dimensional patient representations. IFN and CD40 signatures were derived through automated gene selection and quantified using mGEDO Transition Scores. Associations with clinical and biological phenotypes were assessed in PRECISESADS and independently investigated in ASSESS. Results: Among 692 PRECISESADS participants, mGEDO revealed a continuous SjD - control molecular landscape with substantial heterogeneity beyond discrete clustering. IFN and CD40 signatures exhibited distinct, partially overlapping patterns. IFN was predominantly associated with biological disease activity, whereas CD40 provided complementary information, particularly in IFN-negative patients. Combined IFN/CD40 stratification identified biologically progressive subgroups, with double-positive patients exhibiting the greatest immune dysregulation. In 371 ASSESS patients, CD40 and CD40LG expression associations with biological phenotypes were confirmed. Both genes were associated with circulating immune-cell composition. Conclusions: IFN and CD40 signatures reflect complementary, partially uncoupled innate and adaptive immune programs, potentially improving molecular stratification and precision therapeutic targeting in SjD. Whole-blood CD40LG expression may reflect immune-cell redistribution and serve as a potential marker of immune-cell migration.

Parent-of-origin classification for diploid genomes using Pooch

Kim, J., Pickett, B. D., +6 authors, Phillippy, A. M.

Abstract: Advances in long-read sequencing have enabled the generation of near-complete human genomes at scale, but trio sequencing has typically been required to determine the parent of origin for each haplotype. Parent-of-origin information is important for studying inheritance patterns and imprinting-associated genetic disorders. Here we demonstrate that parent of origin can be reliably determined for all chromosomes of a Hi-C-phased diploid assembly using differentially methylated CpGs alone. Using 123 near-complete, trio-phased assemblies from the Human Pangenome Reference Consortium, we systematically cataloged differential methylation genome-wide, identifying 880 imprinted differentially methylated regions (iDMRs), including 150 with strong and 730 with moderate differential methylation, including two iDMRs on chrX. From these data we trained an elastic net logistic regression model (Pooch) that predicts parent of origin for each haplotype pair with a mean test accuracy of 0.997 and generalizes across sequencing technologies, cell types, and abnormal karyotypes. Notably, 91.7% of informative CpG features identified by the model were located outside of known iDMRs, suggesting that parent-of-origin differential methylation extends beyond previously established imprinted regions. We further identified parent-of-origin signals on chrX by correcting for the effects of X chromosome inactivation, revealing imprinted differentially methylated regions within an intron of DHRSX and in the promoter of SRPK3, where methylation depended on both parent of origin and X chromosome inactivation status. Our results show that it is now possible to achieve haplotype-resolved diploid genome assemblies routinely and determine maternal and paternal haplotypes for all chromosomes without the need for trios.

Discovery of novel drug combinations for oral squamous cell carcinoma through integrated analysis of single-cell RNA-sequencing datasets

Grandits, A. M., Wagner, R., +7 authors, Wieser, R.

Abstract: Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) is associated with high morbidity and mortality, yet treatment options remain limited and resistance driven by intratumoral heterogeneity (ITH) is frequent. To characterize ITH and identify strategies to overcome it, we integrated twelve public single-cell RNA-sequencing datasets into an unprecedentedly large dataset and combined it with spatial transcriptomics. A novel, pathway-based clustering approach revealed seven highly recurrent and distinct tumor cell clusters, each defined by divergent cell-cell interactions, distinct positions along the cell trajectory tree, and spatially defined localization within tumors. To overcome ITH therapeutically, we sought to identify drug targets that were overexpressed in the vast majority of these highly diverse tumor cells, which was reasonably achieved only through pairwise target combinations. Pharmacological inhibition of LGALS1 plus CTSC, and LGALS1 plus TYMP, suppressed growth of OSCC spheroids, with further enhancement by cisplatin. Additional anti-tumor activity is expected in vivo through known effects of these inhibitors on the tumor microenvironment. Our work characterizes ITH in OSCC and reveals strategies to overcome it by broadly targeting malignant and microenvironmental drivers of OSCC, providing a basis for the rational design of novel combination therapies.

A cholesterol addiction creates an Achilles' heel in APC-truncated colorectal cancer

Boukich, I., Bartolome, R. A., +11 authors, Casal, J. I.

Abstract: Cholesterol auxotrophy, dependence on exogenous cholesterol, is an emerging metabolic vulnerability of cancer that remains poorly understood. Here, we show that APC-truncated CRC cell lines displayed cholesterol auxotrophy, whereas CTNNB1-mutant cells were cholesterol-independent. The endolysosomal Niemann Pick type C2 (NPC2) protein is a key transporter of cholesterol from lysosomes to cellular membranes. NPC2 knockdown (KD) in auxotrophic APC-mutant cells depleted plasma-membrane cholesterol, disorganized lipid-raft scaffolding and signaling, and inhibited stemness, xenograft growth and metastasis. In contrast, NPC2 KD CTNNB1-mutant cells retained tumorigenic and metastatic capacity. Genome-wide transcriptional responses to NPC2 loss were nearly uncorrelated between APC and CTNNB1 mutant cell lines revealing genotype-dependent adaptation and opposite cell signaling pathways alterations. NPC2 KD auxotrophs showed abortive compensatory cholesterol synthesis with no alternative egress route, whereas CTNNB1-mutant cells restored lysosomal cholesterol egress by inducing NPC1 and sustaining Wnt/stemness identity, AP-1/YAP/TAZ signaling and a pro-invasive secretome. Lipidomics confirmed the signaling divergences: sphingomyelin collapse, membrane-fluidity remodeling exclusive to the auxotrophs, and opposite-direction diacylglycerol signaling in CTNNB1 mutants. Notably, APC-truncated cells phenocopied selected Niemann Pick type C hallmarks, linking lysosomal cholesterol export to tumor fitness. Thus, NPC2 constitutes a synthetic-lethal vulnerability specific to cholesterol-auxotrophic APC-mutant CRCs, providing a mechanistic rationale for targeting the secreted and recaptured NPC2 pool in this molecular subset of patients.

Three-Dimensional Network Topology of Irradiated Cancer Spheroids Revealed by Optical Coherence Tomography

Arndt, M. D., Hansler, R., +4 authors, Spadea, M. F.

Abstract: Radiation therapy is central to cancer treatment, yet label-free quantification of tumour radiation response in three dimensions (3D) is lacking. Cell-graph topology relates to radiation response in monolayers; here we extend this to 3D tumour spheroids using optical coherence tomography. SAS spheroids were irradiated with 0-12 Gy and imaged at days~8 and 11. High-backscattering subregions from watershed segmentation served as nodes of a proximity graph, yielding eleven network metrics for N=197 spheroids. Ten of eleven metrics differed between doses (Kruskal-Wallis, all p = 0.21). Most dose effects, including density, persisted across broad algorithm parameter ranges; {sigma} and degree assortativity were exceptions, and {sigma} also depended on imaging day. Random geometric graphs with matched node counts did not reproduce the dose-dependent changes in density, clustering, path length or {sigma}. Results are exploratory (single cell line and experiment).

Application of a high molecular weight topical hyaluronan formulation blocks keratinocyte tumor initiation downstream of UVB-induced oncogenic mutations.

Tolg, C., Liu, V. M., +6 authors, Turley, E. A.

Abstract: The polysaccharide hyaluronan in its high molecular weight form (HMW-HA) has been implicated as providing tumor resistance. Since HMW-HA is depleted with age and UVB exposure, we directly assessed the effect of replacing HMW-HA in UVB-exposed skin of Hr-/-:Ptch1LacZ/+ mice, a model of susceptibility to keratinocyte tumors. We developed a fragmentation-resistant, topical HMW-HA formulation (HMW-HA-phosphatidylethanolamine, HAPE) that penetrates skin to form coats around keratinocytes, which are not depleted by UVB. HAPE prevents detectable UVB-induced tumors and promotes epidermal homeostasis even though whole exome sequencing shows that the UVB-induced single base pair mutation burden is similar to control. Mutagenesis of most keratinocyte driver oncogenes is not affected, but mutation of Trp53, which is required for sustained keratinocyte tumorigenesis, is rarely detected in HAPE-treated skin. Immunofluorescence and spatial transcriptomics (10X genomics) show that HAPE blunts expression of oncogenic driver pathways (e.g. sonic hedgehog) throughout the epidermis but selectively reduces TRP53 protein expression and signaling in hair follicles. Spatial transcriptomics identifies a hair follicle KRT15+:TRP53+ stem cell subset that is lost with HAPE treatment. Since KRT15+ hair follicle progenitors are cancer-initiating, we assessed the effect of HMW-HA on the tumorigenesis of human keratinocytes bearing gain-of-function TRP53 mutations. HMW-HA promotes apoptosis of KRT15+ subsets, prevents disorganized and invasive growth of xenografts and supports polarized keratinocyte architecture. These results identify a novel microenvironmental tumor suppression mechanism that selectively targets mutant cancer-initiating cells and maintains skin homeostasis and polarity.

Bacteroides ovatus administration enhanced α-mPD-1 immunotherapy in a mouse colorectal cancer model

Zhang, H., Zhang, S., +12 authors, Zhu, J.

Abstract: The gut microbiota modulates responses to immune checkpoint blockade, yet the metabolic remodeling associated with microbiome-based combination therapies remains poorly understood, limiting insights into the host microbiome interactions underlying therapeutic responses. Here, we investigated whether Bacteroides ovatus (BO) enhances the antitumor efficacy of programmed cell death protein 1 (PD 1) blockade in an MC38 syngeneic colorectal cancer mouse model. BO combined with anti-mouse PD 1 produced the strongest antitumor response, reducing average tumor burden by 53% compared with PBS controls. BO-specific qPCR detected higher BO-associated signals in tumors from BO-exposed groups, while exploratory flow cytometry evaluated treatment-associated immune changes. Paired 16S rRNA profiling revealed Bacteroides enrichment in BO-treated groups and treatment-associated shifts in microbial community composition. Tumor metabolomics identified alterations in glycan turnover, activated-donor metabolism, oxidized lipids, and fatty-acid trafficking associated with combination treatment. Serum metabolomics revealed treatment-associated metabolic profiles, while fecal metabolomics further characterized intestinal metabolic changes. Integrated cross-omics analysis identified notable associations linking microbial taxa with metabolites across biological compartments. Together, these findings suggest that BO enhances the antitumor activity of PD 1 blockade and is associated with microbial and metabolic remodeling, providing a foundation for investigating host microbiome interactions underlying combination immunotherapy.

The tape of life has run once, and what that means for biological foundation models.

Frederick A Matsen, Yun S Song

Abstract: Stephen Jay Gould famously asked in 1989 what would happen if we rewound the tape of life and let evolution run again. He argued that we would not recover the same living world. That thought experiment exposes a central challenge for biological foundation models (large, general-purpose pretrained models). These models are trained on data from the one run of the tape that we have. The data alone cannot tell which patterns reflect what selection consistently favors and which reflect chance events along the path evolution happened to take. Sequence-density objectives, such as masked-language modeling and next-token prediction, fit the observed sequences without modeling how they arose, so they conflate these two kinds of pattern. A model can thus fit this run of the tape well without recovering the mechanisms that generated it. The choice of training data compounds the problem: functional elements make up only a small fraction of many genomes, while mutational processes, repetitive sequences, and other nonfunctional signal can dominate. We argue that scaling alone is unlikely to overcome these limitations. Instead, models should use biology-native objectives and training data designed to reveal biological process. Such objectives can predict measured biological quantities, explicitly model the evolutionary process that generated the data, or combine both. Models built this way already outperform much larger sequence-density models on several tasks, and their outputs can be interpreted and tested experimentally. The tools, data, and infrastructure now exist to build biology-native models at scale and answer long-standing questions in evolutionary genetics.
