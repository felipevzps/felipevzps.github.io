From Screening to Differential Diagnosis: Benchmarking 3D Facial Representations Across Craniofacial Conditions

Chen, T., Ahsan, M. U., +2 authors, Wang, K.

Abstract: Rare genetic disorders often present with highly heterogeneous physical features: some have distinctive craniofacial characteristics, whereas others produce subtler or more variable facial differences, making phenotype-based diagnosis challenging. Three-dimensional (3D) facial scanning is a noninvasive approach that captures facial shape and surface geometry, providing quantitative information on craniofacial morphology beyond conventional 2D photographs. Here, we investigated how well 3D facial representations support clinical tasks ranging from broad screening to fine-grained differential diagnosis without landmark registration, dense correspondence, or template fitting. We benchmarked four 3D models, including PointNet, PointNet++, DGCNN, and a geometric baseline, using 26,990 facial scans from 18,785 subjects across four prediction tasks. Across models, performance declined as greater diagnostic specificity was required. Broad affected-versus-unaffected screening achieved high performance (accuracy 0.893; AUC 0.956), although sensitivity analyses revealed substantial influences of cohort composition and demographic differences. Fine-grained syndrome diagnosis remained more challenging, with limited top-1 accuracy despite meaningful class-wise discrimination. Combining repeated scans improved patient-level diagnosis beyond differences in patient weighting. Prototype inference further improved diagnostic prioritization, placing the correct syndrome among the top five candidates for about 65% of held-out patients across 49 diagnoses. Patient-to-patient retrieval identified phenotypically similar confirmed cases, while useful syndrome prototypes could be constructed from relatively few reference patients. Anatomical region-occlusion analysis revealed syndrome-dependent patterns of facial importance broadly consistent with clinically recognized craniofacial features, although overlapping facial phenotypes contributed to diagnostic confusion. Overall, these findings support the potential of 3D facial representations for diagnostic candidate prioritization and retrieval, while emphasizing the influence of cohort composition on screening performance and the challenges of distinguishing phenotypically similar genetic syndromes.

Scalable recorders of cellular calcium dynamics and molecular interactions across time

Yan, Y., Linghu, C.

Abstract: Cellular processes are mediated by dynamic biochemical events in their native context, including changes in intracellular ion concentrations and transient interactions between molecules. Studying these processes across large cell populations requires recording their dynamics across time in single cells, simultaneously and at scale. Here, we introduce split-1POK, a split protein tape monomer whose two halves reconstitute when the domains fused to them interact. The reconstituted monomer is incorporated into the protein tape as it elongates across time in live cells, enabling scalable, spatiotemporally resolved endpoint readout under conventional light microscopy. Split-1POK provides a programmable scaffold for new recorders in the CytoSense family, our previously introduced framework (ref. 1) in which the same cytosol-localized monomer senses cellular activity and writes it along protein tape recorders. Unlike transcription-based activity recorders, which encode the signal through new gene expression, CytoSense recorders act on monomers post-synthesis, so the activity signal is sensed and written directly in the cytosol without passing through transcription- or translation-associated cascades. Fusing calmodulin and its binding peptide to split-1POK, we built TCaMP (Tape monomer-CalModulin-Peptide), a genetically encoded calcium recorder (GECR) that measures calcium dynamics on a single, analog intensity scale along a continuous time axis, providing quantitative comparison of calcium levels over time within the same cell. In cultured human cells, TCaMP records transient calcium dynamics from transmembrane entry, receptor activation, and intracellular store release, detecting stimulations as brief as ten minutes. With hour-scale kinetics over days, TCaMP complements fluorescent calcium indicators by trading sub-second temporal resolution for scalable, long-term recording. Fusing conditional dimerization pairs to split-1POK yields recorders of molecular interaction histories in response to external stimuli. Split-1POK thus brings scalable, spatiotemporally resolved recording to intracellular calcium dynamics and molecular interactions across time.

An expanded, phenotypically distinct tumor cell subset with low HPV mRNA increases recurrence risk

Raghav, L., Patterson, M. R., +10 authors, Basu, D.

Abstract: Human papillomavirus-related (HPV+) cancers harbor a subset of tumor cells expressing certain senescence-related transcripts and reduced levels of HPV mRNAs. We aimed to define this HPV-low population's biologic traits and clinical significance via integrated analysis of recurrence-prone human HPV+ oropharyngeal cancers (OPCs) and patient-derived xenografts (PDXs) of variable therapy sensitivity. Single-cell mRNA sequencing of PDXs and human tumors detected gene expression profiles in HPV-low cells indicating lack of senescence or terminal differentiation and intact tumorigenic capacity. HPV-low cells in cisplatin-refractory PDXs and particular human OPCs differentially upregulated multiple pathways linked to aggressive tumor biology, including RAS signaling, IL-6 signaling, and EMT. Predicted loss of cell-cell interactions among HPV-low cells reflected reduced epithelial identity, further supporting their more aggressive biology. Accordingly, the size of the pre-treatment HPV-low fraction had strong, independent association with recurrence risk. Genetic subclones within OPCs varied widely in content of HPV-low cells, which were enriched in particular lineages. Our findings uncover novel pro-tumor functions of HPV-low cells and support a central role for them in therapy resistance as well as potential utility as a clinical biomarker. Further elucidating mechanisms of cooperativity and plasticity between HPV-low and high states holds promise to reveal targets in treatment-refractory HPV+ malignancies.

Discovery of molecular glues for modulating MHC presentation of cancer neoantigens

Shen, W., Zhang, Z., +5 authors, Shokat, K. M.

Abstract: Antigen presentation by MHC class I molecules is essential for cancer immunosurveillance, but many cancer-specific antigens fail to form complexes with Class I MHC and are poorly presented on the cell surface. In this study, we present a chemical strategy to overcome this limitation by identifying peptide MHC glues (PMGs) that promote the formation of peptide-MHC complexes with otherwise unpresentable cancer antigens. Crystal structures of these complexes show that our compounds stabilize antigen-MHC binding by making simultaneous interactions with the MHC protein and the antigen peptides. These findings reveal a new approach for chemically rewiring Class I MHC antigen presentation and accessing rare cancer antigens for therapeutic targeting.

Label-free detection of glioma radioresistance using exosomal SERS spectra and machine learning.

Qiong Wu, Sufang Qiu, +2 authors, Youliang Weng

Abstract: Glioma ranks among the most intractable malignancies. Given the intricate anatomy of the brain, complete surgical resection is seldom achievable, making radiotherapy a necessary adjunct. Nevertheless, radioresistance, which is closely linked to recurrence, still represents a critical clinical barrier. Effective tumor biomarkers and novel detection methods are urgently required to predict treatment resistance and monitor therapeutic response. Here, we employed surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS) coupled with proteomics to profile, for the first time, the characteristic spectral patterns of exosomes secreted by our established radioresistant glioma cells. We further uncovered specific shifts in protein expression during the development of radioresistance, including glycolysis-related proteins (ALDOA and GAPDH) and ribosomal proteins (RPS5 and RPLP0). These proteins are correlated with glioma prognosis. Moreover, bioinformatic analysis revealed that expression levels of all four genes positively correlate with tumor malignancy grade. Furthermore, we established a machine learning-based diagnostic model, Principal component analysis and convolutional neural network (PCA-CNN), for the accurate identification of exosomes derived from radioresistant glioma cells. These findings validate exosomes as a strong candidate biomarker for predicting radioresistance. This approach enables rapid and reliable assessment of radiotherapy resistance in glioma, paving the way for personalized and precise clinical management.
