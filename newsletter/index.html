Comparative functional genomics reveals distributed SERPINE1 like characteristics among Drosophila serpins

Thayer, M.

Abstract: Serpins (serine protease inhibitors) regulate proteolytic activity through a suicide-substrate inhibitory mechanism, whereby the serpin covalently traps and inactivates their target protease. Serpins are broadly conserved across eukaryotes, but the evolutionary and functional relationships between human SERPINE1, and Drosophila Serpins in incompletely defined. To identify candidate Drosophila functional analogs of SERPINE1/PAI-1, a multi-omics framework was applied to thoroughly compare the serpins across species. The multi-level analysis integrated orthology prediction, protein sequence and structural characterization, phylogenetic reconstruction, bulk tissue transcriptomics, single-cell transcriptomics, and aging-associated expression analysis. Candidate serpins (Spn42Da, Spn42Db, Spn42Dc, Spn42Dd, Spn28B, and Spn28F) were evaluated using DIOPT, AlphaFold/ChimeraX structural comparisons, reactive center loop analyses, phylogenetic distances, GTEx, Human Protein Atlas, FlyAtlas2, Fly Cell Atlas, and Aging Fly Cell Atlas datasets. Comparative analyses demonstrated full-length sequence and reactive center loop (RCL) divergence despite preservation of the canonical serpin fold. Tissue, cell-type, and age-associated expression analysis revealed distinct candidate-specific patterns. Spn42Dd comparisons uncovered similarities to human SERPINE1 in sequence/structural and broad expression but did not represent the strongest overall cross-species expression correlations. Spn42Da and Spn42Db received the highest cross species integrated support. Quality check of the ranking confirmed that Spn42Da and Spn42Db were stable top candidates across orthology, sequence alignment, and expression domains. The multi-domain analysis was unable to identify a single definitive Drosophila serpin as the closest counterpart to human SERPINE1, indicating that cross-species similarity is distributed across multiple candidate serpins. This study therefore provides a quantitative method for prioritizing potential evolutionary and functional analogs across highly divergent species.

A High-Efficiency Geranylation Platform Enabled by Rational Tuning of a Single-Residue-Coded Chemo- and Regioselective Switch in Aromatic Prenyltransferases

Qiu, R., Zhang, H., +9 authors, Wang, J.-b.

Abstract: Geranylation is an efficient post-modification strategy for enhancing or introducing new properties to molecules; however, achieving both high efficiency and precision remains challenging. Aromatic prenyltransferases (PTs) provide a mild and sustainable approach, yet their practical application is often limited by the trade-off between broad substrate scope and low selectivity, and successfully balancing these properties is rare. Here, using the pharmaceutically relevant cannabigerol framework as a model, we identify a single-residue switch in a prenyltransferase from Aspergillus terreus (AtaPT) that governs both chemo- and regioselectivity. Rational tuning of this switch preserves broad substrate scope while enhancing selectivity, enabling the selective formation of each of the three possible mono-geranylated products in the model reaction. Structural analysis reveals that this residue modulates the selectivity and activity through coupled steric and electrostatic effects. Notably, transplantation of this site into three homologous PTs similarly reprogrammed selectivity, establishing a conserved and generalizable control mechanism. Leveraging these insights, we achieved selective geranylation across 36 aromatic substrates, affording 41 products. Integration with an efficient geranyl donor regeneration system enabled preparative-scale synthesis, reaching titers up to 324 mg/L. This work defines a unifying mechanism for selectivity control in PTs and provides a high-efficiency platform for geranylation.

Paired-unit activation steering for context-aware protein design

Zhou, Y., Qian, X., Yoon, B.-J.

Abstract: Activation steering in protein language models enables controllable sequence generation without changing the model weights. However, existing methods typically construct steering vectors from global differences between property-positive and property-negative protein populations, overlooking how property-improving directions vary across different protein sequence backgrounds. To address this, we propose textit{Paired-Unit local steering of embeddings (PULSE)}, which matches positive- and negative-property proteins by maximizing their representation cosine similarity and constructs steering vectors by aggregating similarity-weighted, unit-normalized differences between matched pairs. Across three protein optimization objectives--solubility, immunogenicity, and toxicity--PULSE consistently outperforms global population-centroid steering in both final predicted property scores and optimization success rates. Further analysis reveals that conventional static steering-vector diagnostics cannot adequately explain optimization performance. To characterize steering reliability during iterative sequence editing, we introduce textit{Local Directional Support (LDS)}, which measures how well successive representation changes align with property-improving directions from similar protein backgrounds. We show that LDS captures differences in trajectory reliability and enables predictor-free candidate prioritization, enriching successful protein designs without requiring property predictions during candidate selection. Together, these findings demonstrate the importance of context-aware steering and trajectory-level evaluation for controllable protein sequence optimization.

RawMod: Modification-Agnostic Detection of DNA Base Modifications from Nanopore Signal Pileups

Srinivasan, B., Nazar, S., +5 authors, Firtina, C.

Abstract: Summary: Identifying epigenetically modified bases (e.g., methylation) is crucial for many important applications such as discovering mechanisms of cancer development, cell differentiation, and bacterial defense. Nanopore sequencing generates electrical signals that can differentiate canonical bases from modified ones. To detect base modifications from signals, machine learning (ML) methods and statistical approaches are commonly used. These methods can either detect a specific modification type or require an additional unmodified copy of the same DNA as a control group. However, no released model detects modification types beyond those it was trained for, which makes it difficult to detect rare modification types without an unmodified control. Our goal is to accurately identify the presence of modified sites regardless of their modification type, which we define as modification-agnostic detection. To this end, we propose RawMod, a transformer-based modification-agnostic and control-free method for detecting epigenetically modified genomic positions. The key idea of RawMod is to compare the raw signals of many reads at each site with the unmodified reference sequence, using a small model that learns many modification types as a single modified class. Based on our extensive evaluations on the detection of various modification types (5mC, 5hmC, 4mC, 6mA, and 5hmU) using various genomes, RawMod achieves the highest mean AUROC across modifications compared to control-based methods and control-free modification models. A single RawMod model with 0.23M parameters detects five modification types with a mean AUROC of 0.964, against 0.799 for the best released model. When a modification type is withheld from training, RawMod still detects it (AUROC 0.91--0.98), provided another modification of the same base was seen in training. We use RawMod to map 5hmU without a control across a 7.7 Mb contig of the Amphidinium carterae genome, consistent with two published trends. Further, we show that the modification-aware embeddings from RawMod can be used to differentiate read origin (e.g., cell types, tumor/normal origin, and native/PCR-amplified DNA). We discuss how RawMod can enable further work on label-free classification of cell types using these modification-aware embeddings. Availability and Implementation: RawMod is available at https://github.com/STORMgroup/RawMod and at https://huggingface.co/STORMResearch/RawMod. We also provide the scripts to fully reproduce our results on our GitHub page and release our datasets on SquiDBase.

OligoFragger: spectrum graph-guided analysis of modified RNA oligonucleotides by mass spectrometry

Shen, J., Polasky, D. A., +4 authors, Nesvizhskii, A. I.

Abstract: RNA modifications are widespread modulators of RNA function, but comprehensive identification remains technically challenging. This is especially true for densely modified RNAs such as tRNAs, where multiple modified residues and many possible chemistries occur within short sequence contexts. Tandem mass spectrometry provides direct chemical and positional evidence for modified oligonucleotides, but current database-search strategies struggle when dozens of candidate modifications must be considered simultaneously. Here, we present OligoFragger, a spectrum graph-guided computational tool that uses fragment evidence to constrain modified-oligonucleotide searches before full database scoring, enabling rapid and robust oligonucleotide identification. Using controlled RNA samples and tRNA datasets from E. coli and human cells, we demonstrate that OligoFragger increases identifications under stringent FDR control while recovering known modification patterns and supporting label-free quantification. This strategy enables sensitive, FDR-controlled identification and label-free quantification of modified RNA oligonucleotides in complex epitranscriptomic search spaces.

PAMP: Continuous-to-Discrete Mutation Projection for Enzyme Engineering

wang, h., yu, z., ren, y., yuan, r.

Abstract: Efficient enzyme engineering requires translating quantitative turnover predictions into specific mutations that im- prove catalytic activity while reducing experimental search. Here we introduce Proximal Adversarial Mutation Projection (PAMP), a gradient-guided framework that maps a differentiable turnover objective directly to single-amino-acid substitutions by combining multi-point gradient averaging with distance-penalized projection in protein embedding space. Using a substrate-conditioned Resid- ual Predictor trained on CataPro-derived data, PAMP achieved a mean first-round predicted gain of 0.1795 log2 units across 2,754 enzyme substrate pairs, with positive changes in 84.64% of cases; among the top five ranked substitutions, 99.49% of records contained at least one positive candidate. Five sequential rounds increased the mean cumulative predicted gain to 0.5631 log2 units, and re-scoring with an independent, non-differentiable predictor left the ordering of the methods intact while attenuating the absolute gain. PAMP provides a computationally efficient strategy for prioritizing experimentally testable enzyme mutations under small candidate budgets.

SubCellSeg: A generalist model for subcellular structure segmentation in 3D fluorescence microscopy

Mishra, S., Samudre, A., +6 authors, Viana, M. P.

Abstract: Three-dimensional (3D) segmentation of subcellular structures from fluorescence microscopy images is the foundation of many analyses in quantitative cell biology. The prevailing approach addresses 3D segmentation one structure at a time, with a separate model trained for each structure type. By developing SubCellSeg, we demonstrate that a single model, trained jointly across a diverse range of structures, substantially and consistently outperforms structure-specific specialist models. SubCellSeg is a 3D segmentation model trained on expert-curated fluorescence images and their computationally generated reference masks, of 13 morphologically distinct subcellular structures in human induced pluripotent stem (hiPS) cell lines from the Allen Cell Collection, spanning compact puncta, planar junctions, reticular networks, and tubular structures. Without any structure-specific input, SubCellSeg achieves a mean Dice score of 0.909 across the 13 structures and outperforms each of the 13 specialist models on the structure it was trained for (specialist mean Dice 0.773). SubCellSeg also achieves zero-shot generalization, segmenting subcellular structures with morphologies not represented in the training data. This generalization extends to images acquired on different microscopes and with different imaging configurations, to cells under drug perturbation, and to a morphologically distinct cell type, hiPS cell-derived cardiomyocytes. Where zero-shot results are insufficient for a specific application, SubCellSeg can be fine-tuned: starting from its pretrained weights, 10% of the labeled images (16 images) gave better results than training a new model from scratch on all of them. SubCellSeg is distributed through the napari plugin allencell-segmenter-ml[1] with pretrained checkpoints, so it can be run on new data without any training; the training and evaluation code is available at https://github.com/AllenCell/SubCellSeg.

Fast Pangenome Matching Statistics in Compressed Space with Move Structures

Brown, N. K., Sanaullah, A., +2 authors, Langmead, B.

Abstract: Matching statistics and supermaximal exact matches (SMEMs) are key inputs for sequence homology search and classification. Methods such as MONI, PHONI, and ropebwt3 compute these statistics using compressed pangenome indexes, but are substantially slower than methods that compute approximations of matching statistics, such as pseudo-matching lengths (PMLs). PMLs can be computed quickly in a single pass, but SMEM computation can require overlapping forward or backward passes over the query. We present ms2, a suite of algorithms that use "move structures" to compute matching statistics and SMEMs efficiently in compressed space. Our four algorithms compose move structures for the LF, {Psi}, {varphi}, and {varphi}-1 permutations with additional longest common prefix (LCP) array summaries and samples. The algorithms offer different tradeoffs among speed, index size, and support for reporting SMEM offsets. They inherit the move structure's fast, constant-time operations and achieve high throughput by leveraging the move structure's locality of reference. Thanks to recent advances, move structures can be constructed in optimal time and compressed space, including the LCP information needed for "match recovery" during SMEM computation. On 100 haplotypes of human chromosome 22, ms2's fastest algorithm computes matching statistics 35 to 48 times as fast as PHONI's online approach, while using an index of similar size. On the 472-haplotype HPRC2 pangenome and human reads, three ms2 modes achieve speedups of at least 22, 19, and 15 times over ropebwt3, whose index is about 15% the size of ms2's fastest mode. When additionally reporting SMEMs and their offsets, ms2 achieves speedups of 25 to 34 times over ropebwt3. Together, these advances enable online computation of matching statistics and SMEMs at a throughput that supports real-time processing of approximately 21 PromethION nanopore flow cells on a single thread. The ms2 software is open source and available at https://github.com/langmead-lab/ms2. It builds on the open-source TeraTools suite (https://github.com/ucfcbb/TeraTools) and Orbit library (https://github.com/drnatebrown/orbit). Experimental scripts and other details are described in: https://github.com/langmead-lab/ms2-experiments.

SpaAxFormer: Fine-Grained Token Modeling of Spatial Transcriptomics with Axial Transformers

Lee, C., Xie, X.

Abstract: Spatial transcriptomics combines gene-expression measurements with spatial relationships between cells. We investigate how spatial context can be incorporated into gene-resolved representations while preserving explicit input-gene correspondence. SpaAxFormer is a spatially constrained factorized formulation that maintains contextual cell-gene states throughout the encoder. It first contextualizes genes within each cell and then exchanges corresponding gene states across spatial neighbors, while a separate CLS representation provides a cell-level summary. Contextual gene states support gene-expression reconstruction, while CLS-conditioned reconstruction and contrastive learning provide complementary supervision for the cell-level representation. We evaluate SpaAxFormer on gene-expression imputation and spatial-domain identification across multiple spatial-transcriptomics technologies. Compared with the included baselines, imputation results were most favorable on dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) datasets and more mixed on additional technologies, while post-hoc spatial refinement consistently improved mean ARI on the Visium spatial-domain datasets where it was applied. In a single-slice component-addition analysis, mean clustering ARI increased as CLS-conditioned reconstruction and contrastive supervision were added. Together, these results show that the same gene-resolved formulation can support complementary gene-level prediction and cell-level spatial representation while preserving explicit input-gene correspondence.

Preferential recruitment of PML isoform II by incoming HPV16 genome transport vesicles facilitates early stage of infection

Kushwaha, A., Bienkowska-Haba, M., +5 authors, Zwolinska, K.

Abstract: The interplay between promyelocytic leukemia nuclear bodies (PML NBs) and viral infections is complex and multifaceted, with viruses often engaging in a delicate balance of exploitation and evasion of host immune responses. It is well-established that incoming human papillomavirus (HPV) virions associate with PML NBs; however, the mechanism by which HPV targets PML NBs to facilitate early transcription and replication remains elusive. We have shown that HPV genomes are delivered to the nucleus inside membrane-bound vesicles during mitosis and that PML proteins are recruited to HPV-harboring nuclear vesicles prior to egress. Herein, we demonstrate that the PML isoform II (PMLII), but not other PML isoforms, is selectively attracted to incoming HPV still residing in transport vesicles. Using individual PML NB isoform and HPV16 minor protein, L2, over-expression experiments in HeLa cells, we conclude that PML II binds to the lipids of the HPV harbouring membrane-bound vesicles rather than L2. This is in line with previous observations that PML II is the only isoform binding to lipid droplets in the nucleus. We observed that the association of PML NBs with the HPV16 viral genome was transient and lost over time. We also found that nuclear Phospholipase C {beta}4 and {gamma}1 colocalize with PML II-L1 complexes in infected cells. Our data support a model in which PML II contributes to the establishment of early HPV16 infection by organizing host lipid regulatory factors at incoming viral transport vesicles.

Longevitomics as the Science of the Longevitome: Towards a Unified Systems Science of Healthy Longevity.

Dimitrios P Vlachakis, Konstantinos Evangelou, +2 authors, George P Chrousos

Abstract: The twenty-first century has transformed longevity research through genomics, epigenomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, microbiome science, digital phenotyping and artificial intelligence. Yet healthy longevity remains difficult to explain because its determinants are commonly studied as separate molecular layers, mechanisms or biomarkers. Here, we introduce Longevitomics as an integrative systems framework for understanding, modeling and predicting healthy longevity and define the Longevitome inspired by the Latin expression vita longa (long life) as the dynamic architecture of biological, environmental and behavioral determinants that collectively shape psychosomatic resilience across the life course. We position this proposal in relation to the Hallmarks of Aging, geroscience, biological-age biomarkers, multi-omics and systems medicine. Longevitomics is not intended to replace these established frameworks or to constitute another molecular "ome." Rather, it provides a systems-level framework in which mechanistic hallmarks, multi-omic layers, physiological phenotypes, exposures and adaptive responses can be integrated longitudinally. We further consider how network analysis, causal inference, machine learning and digital-twin approaches may enable reconstruction of individual Longevitome trajectories and support a transition toward predictive and personalized longevity medicine.

Portable amplification-free diagnostics of head and neck squamous cell carcinoma using SERS biosensor with machine learning-assisted classification.

Hoang KD, Atta S, +11 authors, Vo-Dinh T.

Abstract: Head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) is the sixth most common cancer worldwide, with approximately 890,000 new cases annually, and is associated with disproportionately poor outcomes in low-resource settings due to limited access to early diagnostics and trained personnel. Bridging this gap requires transformative technologies that combine high sensitivity, minimal sample processing, and point-of-care access. Here, we present a portable platform for the direct detection of the mRNA biomarker keratin 14 (KRT14) in HNSCC based on a sandwich hybridization assay coupled with surface-enhanced Raman scattering (SERS) using highly efficient probes: core-gap-shell spiky nanorattles (SpNRs). We systematically investigate the role of adenine spacer length on probe conformation on both magnetic beads (MBs) and SpNRs, as well as its effects on hybridization efficiency and SERS signal enhancement, identifying optimal configurations that enable a picomolar limit of detection without enzymatic amplification. Machine learning models based on the intensity of the characteristic Raman reporter band at 1358 cm⁻1 were used for quantification and classification, discriminating HNSCC from non-cancerous cell-line RNA. In a pilot set of clinical biopsies (two HNSCC patients, three healthy controls), we employed a support vector regression (SVR) classifier, which distinguished cancer from healthy tissue consistent with qRT-PCR, with CatBoost regression used for quantification. Overall, this work presents a nano-plasmonic sensing platform combined with machine learning analysis for nucleic acid detection, with potential applications in point-of-care cancer diagnostics.

Deciphering the role of microplastics-related molecular signatures in cervical cancer progression: Transcriptomic analysis and diagnostic model development.

V B Navya, Asna Fathima, Ravindra Kumar

Abstract: Environmental microplastic exposure has been increasingly implicated in cervical carcinogenesis, yet the underlying biological mechanisms of microplastic-associated disease progression remain largely unexplored. Therefore, this study combined transcriptomic analysis, chemical-gene interaction data, machine learning, and molecular docking to investigate the molecular impact of microplastics exposure associated with disease progression. Transcriptomic data from GEO database was retrieved and differential expression analysis was performed, yielding a consensus core of 330 common genes across disease conditions. Microplastic candidates including Polystyrene, Polyethylene, Polypropylene, Nylon 612, Polymethyl Methacrylate, and Polyvinyl Chloride were selected based on established in vitro and in vivo evidence of carcinogenic potential in cervical tissues. Cross-referencing with chemical-gene interaction data identified 15 candidate genes linked to microplastic exposure and cervical cancer. The combinatorial feature selection across all 15-gene combinations (n = 32,570) using Support Vector Machine and Multinomial Logistic Regression algorithms yielded an optimized 5-gene diagnostic signature (FN1, HTR4, KIF14, PTP4A3, SLC7A2) for multiclass classification of Normal, CIN, and Cervical Cancer samples. The optimized SVM model achieved multiclass AUC values of 0.937, 0.889, and 0.925 for the Normal, cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN), and Cancer classes, respectively, with further validation in independent GSE27678 validation cohort, demonstrating its robustness and generalizability. Molecular docking using AutoDock Vina demonstrated that polystyrene exhibited the highest binding affinities across all five target proteins, with peak scores of -6.681 kcal/mol, with KIF14. Collectively, these findings highlight potential molecular interactions between microplastics, particularly polystyrene, and key genes involved in cervical cancer progression. Thus, the proposed five-gene provides a promising framework for multiclass diagnosis and future studies of microplastics-associated carcinogenesis.
