Artificial Intelligence in Breast Cancer Diagnosis, Treatment, and Perioperative Management: A Systematic Review of Current Applications and Future Perspectives.

Mostafa AF, Mady R, +12 authors, Ahmed IS.

Abstract: Breast cancer is the most frequently diagnosed malignancy in women globally, accounting for approximately 2.30 million new cases and 666,103 deaths in 2022. Artificial intelligence technologies including machine learning, deep learning, radiomics, and natural language processing have been widely applied across the breast cancer care continuum. However, no systematic review restricted to primary studies with patient-level or image-level data has synthesized evidence spanning all clinical domains simultaneously.To evaluate the performance, methodological quality, and clinical relevance of artificial intelligence primary studies across screening and diagnostic imaging, digital pathology, radiomics, perioperative management, prognostic prediction, and natural language processing in breast cancer.PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, and the Cochrane Library were searched from January 2015 to May 2026. Eligibility was restricted to original primary research reporting patient-level or image-level data; secondary syntheses were explicitly excluded. Risk of bias was assessed using QUADAS-2 and PROBAST. Reporting followed PRISMA 2020 guidelines.Of 5,275 identified records, 17 primary studies met eligibility criteria. In screening mammography (seven studies; n = 7,540 to 144,231 examinations), deep learning achieved area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) values of 0.876 to 0.939; a randomised trial in 80,033 women demonstrated non-inferiority to double reading with a 44.3% reduction in radiologist workload. In digital pathology (five studies; 270 to 44,732 whole-slide images), deep learning achieved AUC 0.985 to 0.994, exceeding unassisted pathologist performance in lymph node metastasis detection. Magnetic resonance imaging radiomics predicted pathological complete response in HER2-positive breast cancer with AUC 0.80. No breast cancer-specific perioperative primary study met the eligibility criteria. Deep survival networks achieved concordance index 0.626 versus 0.612 for Cox regression in breast cancer cohorts. Natural language processing identified metastatic recurrence in breast cancer clinical records with sensitivity 83% and specificity 73%. Risk of bias was low to moderate for imaging and pathology studies and high for small sample radiomics studies. Density-stratified performance was reported by only two imaging studies, and receptor status prediction from haematoxylin and eosin staining had not been validated as a safe alternative to immunohistochemistry.Artificial intelligence demonstrated clinically meaningful performance across the five domains with eligible primary studies. Breast cancer-specific perioperative and intensive care primary studies were absent, representing the largest evidence gap. External validation, demographic diversity, and prospective clinical integration evidence remained the most consistently identified limitations. Prediction of molecular receptor status without immunohistochemistry requires rigorous prospective safety validation before it can complement standard testing.

Integrating Machine Learning to Identify Prognostic Subtypes and the Cisplatin Resistance Gene RRM2 in Lung Adenocarcinoma.

Huang H, Tao T, +5 authors, Li B.

Abstract: Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is a highly invasive malignancy associated with poor prognosis. However, reliable molecular subtypes and prognostic signatures for LUAD, particularly their associations with tumor immunity and treatment response, remain poorly characterized. This study aimed to identify distinct molecular subtypes and establish a robust prognostic signature for LUAD, while exploring potential therapeutic targets associated with cisplatin resistance. In this study, we integrated ten clustering algorithms with 101 machine learning methods to identify two distinct molecular subtypes of LUAD and established a robust consensus machine learning-derived signature (CMLS). Patients belonging to the Cluster A subtype exhibited a more favorable prognosis and an enhanced immune response. Furthermore, a CMLS model constructed from 19 genes exhibited strong predictive power. Individuals with high CMLS scores displayed reduced CD4 + T-cell infiltration, poorer survival outcomes, and diminished responses to immunotherapy, whereas those with the low CMLS scores showed improved clinical outcomes, better immunotherapy responsiveness, and features of an immunologically "hot" tumor phenotype. Among the identified genes, ribonucleotide reductase subunit M2 (RRM2) emerged as a core regulator associated with LUAD prognosis. RRM2 was markedly upregulated in lung cancer tissues and the LUAD cell lines and was associated with the cisplatin (DDP) resistance. Suppression of RRM2 expression significantly enhanced the cisplatin sensitivity of A549/DDP cells. In summary, our findings establish a molecular classifier that stratifies LUAD patients into distinct risk categories and highlight RRM2 as a promising biomarker and therapeutic target associated with cisplatin resistance.

Neferine sensitizes oral squamous cell carcinoma to cisplatin by disrupting the CCDC43-PNKP interaction.

Lin Y, Zheng X, +5 authors, Zhu X.

Abstract: Oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) poses a significant global health challenge due to its high recurrence rate and resistance to cisplatin. While DNA repair pathways contribute to chemoresistance, the role of scaffold proteins in stabilizing repair enzymes remains largely unexplored. This study aimed to elucidate the function of CCDC43 in cisplatin sensitivity and to identify a natural product targeting the CCDC43-PNKP axis. Bioinformatic analyses were conducted using bulk and single-cell transcriptomic and proteomic datasets. Molecular assays investigated the CCDC43-PNKP interaction and the impact of neferine on this axis. Xenograft models assessed the efficacy of combined neferine and cisplatin treatment. Machine learning identified CCDC43 as a key prognostic marker and driver of cisplatin resistance. CCDC43 was upregulated in OSCC epithelial cells and associated with poor survival. CCDC43 bound and stabilized PNKP by inhibiting ubiquitin-dependent degradation, thereby enhancing DNA repair and reducing cisplatin sensitivity. Through artificial intelligence-based virtual screening and experimental validation, neferine was found to disrupt the CCDC43-PNKP interaction, promoted PNKP degradation, increased cisplatin-induced DNA damage, and suppressed OSCC growth without acute toxicity. Collectively, the CCDC43-PNKP axis represents a critical therapeutic target in OSCC. Neferine is a promising ethnopharmacological agent capable of improving cisplatin sensitivity by targeting this pathway.

Glial Dysfunction and Memory Impairments in a Model of Pediatric Obstructive Sleep Apnea.

Williamson MR, Botas J, +11 authors, Chandrakantan A.

Abstract: Pediatric obstructive sleep apnea (POSA) is a common childhood disease that often causes aberrant brain development and cognitive deficits. The pathophysiological underpinnings of cognitive impairments in POSA remain unclear. Here, we examined cellular and molecular aspects of pathology in a mouse model of POSA that features learning and memory deficits. We performed single-nucleus RNA-sequencing (snRNA-seq) of the hippocampus to examine gene expression changes in an unbiased and cell type-specific manner. This dataset revealed a striking perturbation of transcriptomes across all brain cell types, particularly within glia and neural stem cells. We validated reduced expression of several differentially expressed genes at protein level: QDPR and SOX8 in oligodendrocytes, LRRK2 and NDUFS4 in neural stem cells, TFE3 in microglia, and GLUT1 in astrocytes. Comparison of oligodendrocyte gene expression changes with proteomic datasets suggested impairments in myelination, which we confirmed in vivo. Furthermore, cellular level analyses demonstrated aberrant morphology of oligodendrocytes, astrocytes, and microglia in the hippocampus, and diminished numbers of neural stem cells in the subgranular zone. Our study identifies cellular and molecular glial cell dysfunction in POSA, validates gene targets for further study, and provides an snRNA-seq dataset to facilitate further data-driven hypothesis generation.

Biosynthetic Pathways and Regulation of Antibacterial Compounds From Endophytic Fungi: From Chemistry to Genomics and Biotechnology.

Eshboev F, Salihu AS, +6 authors, Tsim KWK.

Abstract: Endophytic fungi are non-pathogenic symbionts that represent a rich source of structurally diverse bioactive secondary metabolites. However, genome-mining studies reveal that most of their biosynthetic gene clusters (BGCs) remain transcriptionally silent under standard laboratory conditions, leaving much of their chemical diversity unexplored. This review examines antibacterial natural products from endophytic fungi by integrating three complementary aspects: metabolite chemistry, biosynthetic pathways, and strategies for activating cryptic BGCs. Endophytic fungi produce polyketides, non-ribosomal peptides, terpenoids, phenylpropanoid-derived compounds, and hybrid metabolites with antibacterial activities mediated through inhibition of ribosomes, cell wall and membrane biosynthesis, and bacterial resistance mechanisms. Their genomes encode diverse PKS, NRPS, TPS, and hybrid biosynthetic systems, many of which remain cryptic. Advances in chemical elicitation, co-cultivation, epigenetic modulation, CRISPR-based engineering, genome mining, and machine learning are accelerating the activation of silent clusters and the assignment of BGCs to their metabolites. Several fungal metabolites exhibit potent activity against multidrug-resistant pathogens, including MRSA, and some enhance the efficacy of existing antibiotics. This review highlights current progress, identifies remaining challenges in metabolite production and BGC annotation, and outlines future directions for translating endophytic fungi into a sustainable platform for next-generation antibacterial drug discovery.

Toward a digital urinary biopsy for early risk stratification in diabetic kidney disease: Anchoring shed cells, vesicles, and cell-free DNA to spatial kidney cell states.

Wu J, Sheng P, +1 author, Chen K.

Abstract: Diabetic kidney disease (DKD) is the leading cause of end-stage kidney disease worldwide. However, albuminuria and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), which define and stage DKD, report established injury and do not resolve the cellular heterogeneity underlying variable progression. Urine, the only biofluid to traverse every nephron segment, carries shed cells, extracellular vesicles (EVs) and cell-free DNA (cfDNA), each retaining information about its cell of origin.This review develops the concept of a digital urinary biopsy, in which the three compartments are read together against spatial single-cell kidney atlases to infer non-invasively which cell states are active and whether niche-associated programs are engaged, an approach we term atlas-anchored molecular profiling. For each compartment, we tabulate study population, sample size, processing, assay, performance and validation status. Because shed-cell RNA, selectively sorted vesicle cargo and cfDNA methylation differ in origin, measurement scale and bias, each requires its own reference matrix, sampling function and background terms. All three carry renal information in feasibility studies, yet none is a validated clinical assay in DKD. Moreover, a cfDNA methylation reference at the required resolution is lacking, and no named computational method has been tested on urine.We therefore present the framework as a structured research hypothesis and define the required validation, from analytical and clinical validity to prospective demonstration of utility, with non-albuminuric DKD, which albuminuria-based screening misses, as the index use case. It offers a testable route to cell-resolved, non-invasive staging of DKD rather than a demonstrated capability.

tWhEEL: a transcript-aware workflow for quantifying CRISPR knockout feasibility using transcriptome assembly

Nakamae, K., Bono, H.

Abstract: Gene redundancy can complicate loss-of-function experiments (knockout), and its extent varies among organisms and biological functions. Relating variation in gene redundancy to the availability of genome-editing targets can help researchers select tractable biological functions and experimental organisms. Here, we present the transcript-aware Whole-genomic Evaluation for Editing Locus (tWhEEL), a pipeline that infers redundancy in silico from transcript information and cross-references it with specific guide RNA designs. tWhEEL integrates CRISPR-Cas9 guide design with Gene Ontology (GO) and pathway annotations to compute a knockout feasibility index (KFI) for individual genes and functional categories. Interactive HTML reports link GO-associated KFI and pathway-associated KFI summaries to the contributing groups, genes, and guide candidates with links to QuickGO or WikiPathways. By linking sequence-level guide candidates to functional-level targetability, tWhEEL can support the selection of biological functions and organisms for reverse-genetic experiments. Applications to model and nonmodel datasets revealed distinct targetability profiles, including high full targetability in yeast and query-protein-set dependence in zebrafish. A zebrafish Fanconi anemia case study recovered 14 of 19 published target-site designs. By connecting sequence-level guide candidates with function-level experimental planning, tWhEEL provides a framework for comparing reverse genetic tractability and prioritizing biological functions and organisms for further study.

Transcript quantification using context-aware MAPQ modeling

Hicks, S. C., Parthiban, S.

Abstract: Long-read RNA-sequencing enables accurate transcript-level quantification, but prior static filtering of MAPping Quality (MAPQ) can ignore transcriptome-intrinsic ambiguity, and discarding reads on this basis distorts abundance estimates. We present CALMeR (Context-Aware Long-read MAPQ-based Rescuer), which conditions MAPQ filtering on transcript properties rather than a fixed cutoff. CALMeR fits a zero-one inflated beta regression on various transcript and read-level features to model the expected MAPQ distribution for each alignment. Reads whose MAPQ fits this context, such as low-MAPQ reads from multi-isoform genes and paralog families are retained; reads with anomalously low MAPQ are removed as likely artifacts. Retained reads can be summed per transcript or passed to any downstream quantifier. Compared with a MAPQ [≥] 1 filter and expectation-maximization-based transcript quantification methods, CALMeR recovers more spike-in transcripts and better matches known spike-in concentrations. CALMeR is available at https://github.com/sparthib/CALMeR.

Bioactivity foundation model for hit identification and hit-to-lead optimization from sequence data

Koh, H. Y., Koh, H. J. W., +8 authors, Webb, G. I.

Abstract: Learning from large-scale bioactivity archives is complicated by heterogeneous assay readouts and differences in data reliability. Here we show that a model that predicts probability distributions over binding affinities from sequence data opens model training to vast and diverse troves of heterogeneous bioactivity data. We develop PSICHIC+, a bioactivity foundation model trained on approximately 82 million protein-ligand records using a distributional objective that preserves quantitative resolution while incorporating variation in data reliability. Given a protein sequence and ligand SMILES, PSICHIC+ predicts binding affinity in milliseconds. In zero-shot virtual-screening benchmarks without target-specific training data, it improves enrichment over regression- and classification-based approaches and performs competitively with leading structure-based methods. In lab-in-the-loop campaigns, distribution-guided acquisition improves recovery of high-activity compounds under fixed screening budgets. Combining PSICHIC+ with Boltz-2 improves hit-to-lead affinity prediction over Boltz-2 alone, with mean correlation approaching that of FEP+ on the evaluated benchmarks, without requiring high-resolution protein structures. These results support distributional learning as a scalable approach to using heterogeneous bioactivity data for hit identification and hit-to-lead optimization.

EpiRank: partner-agnostic surface-patch retrieval for epitope prediction of protein binding interfaces

Lin, B., Lu, D.

Abstract: Structure-based interface predictors are normally reported as a single pooled ROC AUC over a residue test set. For a protein designer the operative question is different and much harder: given one target chain, will the top-ranked surface patch be a real binding site? We built EpiRank, a partner-agnostic epitope predictor, on HumanPPI 29,246 predicted human dimers, 12,296 target chains, 4.18 M residues using only single-chain geometry and physicochemistry (170 features, no partner, no sequence search, no structural alphabet), and evaluated it as a retrieval problem with homology- and partner-graph-controlled splits, on the 3,529 chains that survive an explicit partner-contamination filter. The model places a real epitope in its top-ranked patch 64 % of the time. That number is far less informative than it looks. Compact patches placed at random hit a real epitope 41 % of the time under the same rule, so the model's lift is 1.57 x, not 64 %; 54 % of all predicted patches land on nothing; and when the same chains are scored by models trained on a 2,000-chain and an 11,669-chain superset, a substantial fraction change answer while the aggregate barely moves. We document four specific ways this task invites overstatement partner-graph percolation across splits, single-linkage homology percolation at the conventional 30 % identity threshold, a count-only hit rule that rewards spatial smoothing rather than signal, and hotspot precision conditioned on success, which ranks a random baseline above the trained model. Each was hit during development and each inverted a conclusion we had already drawn. We argue that interface-prediction papers should report lift over a geometry-matched random baseline, patch precision, and per-target stability alongside AUC, and we release the evaluation harness that computes them.

Structure-resolved fold annotation of the medicinal-leech salivary-EV cargo: a Ly-6/uPAR three-finger scaffold and a structurally dark fraction

Cen, H., Zhao, F., +3 authors, Zhao, F.

Abstract: Many proteins that medicinal leeches package into salivary extracellular vesicles (EVs) carry no interpretable sequence annotation, so the least-characterised part of a pharmacologically important secretome lacks even a fold assignment. A companion Astral-DIA study of Hirudinaria manillensis nominated 17 ultra-enriched candidate EV cargo proteins. Here we ask whether structure prediction and structural search can assign folds where sequence methods fail, and which candidates remain unresolved under prespecified criteria. With an offline pipeline (ESMFold, TM-align, Foldseek against AFDB-SwissProt and PDB100, UniRef90 search, protein-to-genome alignment), the candidates separate into intracellular/membrane machinery and a secreted, cysteine-dense fraction of three proteins with no Pfam domain. The predicted structure of the leech Protein-sleepless homolog g21166 supports its assignment to the Ly-6/uPAR three-finger fold (TM 0.58 vs CD59; best significant PDB match Lynx1); no function is inferred. g436 and g20791 returned no match meeting the criterion (alnTM [≥] 0.5, E [≤] 0.05) in either database, g436 even after homolog-informed modelling. Gene structure and splice junctions support a conserved locus for the two-Trypsin-segment model g8114 (catalytic unit not established). A Boltz-2 thrombin pilot did not support target engagement, and a pre-registered nicotinic test was terminated as undecidable. The results yield a structure-informed shortlist for experimental follow-up.

Single-cell foundation models transfer across deep evolutionary distances

Trnovec, L., Katoh-Kurasawa, M., +2 authors, Zupan, B.

Abstract: Single-cell foundation models are trained mostly on human and other vertebrate cells, yet are proposed as general-purpose models. Whether they transfer far beyond their training species is unknown. We evaluated zero-shot embeddings from Geneformer, scGPT and UCE against PCA and scVI in developmental atlases of Dictyostelium discoideum and Caenorhabditis elegans, separated from humans by 1.2 billion and 600 million years. Without retraining, the models recovered developmental order and preserved organism-specific cell identities. UCE encodes genes by protein sequence and could use 97% of measured genes, whereas Geneformer and scGPT, after ortholog mapping, used under one-fifth. UCE performed most consistently, often exceeding species-native baselines. Restricted to ortholog-mappable genes, it still scored above vocabulary-based models on five of six annotation tasks, some narrowly. Foundation models can thus yield informative cell embeddings across more than a billion years of evolution, and protein-sequence gene representations make these embeddings more complete and often more accurate.

EMBER: Contextualized gene representations enable compositional prediction across cellular perturbations

Ho, N., Xing, E. P., Ma, J.

Abstract: Predicting the effects of individual and combined perturbations is central to identifying context-specific vulnerabilities and designing targeted interventions. Yet learning gene representations that integrate functional relationships with cellular state and transfer across perturbation tasks remains challenging. Here, we present EMBER, a gene-centric foundation model that learns context-dependent functional representations of genes. EMBER combines interaction-guided contrastive learning with masked expression reconstruction during pretraining on a single-cell corpus curated for diversity and balanced sampling across cell types and experimental batches. EMBER decouples gene representation learning from task-specific prediction through a composable embedding framework: the pretrained encoder remains frozen, while lightweight probes combine contextualized gene embeddings to predict individual and combined perturbation effects. The same pretrained representations support single-gene perturbation-response prediction, digenic interaction prediction, and multi-target drug-response prediction. EMBER improves cross-context perturbation-response prediction and genetic-interaction ranking relative to evaluated baselines. EMBER also prioritized SCFD1, a mediator of autophagosome-lysosome fusion, as a candidate dependency during mTOR inhibition. Together, these results establish contextualized gene embeddings as reusable building blocks for modeling genetic and pharmacological perturbations, supporting the prioritization of genetic interactions and context-specific interventions for experimental testing.

Small molecule structure elucidation from NMR spectra with generative and reasoning models

Xiong, Z., Ying, V. Y., +9 authors, Zhong, E.

Abstract: Elucidating the structures of unknown small molecules remains a major challenge in chemistry, pharmacology, and drug discovery. While nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) spectroscopy is the gold standard for structure elucidation, interpreting spectra remains labor-intensive and requires expert knowledge to navigate the vast space of possible structures. Here, we present an AI system for rapid, automated de novo structure elucidation that combines a molecular generative model with a scientific reasoning agent. Its generative component, ChefNMR2, is an atomic flow-matching model that predicts small-molecule structures from molecular formulae and one-dimensional 1H and 13C NMR spectra. ChefNMR2-Agent augments our generative model with large language models (LLMs) to reason over and refine candidate structures. Across diverse benchmarks, ChefNMR2 substantially outperforms existing tools, offering calibrated confidence estimates and two to three orders of magnitude faster inference than frontier LLMs. We use ChefNMR2-Agent to elucidate the structures of four novel natural products, matching human-chemist accuracy in a fraction of the time. Our work demonstrates how specialized generative models and general-purpose reasoning agents can unite to solve challenging scientific inverse problems and accelerate NMR structure elucidation, thus opening new possibilities for AI-driven small-molecule discovery across diverse disciplines.

From cell counts to cellular interactions: Cu-Cyto and the co-localization index as a spatial framework for the tumor immune microenvironment of rectal cancer.

Kimihiro Yamashita, Toru Nagasaka, +20 authors, Yoshihiro Kakeji

Abstract: The tumor immune microenvironment in colorectal cancer has emerged as a major determinant of patient outcome, with tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, particularly CD8⁺ T cells, mediating antitumor immunity. Standardized density-based metrics such as the Immunoscore have demonstrated prognostic value but cannot capture the spatial interactions among cells that underlie immune-tumor biology. We introduce a deep learning framework that integrates two complementary axes of analysis: Cu-Cyto, a deep learning-based image cytometry platform that detects and classifies approximately twenty cell types from standard immunohistochemistry-stained whole-slide images, using a bit-pattern kernel-filtering algorithm to prevent multi-counting and an off-target labeling strategy for precise nuclear-center localization; and the Co-Localization Index, which converts the classification probabilities and coordinates produced by Cu-Cyto into a single quantitative measure of co-localization between two or three cell types. We illustrate this framework through CD103⁺CD8⁺ tissue-resident memory-like T cells in rectal cancer treated with neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy: their stromal but not intratumoral density independently predicts relapse-free survival. This divergence, which compartment-aware density alone cannot explain, motivates a metric that captures within-compartment spatial relationships. The Co-Localization Index extends naturally to three-cell interactions, providing a quantitative readout of the tri-cellular biology among CD103⁺CD8⁺ T cells, tumor cells, and stromal components. Prospective validation in independent rectal-cancer cohorts, addressing watch-and-wait organ preservation and adjuvant chemotherapy decisions, will determine whether this spatial-cellular framework translates into routine clinical decision support with potential extension to other solid tumor contexts.
