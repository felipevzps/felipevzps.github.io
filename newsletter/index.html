Batch Effect Correction to Enhance Utility of a Publicly Available ALS Proteomics Dataset

Cooper, G., Oh, I., +2 authors, Miller, T.

Abstract: Large, publicly available databases of patient data have been successful in identifying associations between neurodegenerative diseases and biological features that might otherwise go unnoticed. The Answer ALS Consortium is one such database that has generated a rich repository of multi-omic data from people with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS). However, large studies can suffer from batch effects that obscure genuine biological signal and prevent experiment reproducibility. In this study, we identified a batch effect in the Answer ALS 1-2 proteomic dataset (508 samples x 3,643 proteins) and corrected the effect using both the internal controls provided by Answer ALS and the batch effect-correction algorithm (BECA) ComBat. We identified a batch effect in the Answer ALS Dataset 1-2 proteomic dataset that, uncorrected, may obscure biology. Proteins MOS and H4 were outliers of expression in the dataset that correlated with the batch effect. Removing MOS and H4 did not correct the effect fully but did reveal new principal component (PC) contributing proteins related to motor neurons and potentially ALS: AINX, NFL, and NFM. The batch differential controls (BDCs) provided by Answer ALS were used to correct samples in their respective batches. ComBat, a well-characterized BECA, was applied to the dataset both before (ComBat-BDC) and after (BDC-ComBat) correction with the BDCs. BDC-ComBat was the only method that successfully reduced batch effect across the entire dataset. This method also preserved four of the top six PC-contributing proteins from the original dataset, including three neuronal proteins, suggesting that the corrected dataset recapitulates biology as well as the variability of the original dataset. Altogether, we produced a version of the Answer ALS proteomic dataset (354 samples by 3,641 proteins) with globally reduced batch effect and protein-level reduced batch effect. This version of the dataset recapitulates the individual protein variability and important neuronal proteins of the original. We also created a workflow for identifying and correcting batch effects that can be applied to other large data repositories.

Computational Repurposing of FDA Approved Drugs Against Cyclin Dependent Kinase (CDK2)

Deevi, G., Singh, B.

Abstract: Drug repurposing is an approach for identifying new therapeutic with existing FDA approved drugs. Cyclin-dependent kinase 2 (CDK2) is an important regulator of cell cycle progression. The present study aimed to identify FDA-approved drugs with potential binding activity against CDK2 using a Computational drug-repurposing approach. The crystal structure of Human CDK2 (PDB ID: 3PY0) was used for Molecular docking, followed by Protein Ligand Interaction Profiler (PLIP) analysis to evaluate Protein Ligand interactions. Further for drug-likeness and pharmacokinetic properties using Swiss ADME and for Predicted toxicity using ProTox Was done. Based on this docking affinity, interaction profiles, drug-likeness, pharmacokinetics and toxicity, the best target was selected. Carteolol demonstrated the most balanced overall computational profile and was selected as the primary candidate for potential computational finding to provide repurposing against CDK2. Experimental studies are required to validate its direct binding and inhibitory activity against CDK2.

RNA assignment and segmentation geometry in Visium HD profiles of fibrostenotic Crohn's disease

Massimino, L., Ungaro, F.

Abstract: Retrospective reuse of spatial transcriptomics often starts from a deposited bin-level Seurat object rather than the inputs needed to revisit upstream molecular processing. We tested whether image segmentation could support a defensible cell-like reconstruction under this constraint in two complete H&E-stained intestinal sections from a Visium HD study of Crohn's disease-associated fibrostenosis (GSE302569). The available data comprised 18,085 genes, 988,942 original 8 {micro}m bins and 179,596,575 UMIs. We evaluated 20 segmentation configurations in the preserved image coordinate system using a common bin-centre assignment rule, without restarting Space Ranger or introducing higher-resolution expression. RNA assignment ranged from 0% to 74.68% in CD1 and 0% to 64.05% in CD2. Expansion increased recovery but did not eliminate merged regions, straight boundaries or within-bin ambiguity; approximately 70-73% of InstanSeg brightfield nuclear regions received no assigned RNA. All 38 non-empty configuration-slice combinations underwent native full-mode RCTD against the same nine-group scIBD reference. Mean slice-level RNA recovery was negatively associated with primary-component dominance across 19 configurations (descriptive Spearman {rho} = -0.77). Neither dominant-call agreement nor concentrated RCTD support validates boundaries or cell identity. The observed limitations in RNA recovery, region geometry, and compositional support did not establish any tested configuration as a reliable single-cell reconstruction from these inputs. The findings identify limitations of retrospective single-cell reconstruction from the available binned inputs, not the impossibility of cell-resolved analysis from suitable original data.

Differentiable Spin-Center Diffusion Model Enables DEER-Guided Generative Pipeline of Weighted Protein Ensembles

Kao, T.-Y., Meiler, J., Mchaourab, H. S.

Abstract: Biological function entails the interconversion of a protein between distinct conformations, an equilibrium modulated by ligands and other physicochemical conditions. Double electron-electron resonance (DEER) spectroscopy captures these ensembles as distance distributions, P(r), between spin label pairs. However, the width of P(r) convolves protein conformational heterogeneity with the rotameric flexibility of the label. Because current spin label models overestimate this contribution, translating P(r) into ensembles has remained indirect and predominantly qualitative. Here, we developed spin-center (SC) diffusion, a differentiable model of the spin label with a tunable spin label rotameric width. Coupled to a frozen Boltz-2 structure generator, SC diffusion enables the entire experimental P(r) to guide ensemble generation at inference time. We demonstrate the robustness of this pipeline on synthetic P(r) of known composition, recovering the ensemble populations, including both components of a bimodal profile. Guided by experimental P(r) measured under different biochemical conditions, ensembles of the transporters PfMATE, LmrP, MhsT and P-glycoprotein were modeled and free energy differences derived from the ensembles in a condition-dependent manner. By embedding a differentiable label model in structure generation, our approach interprets DEER distributions as weighted conformational ensembles, adding a missing thermodynamic grounding to generative protein structure prediction methods.

The Findings Workflow: An agentic, collaborative, provenance-tracked framework for scientific data analysis with Claude Code

Riffle, M., Zelter, A., MacCoss, M. J.

Abstract: Scientists presented with quantitative data often face two related problems: confidence that an analysis is statistically and computationally correct, and provenance sufficient to reproduce the resulting figures and conclusions. Large language models (LLMs) can make sophisticated analyses accessible through a collaborative, conversational interface, but unstructured use can worsen both problems by producing plausible but incorrect results and obscuring the steps that produced them. Here we present the Findings Workflow, a free, open-source Claude Code plugin designed to retain the flexibility of conversational LLM-assisted analysis while placing it within a structured, verifiable, and reproducible environment. The workflow uses staged, gated progression to ensure the science, metadata, and data are understood and tested data loaders are built before downstream analysis begins. Specialized subagents perform statistical analysis, programming, visualization, research, reporting, and validation, with independent review incorporated throughout. The plugin also provides vetted analysis templates, conventions, and methodological guidance that serve as trusted sources of opinion when deciding how analyses should be performed. Scientists remain actively involved throughout, exploring their data and directing the analysis through natural-language conversation, while the workflow supplies structure, verification, and persistent state around that collaboration. Findings are recorded automatically together with the data, code, methods, figures, and provenance needed to reproduce them. We demonstrate the workflow with a worked example that reproduces central findings from a published plasma extracellular vesicle proteomics dataset examining Alzheimer's disease (Mag-Net). Together, these features are designed to make LLM-assisted scientific analysis easier to trust, inspect, and reproduce without sacrificing its conversational and exploratory nature.

Improving Molecular Docking Pose Recovery through Targeted Side-Chain Monte Carlo Sampling of an Apo Structure

Subramani, P., Shekhar, M.

Abstract: Receptor flexibility complicates molecular docking when only an unbound (apo) protein structure is available. Local side-chain rearrangements can alter binding-pocket accessibility and ligand complementarity, motivating approaches that sample these motions efficiently. Here, we evaluate a targeted Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) protocol that combines local and larger side-chain torsional proposals with periodic molecular dynamics (MD) relaxation under harmonic backbone restraints. Receptor ensembles generated from an apo structure were evaluated against 859 experimental complexes from the OpenBind 2A protease benchmark and compared with the apo receptor and ensembles generated by restrained and unrestrained MD. With regular-space representative selection, MCMC recovered a ligand pose within 2 [A] of the experimental pose for 74.4% of complexes, compared with 51.0% for apo and 59.5% for restrained MD. Median minimum ligand heavy-atom RMSD decreased from 1.96 [A] for apo and 1.82 [A] for restrained MD to 1.62 [A] for MCMC. The largest gains occurred for complexes with greater side-chain displacement relative to pocket backbone displacement, consistent with the motions targeted by the protocol. MCMC representatives retained pocket geometries within the experimentally observed range, whereas unrestrained-MD representatives exhibited reduced pocket free volume and poor recovery. These findings support targeted side-chain sampling as a practical approach to generating apo-derived docking ensembles. The reported improvements measure best-case pose coverage across generated poses and receptors; their extension to prospective pose ranking and virtual screening remains to be established.

LIMBER: A Hybrid RNA Foundation Model for Predicting and Understanding RNA Structure and Function

Wang, Z., Qiu, Y., +4 authors, Li, A.

Abstract: RNA foundation models offer a promising route to decoding the relationships between RNA sequence, structure and function, yet how model shapes the acquisition and organization of biological information remains poorly understood. Here we introduce Layer-Interleaved Mamba-Bidirectional Encoder for RNA (LIMBER), a hybrid foundation model integrating bidirectional self-attention with selective state-space modeling, pretrained on 40.5 million non-coding RNA sequences. Through task-specific fine-tuning, LIMBER achieves leading performance on secondary-structure prediction and non-coding RNA classification benchmarks, while demonstrating strong transferability to translation-related tasks and RNA--small-molecule interaction prediction. Beyond model development, we establish a unified, task-agnostic framework to systematically evaluate and interpret the biological knowledge intrinsically acquired by RNA foundation models. This framework validates LIMBER's strong capabilities in recovering RNA structures and predicting mutational effects, while uncovering a hierarchical, task-dependent organization of different biological information across model layers. By further systematically training and evaluating a broad panel of model variants spanning model capacity, training duration and dataset size, we uncover distinct scaling behaviors across structural, evolutionary and functional capabilities. Together, these findings establish LIMBER as a versatile RNA foundation model and provide broader insights into how architectural design, internal representations and scaling shape the learning of RNA sequence-structure-function relationships.

Interpreting graph neural networks reveals microbial association patterns in inflammatory bowel diseases

Cunha, A. F., Vidal, E., +4 authors, Santos, A.

Abstract: Traditional machine learning models trained on gut microbiome abundance profiles can distinguish individuals with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) from non-IBD individuals. However, their interpretation typically considers taxa in isolation, making it difficult to understand how associations between taxa contribute to predictions. Here, we inferred individual-specific microbial association networks from longitudinal metagenomics data of 106 individuals in HMP2/iHMP and used them to train and interpret graph neural network (GNN) models, which we compared with abundance-based models such as Random Forest (RF). Although RF achieved higher predictive performance (AUPRC ~0.59 vs 0.446), interpreting the GNNs revealed that their predictions relied on a densely connected module of co-abundant Bacteroidales species. Unlike the taxa prioritized by RF, these species were not differentially abundant between conditions. In an independent cohort, this module carried most of the predictive signal, and removing its associations reduced performance to near chance. These results show that coordinated association structure, rather than abundance shifts alone, is a feature of the IBD gut microbiome, and that interpreting GNNs can uncover such patterns that complement abundance-based analyses.

Single-cell perturbation prediction near experimental reproducibility with GENESIS

Conci, R., D'Ascenzo, D., Cultrera di Montesano, S., +4 authors, Lotter, W.

Abstract: Single-cell perturbation atlases now measure cellular responses across millions of cells, but the number of possible combinations of biological contexts, perturbations, doses and other experimental variables make exhaustive measurements infeasible. Here, we present GENESIS (Generative ENErgy-based Single-cell Intervention Simulator), a generative framework that predicts single-cell response distributions directly from the covariates defining an experimental condition. GENESIS uses a lightweight neural network trained with the energy score, a strictly proper scoring rule for distributions. We introduce an experiment-calibrated evaluation framework in which prediction error is interpreted relative to the reproducibility of repeated measurements of the same condition. Across the Tahoe-100M small-molecule atlas and a large PBMC cytokine atlas, GENESIS significantly outperforms existing generative models and statistical baselines and approaches experimental reproducibility on independently repeated conditions. Importantly, GENESIS remains accurate when trained on only a fraction of the available experimental conditions or cells, and generated profiles recover experimentally observed context-dependent transcriptional programs. At comparable training-cell budgets, GENESIS achieves more accurate predictions when measurements span more experimental conditions rather than profiling fewer conditions more deeply. Together, these findings suggest a complementary strategy for perturbation studies: broader, lower-depth measurements can expand experimental coverage, while GENESIS augments sparsely sampled populations and predicts responses in unmeasured conditions.

Long Non-Coding RNAs: Key Regulators of Economic Traits in Livestock From Mechanisms to Breeding Applications.

Khani K, Harkinezhad MT, Sepehri R.

Abstract: Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) have emerged as critical regulators of economically important traits in livestock, including growth, reproduction, disease resistance and product quality. Unlike protein-coding genes, lncRNAs orchestrate gene expression at epigenetic, transcriptional and post-transcriptional levels, acting as molecular switches, scaffolds, decoys, and competing endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs). This review synthesises current knowledge on key lncRNA mechanisms such as ceRNA networks, liquid-liquid phase separation (LLPS) and RNA-targeting clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats (CRISPR-Cas13) screening alongside trait-specific case studies in muscle hypertrophy (Callipyge locus), embryonic development (lnc_2217 in goats) and fecundity (ceRNA networks in sheep and chickens). We highlight the integration of lncRNAs with Genome Wide Association Studies (GWAS) data, where over 90% of trait-associated variants reside in non-coding regions, and propose a practical roadmap for functional validation and incorporation of lncRNAs into genomic selection and breeding programs. Harnessing lncRNA biology supported by recent functional annotation of animal genomes (FAANG) epigenomic maps and CRISPR-Cas13 applications in livestock, researchers will accelerate genetic gain in growth rate, marbling score, litter size and disease resilience, marking a paradigm shift toward precision livestock breeding.

Lachnospiraceae in the Food-Gut Axis: A Critical Review of Dietary Modulation, Immune Regulation, and Translational Barriers.

Wang R, Li Z, +2 authors, He Z.

Abstract: The interplay between diet, gut microbiota, and host immunity is central to food science. The Lachnospiraceae family ferments dietary fibers to produce short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), notably butyrate and propionate, with immunomodulatory potential. This critical review distinguishes established findings from hypotheses. Strain-level functional heterogeneity is substantial; findings from one genus cannot be generalized. Specific substrates (inulin, pectin, resistant starch, and arabinoxylan) and food processing methods shape SCFA profiles. Mechanistically, SCFAs regulate immunity via histone deacetylase inhibition, G-protein-coupled receptor activation, and Treg/Th17 modulation. However, most mechanistic data derive from rodent models; human evidence remains largely correlational and cross-sectional. Three major knowledge gaps exist: the lack of strain-level functional characterization, the absence of causal human intervention trials for the vast majority of Lachnospiraceae strains (with Anaerobutyricum soehngenii being the sole exception, tested only in metabolic disease), and limited translation into food systems. Regulatory barriers (generally recognized as safe [GRAS] status exists only for A. soehngenii CH106 and strictly for food use; no other strain has GRAS or qualified presumption of safety [QPS]), safety concerns (rare opportunistic pathogenicity), and technological challenges (oxygen sensitivity and viability in food matrices) further hinder development. Postbiotics (preparations of inactivated microbial cells or their components) and metabolite-based preparations (e.g., purified SCFAs or fermentation supernatants), alongside synbiotic formulations, offer practical near-term alternatives. Importantly, under the International Scientific Association for Probiotics and Prebiotics (ISAPP) definition, purified metabolites without cellular biomass do not qualify as postbiotics. We conclude that although Lachnospiraceae hold promise for immunomodulatory functional foods, current evidence does not support clinical or commercial claims. Priority research directions include longitudinal human cohorts, strain-level culturomics, and multi-omics integration. This review provides a realistic, food science-centered framework for future investigations.

Integrated Multi-Omics Analysis of Gut Microbiota-Associated Metabolites and Related Host Molecular Signatures in Cervical Squamous Cell Carcinoma.

Bin Chen, Changchang Xu

Abstract: Cervical squamous cell carcinoma and endocervical adenocarcinoma (CESC) is a common female malignancy. Gut microbiota and metabolites are critical regulators of tumor immunity and therapy, but their roles in CESC remain unclear. This study integrated TCGA and GEO datasets with gut microbiota information to construct a protein-protein interaction network. These targets were prioritized using machine learning, SHAP interpretation, and Mendelian randomization analysis. Immune-related pathways were explored using single-cell and spatial transcriptomic analyses. Molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations were performed to evaluate potential interactions between key metabolites and their targets. A total of 136 gut microbiota-related DEGs were identified, which may be associated with intercellular immune interactions. KDR was selected as a core target and may exert protective effects. Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic analyses suggested that specific metabolites may be associated with changes in the tumor microenvironment potentially involving the MIF signaling pathway. Network analysis of microbes, metabolites, and targets suggested that metabolites such as 5-(3,4-dihydroxyphenyl) pentanoic acid may serve as key mediators linking gut microbiota and KDR signaling. Additionally, eight non-toxic metabolites with favorable drug-likeness were identified, molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulation demonstrated stable binding to KDR with potential bioactivity, providing a theoretical basis for developing microbiota-related therapeutic strategies. The gut microbiota and its metabolites may be associated with the immune microenvironment and tumor progression in CESC, potentially involving the MIF signaling axis. This study provides a computational framework and preliminary evidence supporting microbiota-related hypotheses, and may inform future experimental investigations and therapeutic strategy development.
