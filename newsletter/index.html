EG-BINN: Parameter-Efficient Ensembling of Gated Biologically Informed Neural Networks for Epigenetic Age Estimation

Edwards, T. A., Long, Q., Shen, L.

Abstract: Epigenetic aging clocks are widely used to evaluate biological aging, disease risk, and clinical interventions. However, most existing clocks rely on linear models that overlook nonlinear relationships between age and methylation patterns, or on deep learning architectures that are parameter-intensive and difficult to interpret. We developed EG-BINN, a parameter-efficient ensemble of biologically informed neural networks that uses sample-specific gating to modulate representations across biologically annotated nodes. Trained on 93 multi-tissue datasets, EG-BINN achieved the lowest mean and median absolute errors among matched linear and nonlinear baselines in five-fold cross-validation, as well as the lowest mean absolute and root mean squared errors across eight held-out Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets. Analysis of the gating mechanism revealed widespread associations between pathway gate values and age, including trajectories that varied across blood, adipose tissue, skeletal muscle, and liver. Across 11 TCGA cohorts, EG-BINN identified significant tumor--normal differences in residual epigenetic age acceleration in six cohorts, whereas Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) highlighted recurrent pathway nodes contributing to tumor age predictions. EG-BINN combines accurate pan-tissue age prediction with an explainable architecture for examining biologically labeled nodes across age, tissue, and disease contexts.

Decoding Cell-level Circadian Phase and Rhythm-state Remodeling in Single-cell Transcriptomes With scRhythmBayes

Zhang, Q., Li, C., +6 authors, Sun, P.

Abstract: Current circadian omics analysis tools have been developed primarily for bulk time-series. However, heterogeneous single-cell datasets require analysis and interpretation of rhythmic programs across different cells, cell types, and biological conditions. In these datasets, cellular circadian phase is usually unobserved, and sample-level zeitgeber time provides temporal information but does not define the exact phase of every cell. Existing methods address only selected aspects of this problem and do not model how cell-level phase evidence informs gene-level rhythmic parameters and rhythm-state remodeling. Here we present scRhythmBayes, a new probabilistic algorithm for phase-resolved circadian analysis in heterogeneous single-cell transcriptomes. scRhythmBayes combines a seed-gene count likelihood with a stratum-aware circular prior to infer cell-level circadian phase, incorporates phase support into gene-level rhythmic modeling, and assigns posterior probabilities to interpretable rhythm-state categories, including rhythm gain, rhythm loss, amplitude remodeling and phase shifts. In heterogeneous simulations, scRhythmBayes achieved higher phase-recovery accuracy than existing phase-inference methods and maintained balanced performance across rhythmic gene detection, parameter recovery, and rhythm-state classification. Stress tests defined practical requirements for seed-gene support, zeitgeber anchoring, temporal sampling density, and cell number. In mouse liver, scRhythmBayes revealed distinct circadian phase occupancy and rhythm-state organization between periportal and pericentral hepatocytes, supporting a temporal dimension of liver zonation. It also identified cell-type-specific rhythmic markers that were largely distinct from conventional static markers, indicating that cellular identity includes temporal programs not captured by average expression. In human heart disease data, phase-coordination and phase-distribution analyses identified cell-type-selective temporal disorganization rather than uniform circadian disruption across the tissue. scRhythmBayes therefore provides a statistically grounded approach for resolving circadian organization across cells, genes, cell types and biological conditions.

An Interpretable Machine Learning Framework for Early- versus Late-Stage Classification of Breast Cancer from Gene Expression, with Co-expression Network Analysis

Biswas, S., Huda, T.

Abstract: Anatomical stage is a principal determinant of treatment and prognosis in breast cancer, yet it is unclear how much information about stage is carried by the transcriptome of the primary tumour. We asked whether an a priori panel of 65 cancer-related genes can distinguish early-stage (AJCC Stage I-II) from late-stage (Stage III-IV) breast cancer, and which genes contribute to any signal. RNA-seq expression and pathologic stage for The Cancer Genome Atlas breast invasive carcinoma cohort (TCGA-BRCA) were obtained from the UCSC Xena platform. After restricting the data to primary tumours with a known stage, 1,075 tumours were analysed (805 early, 270 late) and split into stratified training (n = 860) and held-out test (n = 215) sets. Logistic regression, a support vector machine, random forest and gradient boosting were tuned by stratified five-fold cross-validation, with median imputation, standardisation and SMOTE oversampling fitted on training data only and classification thresholds chosen on out-of-fold training predictions. On the test set, Logistic Regression had the highest ROC-AUC, 0.618 (95% CI 0.530-0.704), with balanced accuracy 0.578, sensitivity 0.741 and specificity 0.416; differences in AUC between models were not statistically significant (DeLong p = 0.199-0.585). Permutation importance in the Gradient Boosting model ranked POU5F1, FLT3 and NOTCH1 highest, but this model's AUC confidence interval included 0.5, so the ranking is exploratory. No gene was differentially expressed between stage groups after false-discovery-rate (FDR) correction (smallest FDR = 0.057, CD44). A co-expression network built from training tumours identified REL, EP300 and ESR1 as the most connected genes, a property that reflects co-expression rather than regulation or association with stage. Limitations include a single cohort, no external validation, no clinical covariates and a small number of late-stage test tumours. Expression of this gene panel carries limited but measurable information about anatomical stage; expression-based stage models should be combined with clinicopathological variables and validated in independent cohorts before any clinical application is considered.

Active Learning framework for Antibody Binding Affinity Prediction

Krishna, C. S., Singh, H., +1 author, SINGH, R. K.

Abstract: Antibody engineering campaigns must prioritise variants from large combinatorial libraries under limited assay budgets. We present a Gaussian process (GP) active-learning benchmark for antibody-antigen binding affinity prediction across eight public AbBiBench assays spanning influenza hemagglutinin antibodies CR6261 and CR9114 and AAYL-series scFv libraries against the SARS-CoV-2 Spike HR2 peptide. The framework embeds variable heavy and light (VH/VL) sequences with ESM-2 650M, trains GP surrogates over compressed protein-language-model representations, and evaluates kernel, acquisition, representation, classical featurisation, and labelling-budget choices under a unified budget of 10% of each full pool with a label-blind seed and a fixed held-out evaluation split. Across the benchmark, Tanimoto+UCB has the highest mean Recall@2% point estimate among tested kernel-acquisition configurations, achieving textbf{57.8%} mean Recall@2% across five random seeds (68.3% when scored against the acquisition-eligible pool), compared with 9.6% for random selection -- a gap that is significant by a paired Wilcoxon test across datasets ($p=0.0078$); its point estimate is not significantly different from RQ+UCB or Tanimoto+EI at five seeds. We adopt the continuous Tanimoto kernel of citet{gessner2024}. The same configuration recovers the largest fractions of top binders in the influenza libraries (74.2% on texttt{3gbn_h1}, 82.7% on texttt{3gbn_h9}, and 84.9% on texttt{4fqi_h3}) while exposing harder AAYL combinatorial landscapes where recall remains lower and more variable. An additional three-seed representation ablation, run under the original protocol and not repeated after the evaluation corrections, ranked ESM-2 650M highest (mean Recall@2% 0.673 $pm$ 0.28) among ProtBert, AntiBERTy, AbLang2, ProGen2, and classical encodings. On BindingGYM, the same configuration matches or exceeds ALLM-Ab on two of three datasets using the 100-sample held-out test metric. Structure-mapped Integrated Gradients and KernelSHAP analyses recover CDR-region attribution patterns consistent with contact-derived paratope geometry, though threshold-free (AUROC) analysis shows this qualitative agreement is not matched by strong residue-level discrimination. These results support Tanimoto-kernel GP active learning as a simple, reproducible strategy for label-efficient antibody affinity optimisation with qualitative structural validation.

TACTIC: hybrid denoising and clustering for robust 16S amplicon diversity estimates

Pourjam, M., Sewald, Z., +3 authors, Lagkouvardos, I.

Abstract: Denoising algorithms for analyzing 16S rRNA amplicon data, such as UNOISE3 and DADA2, recover error-corrected sequence variants and enhance resolution by preserving single-nucleotide variation in contrast to previous clustering-based methods. However, sequence-level resolution does not necessarily capture species-level ecological diversity. Variability at both the intra-genomic and intra-species levels within 16S rRNA genes may split a single species into multiple inferred sequence variants (i.e., zOTUs), thereby inflating estimates of microbial diversity. Using 16S rRNA sequences from the All-Species Living Tree Project (LTP) and Greengenes2 databases, we demonstrated that relying solely on sequence variation inflates species-level ecological diversity. We used sequence length to introduce variation and observed an increase in richness when using sequence diversity, particularly in cases where within-species redundancy existed in the database. Moreover, we analyzed and illustrated the amplifying effect of within-species sequence variation on inferred alpha and beta diversity across 27 V4 Illumina sequencing runs from 11 mock communities of known composition. Within-species splits, caused or picked up by denoising algorithms, inflated inferred richness and phylogeny-agnostic distances (i.e., Jaccard, weighted Jaccard, and Bray-Curtis) to the expected compositions. Post-denoising clustering reduced inflation in diversity estimates, thereby reducing the bias introduced by dissimilarity measures and converging distance values. We propose that denoising and clustering should be viewed as two complementary measures for biological resolution. We introduce TACTIC, a 16S rRNA amplicon analysis tool that offers operational taxonomic unit (OTU) clustering (UPARSE) and zOTU inference (UNOISE3), as well as two hybrid post-denoising clustering strategies that overcome the limitations of each approach: Taxonomy-Informed Clustering (TIC) and Taxonomy-Agnostic Clustering (TAC).

Widespread Recruitment of ParA-like NTPases into Prokaryotic Immunity reveals a diversity of anti-selfish element strategies

li, h., Burroughs, A. M., Aravind, L.

Abstract: The SIMIBI subgroup within the GTPase class of P-loop NTPases, including ParA/MinD, is best known for chromosome segregation, cell division, and macromolecular localization. Its role in immunity remains largely unexplored relative to TRAFAC-subgroup GTPases and other NTPases. Using systematic sequence, structure, and comparative genomics analysis, we surveyed the ParA clade for roles in immunity. ParA underwent at least two major, independent transitions from core cellular functions to prokaryotic immunity, producing two large "conflict" branches with diverse, uncharacterized systems, plus smaller, restricted immune lineages arising sporadically outside these branches. Across 28 conflict systems, ParA couples to a wide range of partners, including nuclease, NADase, and peptidase effectors, novel sensor domains such as a variant HeH domain, and apoptosis-inducing modules reminiscent of eukaryotic programmed cell death; several systems are enriched in multicellular bacteria, and a subset was transferred to eukaryotes on multiple occasions. Newly characterized ParA partner domains, including a nucleotide cyclase, a TIM-barrel domain, START, and permuted FHA domains, further define new antiselfish-element systems independent of ParA. Together, these findings establish ParA as a recurrently coopted "fulcrum" NTPase in prokaryotic immunity, predicted to act as an interceptor of invasive nucleic acids, a multimerizing effector, and a switch regulating other immune effectors.

GeoSinkhorn Flow: Geometry-Aware Flow Matching for Perturbation-Conditioned Dynamics in Single cell Data Phenoscapes

XU, K., Wilkinson, A., +5 authors, Krishnaswamy, S.

Abstract: Single-cell technologies increasingly enable large-scale profiling of complex biological systems across many experimental perturbations, but understanding how a system reorganizes across an entire space of conditions is difficult to interpret from single-cell embeddings alone. Phenoscapes address this by embedding entire cell-state distributions, rather than individual cells, as points in a data-driven landscape. Thus, a single dot represents one experimental condition rather than one cell, yielding a map of the perturbation or patient landscape that reveals how phenotype varies continuously across conditions. Phenoscapes remain a comparatively new and underexplored representation and existing static phenoscapes position experimental conditions relative to one another without specifying trajectories that describe how a cellular population moves from one condition to another. Flow- and trajectory-based models for single-cell data are numerous, but existing approaches operate at the level of individual cells, are not built to respect the geometry of a phenoscape, and typically cannot generalize their predicted flows to conditions not seen during training. Here we introduce GeoSinkhorn Flow, a perturbation-conditioned, geometry-aware flow-matching framework specialized designed to convert a static phenoscape into a dynamic phenoscape, in which each condition-level point is additionally connected by geodesic, condition-aware transport trajectories that are consistent with the phenoscape's underlying geometry. GeoSinkhorn Flow can also model transitions directly between time points without constructing a phenoscape. GeoSinkhorn Flow uses heat-diffusion geometry to define manifold-aware transport costs and trains a geometric autoencoder whose latent Euclidean distances stay aligned with the static phenoscape geometry while extending it to unseen cells. By explicitly conditioning the learned transport dynamics on perturbation-specific gene or drug embeddings, GeoSinkhorn Flow generalizes its trajectories to new measurement conditions, produces intermediate progression states along a trajectory, and extends predictions to unseen cells and held-out perturbations. An unbalanced optimal transport variant further learns growth or transition weights, so that changes in relative cell-state abundance or transition propensity are modeled together with state movement. Across synthetic manifolds, patient-derived organoid drug responses, Perturb-seq knockdowns, and time-resolved CyTOF signaling, GeoSinkhorn Flow recovered intrinsic manifold geometry, inferred biologically coherent response trajectories, and generalized to held-out drug combinations, perturbation genes, and time points. Together, these results establish GeoSinkhorn Flow as a perturbation-conditioned, phenoscape-consistent dynamical framework, distinct from standard flow-matching approaches, for converting static phenoscapes into dynamic ones that generalize across experimental conditions.

Computational Profiling and Pathogenicity Assessment of VHL Missense Mutations

R, R., Levine, E., +1 author, Ciochina, A.

Abstract: Mutations in the VHL tumor suppressor gene promote tumorigenesis by stabilizing HIF-1. In this study, we investigated the functional impact of VHL missense mutations using AlphaMissense pathogenicity scores, protein stability simulations, and molecular dynamics. AlphaMissense identified high pathogenicity scores for residues 60-200, including the {beta}-domain and HIF-binding domain, suggesting these regions are critical for VHL's tumor suppressor function. We categorized the mutations into two groups: those that primarily destabilize protein folding and those that likely disrupt VHL-HIF-1 binding. FoldX stability simulations revealed high {Delta}{Delta}G values for both groups, with proline substitutions exerting significant structural stress. Structural modeling confirmed that these mutations disrupt secondary structures, particularly {beta}-sheets. Molecular dynamics simulations showed that the VHL monomer is flexible and unstable in several regions, whereas the VHL-HIF complex, particularly the HIF-binding domain, exhibits enhanced stability. Mutational scanning revealed that bulky amino acid substitutions, such as tryptophan, cause notable destabilization. Interestingly, some mutations predicted to be structurally stable were still classified as pathogenic by AlphaMissense and validated in the literature, suggesting that pathogenicity may arise from subtle functional impairments rather than just structural instability. These findings underscore the value of combining machine learning, structural simulations, and experimental data to predict and interpret the pathogenicity of VHL mutations.
