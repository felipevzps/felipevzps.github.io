Development of a Temperature-Based Method for Repeated Markerless Gene Integration in cyanobacterium Synechococcus elongatus PCC 7942

Ohdate, K., Tsuboi, T., +2 authors, Watanabe, S.

Abstract: Genetic engineering of cyanobacteria can require multiple rounds of genome modification, highlighting the need for flexible markerless engineering tools. Here, we established a temperature-based markerless gene integration method using the temperature-sensitive H1 strain of Synechococcus elongatus PCC 7942, which, unlike the wild-type strain, cannot grow at 40 {degrees}C. Disruption of nynA, encoding a putative endoribonuclease, allowed H1 to grow at 40 {degrees}C, enabling transformants with genes integrated at the nynA locus to be selected without an antibiotic-resistance marker. Complementation of nynA at a separate genomic locus restored temperature sensitivity, allowing the same selection principle to be reused. Using this strategy, gfp, mScarlet, and luciferase were sequentially integrated, with all three genes retained and expressed. To demonstrate its applicability to metabolic engineering, markerless integration of cnsA, encoding cineole synthase, enabled 1,8-cineole production at 81.4 {+/-} 9.52 {micro}g /g wet cell weight. This strategy enables repeated markerless genome modification in the H1 strain using temperature-based selection.

AlphaGenome 2 predicts regulatory activity and variant effects across cell states

Gayoso, A., Mottram, A., +21 authors, Avsec, Z.

Abstract: Most genetic variants associated with human traits and diseases lie in non-coding DNA, where they can alter gene regulation in specific cell types and states. Single-cell atlases chart these states across tissues and development, but rarely profile enough donors to associate variants with expression directly. Deep learning models could bridge this gap by predicting variant effects from DNA sequence, yet they poorly distinguish related cell states and require fine-tuning for new datasets. Here we present AlphaGenome 2 (AG2), which substantially advances cell-type-specific prediction of regulatory activity and variant effects for gene expression and chromatin accessibility. Trained on organism-wide human and mouse single-cell atlases and bulk functional genomics data, AG2 takes as input a 1-Mb DNA sequence and a condition specifying a target tissue, cell type, or cell state. Conditioning enables AG2 to impute unmeasured epigenomic tracks and predict multimodal variant effects across cell types. Inferring this condition directly from gene expression extends AG2 to unseen datasets and cell states without fine-tuning. AG2 outperforms fine-tuned AlphaGenome 1 and specialized models trained on target datasets, doubling recall of causal single-cell eQTLs at 90% precision. AG2 also achieves state-of-the-art enhancer-gene linking and can pinpoint causal variants at complex trait loci. Across hundreds of human cell types, we generated an atlas of enhancer-gene links and scores for 7 million common variants. As single-cell profiling expands across development, tissues, and disease cohorts, AG2 provides a blueprint for turning observational atlases into variant effect maps.

Evaluating local assembly strategies for human long-read structural variant calling

Mirus, T., Luepken, R., Kehr, B.

Abstract: Long-read sequencing enables detection of structural variants (SVs) with far greater accuracy and completeness than was previously possible. This advance has spurred the development of many SV callers that implement diverse algorithmic strategies. Commonly, SV calling proceeds in several processing steps. Evaluations of SV callers, however, typically focus on overall performance and conceal which steps drive observed differences. This hinders targeted, evidence-based improvements to existing SV calling approaches. Here, we implemented five consensus computation strategies similar to those employed by popular SV callers that perform a local assembly. We compare their outputs on extensive simulated and real data and reveal that some strategies are more effective for particular input data types. For example, the strategy employed by PacBio's tool Sawfish works very well on HiFi reads and recent ONT data, but fails on the older and noisier ONT reads. While the read error profiles and the read pre-processing has a substantial influence on some strategies, other strategies are more robust overall. These findings guide algorithmic decisions for the development of improved future SV callers.

statLens enables accurate and interpretable statistical intelligence

Fu, D., Patil, A., +2 authors, Rahnavard, A.

Abstract: Diverse existing approaches and analyses of large-scale biomedical omics datasets along with the complexity of the corresponding statistical analytical workflows pose challenges to the direct application of large language models (LLMs) to data analysis tasks. We present statLens, a model-agnostic AI-driven statistical intelligence framework that integrates LLMs, code and human review. We demonstrate statLens using differential expression analysis across heterogeneous biomedical data as a use case. On 102 benchmark tasks from 74 datasets across six omics modalities, statLens maintained consistently high accuracy (92.5--95.0%) across seven language models and outperformed LLM-only inference (17.5--46.8%), whereas autonomous-agent accuracy varied widely (58.7--99.5%). We also showed that statLens substantially improved the accuracy of the four small language models (92.5--94.4%) over autonomous agents (58.7--92.2%) and LLM-only inference (17.5--43.5%). By decomposing analytical workflows into complementary tasks for code, LLMs and human experts, statLens provides an integrated framework for more accurate, interpretable and reproducible AI-assisted data analysis, particularly in complex omics settings.

SpaceMACE: Spatially-Aware Tumour Cell Annotation for Spatial Transcriptomics

D'Agostini, D., Abdelaal, T.

Abstract: Accurate discrimination between tumour and non-tumour cells is essential for interpreting spatial transcriptomics data and identifying spatially resolved therapeutic targets. Existing tumour-cell annotation approaches were primarily developed for single-cell RNA sequencing and do not explicitly incorporate the spatial organization of cells. Here, we introduce SpaceMACE, an extension of the MACE tumour-cell annotation framework for sequencing-based and imaging-based spatial transcriptomics. SpaceMACE first combines reference-based cell-type classification with copy-number-variation-based tumour classification. The resulting consensus annotations are subsequently refined using spatial proximity and gene-expression similarity. We evaluated SpaceMACE across multiple spatial transcriptomics platforms, including Stereo-seq, Visium HD, CosMx and Xenium, and across colorectal, hepatocellular and ovarian cancer datasets. SpaceMACE retained the high precision of the original MACE framework while improving the spatial coherence of tumour-cell annotations. Together, these results demonstrate that SpaceMACE provides a scalable and platform-independent framework for tumour-cell annotation in spatial transcriptomics and supports the spatial investigation of tumour-specific molecular targets.

Measuring Phylogenetic Information Content Based on Clade Support

Nylander, J. A. A.

Abstract: Phylogenetic information content is often understood as the degree to which data lend support to hypotheses about the monophyly of clades. This is most commonly expressed in terms of clade support, typically estimated by resampling techniques such as the bootstrap and the jackknife. Here, I discuss how to summarize this aspect of data quality. I show that raw bootstrap or jackknife proportions are unsuitable for this purpose and instead suggest a logarithmic transformation, which can be interpreted as translating the clade support values to the number of characters expected to give the same resampling proportion in parsimony analysis of a homoplasy-free data matrix. The distribution of the number of these "supporting characters" and descriptive statistics derived from this distribution, such as the mean (Mean Character Support, or MCS) and its standard deviation, provide powerful summaries of the quality of the underlying data set. I demonstrate for both empirical and simulated data sets that MCS accurately reflects information content, and that some popular alternative measures of data quality (the ensemble consistency index, the ensemble retention index, data decisiveness and the total support index) do not. Unlike most other measures of data quality, MCS can be applied to trees resulting from a range of alternative phylogenetic methods, including parsimony, distance and maximum likelihood approaches.

Policy-Enforced Genomic Repositories and Institutional Data Governance for African Pathogen Surveillance with UbuntuBio

Tetteh, A. S., Salami, R. O., +17 authors, Senyo, M. T.

Abstract: Research centers curating pathogen sequence cohorts often operate under constrained bandwidth, multi-institutional data-sharing agreements, and strict biosafety requirements. Global public archives keep released sequences permanently open, external viral portals enforce terms outside local institutional custody, and standard Git Large File Storage (LFS) duplicates identical binaries across repositories without validating taxonomy or geography. We present UbuntuBio (v1.0.0), a Git-native, policy-enforced genomic repository platform deployed at https://ubuntubio.org and developed at the West African Centre for Cell Biology of Infectious Pathogens (WACCBIP), University of Ghana. UbuntuBio integrates four mechanisms under a single storage root: (1) bare Git repositories storing dataset cards, sample sheets, and approximately 130-byte LFS pointers backed by prefix-sharded SHA-256 content-addressed storage (CAS) with visibility-scoped wire deduplication; (2) a four-phase commit pipeline recoverable via cross-plane reconciliation that enforces 55 African Union country codes against offline Natural Earth polygons, core MIxS-derived fields, Taxon 9606 rejection, and server-keyed targeted human-marker screening identically across HTTP commits and Git pre-receive hooks; (3) institutional custody controls combining Data Use Agreements (DUAs), revocable 900-second RS256 capability tokens, key rotation, tamper-evident hash-chained logs, and externally anchorable RS256 audit checkpoints; and (4) an embedded SQLite WAL and FTS5 catalog filtered by access tier. In single-node evaluation, UbuntuBio classified 18/18 policy boundary fixtures consistently across Python, Git, and HTTP paths; showed 0/265,157 false cross-matches on 21/22 RefSeq pathogen sequences (while cross-matching Candida albicans 18S rDNA at 0.113% and Chlorocebus sabaeus Alu repeats in ENA stool reads, confirming targeted marker detection rather than verified human DNA decontamination); matched conditional uniform (n = 2,000; 200-seed 0.01% convergence 19.0% vs. 18.13% expected) and interior (n = 1,000) detection theory while detecting all spike-ins at [≥] 0.5% (30/30); halved two-repository disk usage on 613.83 MiB Nigerian E. coli runs; passed 27/27 structured-filter correctness checks over 10,000 biosamples and reached graded NDCG@10 = 0.920 on independent free-text BM25 queries with zero private leaks; and recovered cleanly across seven injected phase-transition faults.

Generative Data Augmentation for Small-Cohort Glioblastoma Diagnosis from Circulating microRNA: Why Same-Modality Validation Overstates Performance

Rane, S., Mishra, S., +3 authors, Mishra, P. K.

Abstract: Circulating microRNAs (miRNAs) have potential as minimally invasive biomarkers for glioblastoma (GBM), but the small size of discovery groups makes it difficult to develop a classifier. The discovery cohort, GSE122488, comprised 38 patients (n = 38). Generative data augmentation was applied to increase the dataset, although evaluations based on held-out samples from the same cohort or a single validation set make it difficult to distinguish biological signals from cohort- or platform-specific effects. CTGAN and Tabular Variational Autoencoder (TVAE) models were trained on the 38-patient cohort producing 500 synthetic samples each. Random Forest and XGBoost classifiers were trained on augmented datasets and assessed using an escalating validation approach: first on an independent plasma cohort of the same modality (GSE184472, n = 118), and then on a tissue cohort from a different platform (Chinese Glioma Genome Atlas (CGGA), n = 198). XGBoost using TVAE-augmented data achieved an AUROC of 0.59 (95% CI 0.49-0.70) on GSE184472, compared with 0.42 to 0.53 without augmentation, although no AUROC on this cohort was significantly different from chance. However, this improvement did not carry to CGGA, where AUROC was 0.40 (95% CI 0.32-0.48) before harmonisation and 0.47 after Min-Max harmonisation. After harmonisation, a recall of 1.00 resulted from predicting every sample as GBM, leading to an accuracy of 0.41 and showing poor discrimination. The generalisation gap was examined in terms of modality mismatch, differing class definitions between cohorts, synthetic-data-related class imbalance, and incomplete cross-platform harmonisation. These findings show the need for independent, cross-platform validation before using data augmentation for diagnosis. The escalating validation framework provides a way to evaluate generalizability in small-cohort studies.

Deciphering the Biological Function of Tandem Mass Spectra Using a Dual-Modal Metabolite Language Model

Zhang, F., Wang, Y., +6 authors, Shen, X.

Abstract: Liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry untargeted metabolomics captures thousands of metabolic peaks from biological samples. Conventional identification-dependent metabolomics workflows require confident structural annotation before identified features can enter downstream pathway or functional analysis. However, only a small fraction of detected metabolic features can be assigned confident structural annotations, leaving a large fraction difficult to incorporate into downstream biological interpretation, a form of "functional dark matter". Here, we introduce Spec2Function, a function-centric framework that infers biological functions from tandem mass spectrometry (MS2) spectra. Spec2Function is trained using characterized spectrum-text pairs but does not require prior structural identification of the query spectra at inference time. At its core is Spec2Biotext, a dual-encoder contrastive model that embeds MS2 spectra and curated multi-aspect natural language functional texts into a unified functional space. Combined with a retrieval-augmented generation framework grounded in curated databases and biomedical literature, Spec2Function retrieves evidence-grounded biological knowledge and produces coherent functional summaries for individual spectra or groups of spectra. Across metabolite- and pathway-level benchmarks, Spec2Function achieved higher similarity to reference functional descriptions than the evaluated comparators and expanded the fraction of the metabolome accessible to functional interpretation. Application to a human liver cirrhosis cohort recapitulated known metabolic alterations and identified additional literature-supported functional modules. Applying Spec2Function to a 30-organ macaque aging atlas revealed organ-specific metabolic functions and previously unreported functional modules that were supported by independent human and mouse datasets. Together, these results position Spec2Function as a function-centric framework for scalable biological interpretation of structurally unannotated MS2 spectra.

Automated segmentation of renal structures using brightfield images of unstained kidney biopsies

Osiecka Carvalho, M., Brandao, I., +3 authors, Santos Cardoso, J.

Abstract: The automated segmentation of unstained kidney brightfield microscopy images remains a significant challenge in medical imaging. In conventional histopathology, staining provides crucial structural and molecular contrast, allowing renal compartments and structures to be readily distinguished. The absence of staining markedly reduces this contrast, obscuring the fine architectural details that are essential for diagnosis. Addressing this gap would reduce reliance on staining protocols, reduce technical and interpretive variability, and preserve valuable tissue for downstream molecular or multi-omics analyses. Ultimately, automation of unstained image segmentation could enable faster, more objective tissue analysis while preserving samples for further molecular testing. In this work, we provide a comprehensive review of state-of-the-art segmentation methods applied to renal brightfield microscopy images. Current findings highlight a predominance of Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) based architectures for semantic segmentation approaches while recent trends point to the growing application of transformer-based and instance-segmentation strategies. However, the field is still hampered by the scarcity and poor annotation of publicly available renal datasets. Aiming to further contribute to the enhancement of the field, a dataset of 18 Whole Slide Images (WSIs) of unstained human kidney tissue is made publicly available. Additionally, in the dataset, we provide 543 annotated tiles extracted from the WSIs, facilitating further research into stain-independent segmentation approaches. As a third contribution, we present a preliminary case study, evaluating state-of-the-art deep learning segmentation architectures, including U-Net and Segment Anything Model (SAM). Our preliminary results indicate that deep learning models demonstrate promising segmentation performance in unstained kidney biopsies. In alignment with these findings, advanced customised loss functions and data augmentation were integrated into the supervised training of the promising U-Net, DeepLabv3+, and TransUNet models, achieving significant improvements compared to the baseline experiments. To exploit the abundance of unlabelled data, we further tested FixMatchSeg, a semi-supervised learning method; however, its segmentation performance remained slightly behind the supervised methods. Taken together, these contributions establish a foundation for future research in stain-independent Computational Pathology, demonstrating that unstained brightfield microscopy images can reliably support downstream tissue analysis tasks. Our dataset, validation experiments, and methodological insights provide the community with both resources and benchmarks to advance robust, data-efficient, and generalisable kidney tissue segmentation.

HDAC1/2 maintain TERT mRNA stability in TERT-rearranged neuroblastoma

Finkler, S., Szymansky, A., +16 authors, Deubzer, H. E.

Abstract: Genomic TERT locus rearrangements define an aggressive high-risk neuroblastoma subgroup characterized by enhancer hijacking, high-level TERT expression and continuous telomerase activation. Although these structural alterations supply a strong transcriptional drive, the post-transcriptional mechanisms preserving TERT mRNA presence remain incompletely understood. We identified histone deacetylases 1 and 2 (HDAC1/2) as essential post-transcriptional guardians of TERT mRNA stability in TERT-rearranged neuroblastoma. Pharmacological inhibition or RNAi-mediated depletion of HDAC1 and HDAC2, but not HDACs 3-11, rapidly downregulated TERT transcript levels and suppressed telomerase enzymatic activity. Transcript decay kinetics following transcriptional arrest with actinomycin D confirmed that HDAC1/2 blockade accelerates TERT mRNA decay, reducing transcript half-life by 30-47%. High-resolution ChIP-seq profiling across the translocation breakpoint demonstrated HDAC inhibition does not alter local chromatin accessibility. Array-based DNA methylation profiling showed only a mild, non-specific global reduction in CpG island methylation upon panobinostat exposure and no focal correlation with TERT transcript loss across the breakpoint locus. Depleting TERT mRNA via HDAC1/2 inhibition dismantled a core 50-gene TERT-associated oncogenic expression network. Ectopic re-expression of TERT cDNA partially rescued cellular viability and normalized expression of key signature genes despite continuous HDAC inhibition. HDAC1/2 inhibition significantly attenuated tumor growth, reduced intratumor TERT expression and prolonging overall survival in both preventive and therapeutic TERT-rearranged xenograft mouse models. Our data establish a post-transcriptional rather than transcriptional mode of TERT regulation and confirm tumor suppression is partly driven by depleting TERT transcripts, uncovering a HDAC1/2 dependency in TERT-driven neuroblastoma. We provide a molecular rationale for therapeutic strategies disrupting HDAC1/2-mediated TERT mRNA stabilization.

From Calcium-dependent currents to Multitarget Therapy: A Pharmacological Strategy for Glioblastoma

Sanchez Trivino, C. A., Norbedo, A., +12 authors, Torre, V.

Abstract: Glioblastoma (GBM) is an aggressive primary brain tumor with limited treatment options and substantial interpatient heterogeneity. Ion transport and cell-volume regulation may represent pharmacological vulnerabilities in glioma stem cells (GSCs). Here, we characterized membrane currents in patient-derived GSCs, identifying a calcium-dependent anion conductance consistent with volume-regulated anion channels (VRACs) and an inward current consistent with Na+/Ca++ exchanger exchanger (NCX) activity. Based on these findings, we investigated individual compounds and developed a multitarget cocktail comprising niflumic acid (NFA), bumetanide (BTN), and R-ketorolac (R-K) to target complementary mechanisms involved in ion homeostasis, cell-volume regulation, and cellular dynamics. The cocktail affected cell viability and mechanical properties and showed synergistic effects in U87 cells and patient-derived GSCs with similar electrophysiological profiles. Treatment did not measurably alter spontaneous activity in cultured hippocampal neurons but caused substantial HMC3 cytotoxicity. Encapsulation in transferrin-functionalized liposomes attenuated HMC3 toxicity while preserving activity against glioblastoma cells, suggesting a potential strategy to improve treatment selectivity. Further pharmacological studies identified a potential vulnerability associated with NCX activity, although its contribution to cytotoxicity remains unclear. Overall, our findings highlight electrophysiological heterogeneity in patient-derived GSCs and support further investigation of ion transport as a therapeutic target in GBM. Combining multitarget pharmacology with targeted drug delivery may help address heterogeneous treatment responses, although further studies are needed to establish the underlying mechanisms, safety, and vivo efficacy.

Extracellular matrix spatial ecosystems define stem-like niches and predict post-recurrence survival in glioblastomas

Ayad, M. A., Shih, L.-K., +4 authors, Brat, D. J.

Abstract: Extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling is a hallmark of glioblastoma (GBM) yet has typically been studied through a reductionist lens, as individual genes or pathways rather than large scale, spatially integrated analyses coordinated with biological states. Focusing on whole matrisome gene expression, we identified three reproducible and spatially coherent ECM transcriptional programs, termed Homeostatic, Stress, and Fibrous, that were associated with specific microenvironmental functions and neoplastic cell states, with the strongest association noted between Homeostatic ECM and neoplastic quiescent stem cell-like (QSC) niches. Ligand-receptor analyses identified FGF1 and BGN as candidate ECM-driven regulators of QSC. In an independent longitudinal bulk transcriptomic cohort, ECM-based models outperformed traditional clinical and biomarker variables for predicting post-recurrence survival, with elevated Homeostatic ECM activity associated with improved post-recurrence survival, while MGMT promoter methylation no longer remained prognostic. Overall, these findings establish ECM organization as a clinically relevant dimension of GBM biology and nominate ECM-associated signaling as candidate therapeutic targets.

Mass spectrometry and machine learning for classification and molecular phenotyping of renal cell carcinoma and benign tumors.

Andreas A Sparre, Maj Rabjerg, +2 authors, Ole N Jensen

Abstract: Pathology classification of cancer tumor subtypes and benign tumors can be difficult due to cellular heterogeneity and similar morphological features. Renal Cell Cancer (RCC) poses a major challenge for uropathologists as advanced RCC is often incidentally diagnosed with complex histological characteristics. We hypothesised that molecular profiling of RCC and benign tumor specimen by mass spectrometry with machine learning methods enable accurate tumor classification and identification of candidate RCC biomarkers. MALDI-MSI and quantitative proteomics (LC-MS) provided peptide and protein profiles of RCC neoplasms and benign tumors presented in tissue microarray (TMA) format of 541 tissue cores from 126 patients. Machine learning models classified RCC subtypes: Clear cell RCC (ccRCC), Chromophobe RCC (chRCC), papillary RCC (pRCC), and oncocytoma (OC). Known and novel candidate protein biomarker panels for discrimination of RCC subtypes were identified. RCC peptide mapping, evaluated using patient-level cross-validation, reliably classified ccRCC and healthy tissue, but did not reliably discriminate the diagnostically challenging RCC subtypes chRCC vs OC. Quantitative proteomics identified over 5000 proteins and classified all four RCC subtypes with 95.5% patient-level accuracy, including robust discrimination of chRCC from oncocytoma. We report new candidate protein biomarkers for ccRCC and pRCC and distinguish histologically similar subtypes OC (benign) and chRCC (malignant), as demonstrated by mass spectrometry-based immunohistochemistry. Candidate prognostic biomarkers for ccRCC contribute to risk of relapse (CDK6, ATM) and risk of metastasis (DDB2, MK03). Digital histo-molecular differentiation of cancer tumor subtypes and benign tumors by mass spectrometry and macbine learning enables retrospective and prospective studies. Identified candidate protein biomarkers complement existing protocols in tumor pathology.

Computational and experimental strategies for functional validation of non-coding variants in pancreatic cancer.

Karla Jenkac, Jane Herlihy, +7 authors, Naomi Walsh

Abstract: Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) is a highly aggressive malignancy characterised by extensive genomic heterogeneity and limited therapeutic options. Although recurrent coding mutations in key driver genes have been well researched, large-scale sequencing studies have identified a broad array of non-coding variants which may contribute to tumorigenesis by interfering with gene regulatory mechanisms. However, understanding the functional significance of these variants remains a major challenge due to their abundance and the complexity of non-coding regulatory structures. This review outlines current computational and experimental techniques to identify, prioritise, and functionally validate non-coding variants in PDAC. We discuss in silico methods including genome-wide association study (GWAS) fine-mapping, colocalisation, machine learning-driven functional annotation, and the use of established computational tools, in addition to epigenomic profiling technologies including, ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq, and CUT&RUN which define regulatory landscapes. We also emphasise new AI-based methods involving radiogenomics, AI imaging analysis and data integration, which provides novel potential to link genomic variation with phenotypic and imaging characteristics. Additionally, we highlight experimental validation techniques including reporter assays, electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSA), chromatin conformation capture methods, and CRISPR-based perturbation systems used to study variant functionality. Together, these complementary approaches establish a framework for correlating non-coding variation to regulatory mechanisms in PDAC.
