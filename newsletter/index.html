DNA methylation assigns sequencing reads to closely related bacterial strains when their sequence cannot

Kakabra, K., Claesson, M. J.

Abstract: Clinical and public-health questions often concern strains rather than species, yet co-resident strains of one species can be so closely related that a single long read carries no sequence difference between them. We measured how often this happens in 639 pairs of isolates of Enterococcus faecium, Klebsiella pneumoniae, Listeria monocytogenes and Staphylococcus aureus sequenced on one nanopore platform. Among the 297 pairs above 99% average nucleotide identity (ANI), 40.37% of read placements (a read counted against one strain's genome) carried no sequence difference between the two strains, and above 99.9% ANI about three quarters did. Strains of one species also differ in the DNA motifs they methylate. Our method, Epilect, assigns single nanopore reads to strains from the basecaller's methylation calls, given a methylation profile for each strain, and declines to call when the evidence is weak. In the pairs above 99% ANI it correctly assigned 21.8% of the read placements that sequence could not place, at a precision of 0.959. In computational mixtures of 65 pairs of distinct strains at or above 99.5% ANI that were predicted to differ in methylation, its per-read F1 score exceeded that of the best of three sequence-based read-phasing tools by 0.25 (95% CI 0.08 to 0.37); where equally related strains were predicted to share a methylome, the comparison reversed. DNA methylation therefore supplies strain evidence on reads whose sequence carries none, and whether it will do so for a given pair of strains can be estimated before sequencing.

Inferring single-cell gene expression profiles from multicellular spatial transcriptomics data with st2sc

Lee, Y. J., Shah, S., +1 author, Lugo-Martinez, J.

Abstract: Spatial transcriptomics (ST) enables the quantitative measurement of gene expression within tissue sections but is currently limited in spatial resolution, as each spot aggregates signals from multiple cells. Recovering single-cell gene expression profiles from these multicellular measurements requires two steps: explicit identification of individual cells and accurate estimation of per-gene expression. While existing methods have demonstrated the feasibility of recovering single-cell expression profiles, accurately estimating gene-level expression for individual cells remains an important challenge. To address this challenge, we propose st2sc (Spatial Transcriptomics to Single-Cell), an end-to-end framework for inferring single-cell gene profiles from multicellular ST data. st2sc introduces a novel Attention Gradient Flow Tracking (AGFT) algorithm that extracts attention maps from a Vision-Gene Transformer to identify candidate cell instances without predefined background constraints. The detected instances are then used by a set of regressors trained under a multiple-instance, multiple-target regression setting to predict precise single-cell gene expression counts. Comprehensive evaluations on synthetic and Visium-like datasets demonstrate that st2sc significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods in accurately segmenting cellular instances and predicting gene counts. The method can further resolve contested 2um bin in Visium HD. Application to human IPF lung showed the model can recover the subtype marker level analysis. This advancement further improves single-cell resolution analysis in spatial transcriptomics, facilitating more accurate downstream biological analyses.

Minor intron splicing restricts E2F transcription factor activity in RB-deregulated prostate cancer

Thienger, P., Paassen, I., +16 authors, Rubin, M. A.

Abstract: Castration-resistant prostate cancer with neuroendocrine differentiation (CRPC-NE) arises in RB1-mutated lineage plastic cells in response to androgen receptor (AR)-targeted therapies. RB1 loss leads to constitutive E2F activation and cylin-dependent kinase (CDK) inhibitor resistance, making it challenging to restrict E2F activity. A post-transcriptional node is that typical E2Fs contain a rare but highly conserved minor intron, processed by the minor spliceosome (MiS). In an in vivo CRISPR screen, we nominate the MiS as the strongest dependency, and U6atac correlates strongest with E2F transcriptional programs in a clinical CRPC cohort. U6atac depletion led to mis-splicing of typical E2Fs, cell cycle arrest, and translation of a minor intron-containing E2F1 isoform (E2F1-MIRI) as a pharmacodynamic biomarker. Combined E2F activator depletion, induced further neuroendocrine (NE) specification. U6atac depletion shifted NE identity along the ASCL1 axis without the secretory granule program, and induced cell-surface antigens including DLL3, nominating MiS inhibition as a combination partner for antigen-directed agents.
