NEPTUNE: a neuronal cell death pathway prediction tool used to decipher neurodegeneration using single cell RNA-seq datasets

Padvi, A. P., Brunet, A. A., +1 author, Carvalho, L. S.

Abstract: In the constantly evolving landscape of cell death mechanisms, much of the literature is dominated by research performed in cancer cells and regenerating cell types. However, neurons differ in that they are non-regenerating and post-mitotic. Therefore, assessing neuronal specific disease biomarkers, including cell death markers, without the bias of non-neuronal or cancerous cells, could provide a more biologically accurate neurodegeneration profile. In this study, we developed the Neuronal cEll death Prediction Tool for Understanding Neurodegenerative Events (NEPTUNE), a user-friendly tool for researchers that bioinformatically identifies neuronal cell death markers from user provided differentially expressed gene lists obtained by single cell RNA sequencing data. We applied NEPTUNE to a publicly available single cell RNA sequencing dataset and analysed the progression of photoreceptor degeneration in the rd1 mouse model of severe retinitis pigmentosa. The analysis interpreted the dataset in a neuronal-specific manner and showed that late-stage photoreceptor death is not purely apoptotic but possibly engages overlapping cell death programmes. From the analysis, the user is provided with publishable graphs and a list of cell death markers with fold change, type of impact, cell death type and a strength score. Overall, our tool provides markers specific to neurodegeneration, allows mapping to known WikiPathways and KEGG pathways, performs GO and KEGG enrichment analysis and plots the data for further analysis, greatly facilitating the study of neuron specific degeneration. The tool can be found at: https://neptune.rgtcarvalholab.com/.

Consensus methods for trees of blobs and level-1 phylogenetic networks

Wicke, K., Banos, H., +3 authors, Gascuel, O.

Abstract: Phylogenetic networks are increasingly used to represent evolutionary histories involving hybridization, introgression, and other reticulate processes that cannot be captured by a single tree. Recent advances have produced numerous methods for inferring networks from genomic data. These methods may produce multiple plausible networks due to optimality ties, bootstrap analyses, Bayesian posterior samples, or differences in tools, datasets, and parameters. While consensus methods are well established for trees and some approaches exist for rooted networks, no consensus framework currently exists for semi-directed (unrooted) networks. We address this gap by introducing a new consensus framework for phylogenetic networks, rooted or semi-directed. We first develop a method for constructing a consensus tree of blobs, taking as input networks of any complexity. Building on this approach, we construct a consensus for level-1 networks (i.e., networks with non-overlapping cycles). This method, called 'L1N consensus', takes a collection of level-1 networks as input and returns a level-1 network. The different components of this consensus network (e.g., blobs, hybrid clades, and edges) have support values that correspond to the frequency of the given component among the input networks. These methods are implemented in PhyloSummaries, building on the foundation of PhyloNetworks. We evaluate the performance of L1N consensus using large-scale simulated data. Our results demonstrate that L1N consensus outperforms existing methods in the rooted setting in terms of informativity and accuracy. Similar results are obtained in the semi-directed setting. Applying L1N consensus to Leopardus and HIV datasets confirms these findings and demonstrates the usefulness of the method.

CMVAE: Contrastive Supervision Improves Single-Omic Latent Manifold for Breast Cancer Survival Prognosis

Singh, A., Kohut, A., +1 author, Kabir, A.

Abstract: High-dimensional omics data are vital for fore- casting patient prognosis in oncology. While deep multi-omics learning methods are increasingly popular, they suffer from data missingness and low patient overlap across modalities. Consequently, advancing single-omic representation learning re- mains a critical yet challenging research problem. Standard generative dimensionality reduction methods rely on recon- struction objectives that optimize the global manifold topology; however, overlook downstream clinical outcomes. To address these limitations, we introduce a Contrastive Masked Denois- ing Variational Autoencoder (CMVAE), a self-supervised dual- optimization framework designed to map single-omic information into a clinical outcome-aware latent space. A stochastic feature masking strategy corrupts the input omic profiles, and CMVAE is optimized to simultaneously infer missing molecular data and contrastive alignment of patient survival trajectories. We evaluate CMVAE using leak-proof five-fold stratified cross-validation on the TCGA Breast Invasive Carcinoma (BRCA) cohort. The method outperforms traditional unsupervised methods, achieving an AUROC of 0.7397 {+/-} 0.0239 on patient prognosis. Further analysis demonstrates a highly significant (p < 0.0001) risk stratification among low vs. high risk groups. Beyond comparing within single-omic methods, CMVAE outperforms six state-of- the-art multi-omics survival baselines by a minimum of 3%. Availability: All codes including data preparation scripts, training and validation recipes, and experimental configurations are available at: https://github.com/kabir-ai2bio-lab/CMVAE. Note: Accepted at the IEEE BIBM 2026 workshop RLPD-Bio (Representation Learning and Pattern Discovery for Bioinformatics and Biomedicine).

Top2All: Benchmarking sparse-to-transcriptome reconstruction from high-expression profiles across sex- and sex-steroid-defined conditions

Nishio, Y., Yamashita, T., Baba, T.

Abstract: Transcriptome-wide profiling at single-cell resolution is limited by sparse detection of gene expression, raising the question of how much transcriptomic information can be recovered from a small set of highly expressed genes. We developed Top2All, a Mouse-Geneformer-based framework for reconstructing transcriptome-wide expression from sample-specific sparse inputs. Using 24 cell-number-normalized RNA-seq profiles of mouse adrenal zona fasciculata cells across eight experimental conditions defined by sex and sex-steroid milieu, we evaluated condition-aware and no-condition models under replicate-wise out-of-fold and leave-one-condition-out validation. Top2All achieved near-unity sample-wise correlation with measured expression, but similarly high correlations were obtained using simple reference profiles, whereas gene-wise evaluation revealed substantially greater differences among methods. Explicit condition information did not consistently improve reconstruction, indicating that sex- and sex-steroid-associated condition information was already strongly encoded in the sparse expression profiles. Nearest-neighbor retrieval provided a stronger baseline than learned reconstruction, and input-depth analysis showed that increasing the number of input genes did not monotonically improve performance. These findings show that a relatively small set of highly expressed genes contains substantial information about transcriptomic state, while accurate reconstruction of gene-specific biological variation remains challenging. Sparse-to-transcriptome models should therefore be evaluated using gene-wise metrics, strong reference baselines, and tests of generalization beyond conditions represented during training.

Prefrontal cortex transcriptomes distinguish Alzheimers disease from asymptomatic amyloid pathology, neuropeptide loss, immune-complement activation and glial remodelling

Munir, Y. A., Sagir, U., +3 authors, Sani, I. M.

Abstract: Alzheimer's disease (AD) is now defined biologically by cortical amyloid-{beta} (A) and tau (T) pathology, yet the transcriptional programmes that separate symptomatic AD (A+T+) from amyloid-positive, tau-negative pathology ageing (A+T-) and from pathology-free controls (A-T-) remain incompletely resolved in the association cortex. We re-analysed bulk RNA-sequencing data from the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) of 15 donors (BioProject PRJNA1017546; 87 sequencing runs aggregated to donor level): Normal (n = 2), pathology ageing (n = 5) and AD (n = 8). Differential expression was tested with DESeq2 (adjusted P 1), and up- and down-regulated gene sets were analysed separately by Gene Ontology (GO) and KEGG over-representation. APOE and TREM2 p.Arg47His contrasts were treated as exploratory. AD versus Normal yielded 341 differentially expressed genes (DEGs; 223 up, 118 down) and AD versus pathology ageing yielded 496 (358 up, 138 down), whereas pathology ageing versus Normal yielded only six. Genes reduced in AD were dominated by neuropeptide and hormone signalling (VGF, VIP, CRH, GRP, TAC1, PENK; neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, adjusted P = 8.8 x 10-5; neuropeptide hormone activity, adjusted P = 6.3 x 10-8), most of which also differed from pathology ageing. Genes increased in AD were enriched for interferon-{gamma} response, myeloid activation, adaptive immunity and classical complement (C1QA, C1QB, C1QC). Relative to pathology ageing, AD additionally showed induction of gliogenesis, oligodendrocyte differentiation and myelination programmes (gliogenesis, 21 genes, adjusted P = 2.6 x 10-6; SOX10, MYRF) and of the microglial purinergic receptor gene P2RX7 (log2FC = 1.46, adjusted P = 1.5 x 10-5). All six pathology-ageing DEGs, including the myeloid genes VSIG4 and CD163, were also DEGs in AD, and 50 DEGs shared by the two AD contrasts moved in the same direction. Genotype-stratified analyses were underpowered or confounded with diagnosis. In the DLPFC, AD is therefore characterised by a graded loss of neuropeptidergic signalling, myeloid-complement activation that begins at the pathology-ageing stage, and an AD-specific glial/oligodendrocyte response. These signatures nominate candidate biomarkers and mechanisms for early intervention, but the small cohort demands replication in larger, covariate-annotated datasets.

GraphExperiment: infrastructure for high-dimensional quantitative data and associated networks in R/Bioconductor

Almeida-Silva, F.

Abstract: Networks are widely used to represent relationships among features (e.g. genes, proteins, and microbial taxa) and among observations (e.g. cells, samples, and species) in omics data, as well as their neighborhoods. The Bioconductor ecosystem provides users and developers with modular and interoperable software tools by relying on standard data structures that are reused across packages. However, Bioconductor lacks a standard data structure to store such networks together with the quantitative data from which they are inferred. Here, we present GraphExperiment, an R/Bioconductor package that provides an S4 class extending SingleCellExperiment with containers for feature- level (rowGraphs) and observation-level (colGraphs) networks stored as igraph objects. Networks are kept in sync with assays, row and column metadata, allowing the simultaneous manipulation of quantitative data and graphs. We demonstrate GraphExperiment in seven use cases covering bulk and single-cell transcriptomics, single-cell proteomics, microbiome, and comparative genomics data, with networks ranging from gene coexpression and protein co-abundance networks to cell-cell communication and phylogenetic networks. GraphExperiment is available on Bioconductor (https://bioconductor.org/packages/GraphExperiment).

Refining RNA-Seq Transcriptome Profiles with Transcript-Specific Minimal-Shared Regions

Su, Q.

Abstract: Accurate transcript quantification remains a critical challenge in RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) due to ambiguities in read assignment caused by shared regions among transcripts. Beyond shared exons among homologous isoforms, we identified the widespread presence of extensive undefined shared regions-overlapping sequences common to exons or transcripts of heterologous genes-across diverse species, including animals and plants. To overcome this issue, we developed a minimal-sharing strategy that partitions transcripts into unique sets of k-mers and systematically evaluates overlap across all transcripts. By isolating minimal-shared regions specific to each isoform, our approach reduces mapping uncertainty, thereby minimizing false positives and false negatives in transcript detection. Comprehensive evaluations using both in silico simulations and empirical sequencing data demonstrated that our method outperforms traditional probabilistic model-based quantification methods. This strategy improves isoform abundance estimates, enhances gene ontology annotations, and identifies distinct transcript isoforms critical for understanding biological processes, as exemplified in a Huntington's disease mouse model.

SENgEAR: an interactive visualization platform for comparative analysis of senescence signatures and cellular communication

Nagaraj, S., Adkins, R. S., +11 authors, Fertig, E. J.

Abstract: Single-cell and spatial transcriptomic datasets provide tremendous potential to identify the cellular states and communication networks associated with senescence. Annotating senescent cells in these datasets is hindered by the lack of universal markers for senescent cells and their context-dependent variability. To overcome this, several groups have developed gene signatures to annotate cells with distinct senotypes. However, comparing these signatures and their biological contexts to determine the specificity of senescence signatures remains complicated by the separate data platforms for signatures and datasets. We present SENgEAR, a no-code web platform for senescent cell annotation, visualization of cell-cell communication networks, and comparison of senescence signatures across studies, tissues, species, and data modalities. SENgEAR provides a unified framework to explore contextual signature specificity, cellular communication pathways, and senotype heterogeneity. We demonstrate the use of SENgEAR for applications spanning aging, fibrosis, and wound healing and its ability to interrelate these states.

Peripheral Exercise Transcriptomes Do Not Converge on Glutamatergic or Neuroinflammatory Signatures: A Cross-Platform Meta-Analysis

Shahab, M., Balajee, A.

Abstract: Executive dysfunction following physical overtraining has been linked to glutamatergic dysregulation in the lateral prefrontal cortex (LPFC), but whether this central mechanism is reflected in peripheral gene expression is unknown. This study tested whether peripheral transcriptomic responses to exercise converge on the glutamatergic or neuroinflammatory pathways implicated in LPFC dysfunction. Three publicly available GEO datasets spanning skeletal muscle, whole blood, and circulating neutrophils were analyzed across four exercise groups using limma, with a discovery threshold of nominal p < 0.05 and absolute log2 fold change above 0.5. Differential expression analysis identified 7,311 unique differentially expressed genes (DEGs) across the four contrasts, but overlap between datasets was limited: 7 genes were shared between skeletal muscle and blood, 38 between skeletal muscle and the GSE43856 contrasts, and 90 between blood and the GSE43856 contrasts, with none significant across all three studies. Gene Ontology and KEGG enrichment of the DEG union identified ribonucleoprotein complex biogenesis, ribosome biogenesis, protein folding, and T cell activation as the dominant biological processes; no glutamatergic or neuroinflammatory terms reached significance. Cross-tissue concordance between skeletal muscle and blood was absent (r = 0). Effect sizes among the genes driving the top enriched pathways were dominated by ribosomal proteins, heat shock proteins, and translation initiation factors rather than glutamate receptors or transporters. These findings indicate that peripheral transcriptomic responses to exercise are tissue-specific and dominated by proteostasis, translational, and immune programs rather than glutamatergic signaling. Direct assessment of central nervous system tissue or fluid-based glutamatergic biomarkers may be required to link overtraining-induced fatigue to LPFC dysfunction.

ToxINsight: An Integrated Web Platform for Sequence, Structure, and Ligand Binding Analysis of Snake Venom Toxins

Gupta, A., Krishna, T. V. L., Singh, B.

Abstract: Around 5.4 million people worldwide are bitten by snakes each year, with 1.8 to 2.7 million cases of envenomation, of which 81,410 to 137,880 people die from snake envenomation worldwide each year. It is categorized as a high-priority Category A Neglected Tropical Disease (NTD) by the World Health Organization (WHO). A molecular level understanding of snake venom toxins and their mechanisms of action is crucial for development of efficient therapeutics tackling snake envenomation. Snake venoms are complex mixtures of bioactive peptides and proteins that have also served as templates for drug discovery. Resources dedicated to snake venom toxins sequence, structure, functions, geographical distributions and molecular-level analysis remain fragmented across general-purpose repositories. Here we present ToxINsight, an integrated, freely accessible database and web-based analysis platform built exclusively for snake venom toxins. ToxINsight curates 2,078 toxin entries spanning 11 medically and scientifically relevant snake toxin families across 237 venomous snake species from five different snake families, and provides 2,540 three-dimensional structures associated with these entries, which are assembled from the RCSB Protein Data Bank , AlphaFold DB and ESMFold-generated 3D models. ToxINsight offers built-in tools alongside toxin records for comparisons and analysis of toxin sequences and structures and their properties for efficient target selection and prioritization. ToxINsight allow the user to select multiple entries and align them, or select a pair of entries to compare structural and physicochemical properties. ToxINsight provides all reported-ligand annotations for each toxin entry, and bulk export of sequence, structure, and metadata for building sequence and/or structure based machine learning models . ToxINsight also offers a visual ATLAS, which provides geographical information about venomous snake families/species for a more focused understanding of snake venom toxins across different geographical landscapes. By combining sequence, structure, ligand and geographical information along with analysis and comparative tools in a single interface, ToxINsight is designed to support and encourage researchers across the world towards the development of efficient targeted therapeutics against snake venom toxins from diverse families. It provides an interface for integrated understanding toxin sequences, structures and physicochemical properties, for providing scaffolds for designing antivenom and for comparative venomics. ToxINsight is freely accessible at https://toxinsight.in/.

Strategies of mechanical adaptations of circulating tumor cells and properties of their cellular microenvironment have predictive and prognostic biomarker value

Gaczynska, M., Ataei, F., +10 authors, Osmulski, P. A.

Abstract: Mechanically demanding conditions in the blood circulation damage and kill majority of tumor-shed circulating tumor cells (CTCs). Mechanical fitness: a set of mechanical properties detected with atomic force spectroscopy based force spectrometry supports survival of CTCs. We propose that live CTCs isolated from peripheral blood of prostate cancer patients represent distinct strategies of fitness promoting mechanical adaptations to the fluid shear stress. Some of the cells mimicked properties of erythrocytes (high stiffness) or leukocytes (high deformability). The clustering strategy characterized by exceptionally high adhesion is ingenious to CTCs and not found in blood cells. The abundance of CTCs with elevated adhesion has a predictive value for prostate cancer patients starting first round of androgen deprivation therapy. Moreover, a high adhesion score based on individual cells or average patient-specific adhesion is instrumental to stratify recurrent patients that underwent diagnostic imaging by PSMA-PET/CT and clarify inconclusive PSMA scan results. Including contribution of erythrocyte-like strategy broadened the predictive power of CTC mechanophenotyping. Improved biomarking opportunities to predict early castration resistances were noted when the composition of CTC microenvironment was included alongside the mechanical properties. We demonstrated previously that the CTC microenvironment is formed by large cells co-purifying by microfiltration of the blood of cancer patients. Analysis of the association of particular components of CTC microenvironment with the clustering strategy led to pointing at highly adhesive CTC-EMT. However, in the advanced patients adhesiveness of CTC-EMT was surpassed by tumor hybrid cells (THCs), a distinctive kind of invasive species formed by incomplete phagocytosis of CTCs by macrophages. THCs changed their mechanical phenotype and strategy preference with disease progression pointing at the dynamic nature of adaptations of tumor-shed cells to circulation conditions.

Revamped transposon-induced hepatocellular carcinoma model reveals deep similarities of cancer-driver landscapes across mice and humans

Imre, G., Mates-Nagy, A., +15 authors, Mates, L.

Abstract: Genome-scale cancer-driver discovery is limited by statistical uncertainty in driver calling and by experimental constraints on mutations available for selection. We developed 3xfOnc3 - a three-frame Sleeping Beauty (SB) mutagenesis system designed to broaden functional perturbations and reduce integration biases of traditional SB screens - and tested it in vivo in mouse liver. Within 12 months, 60% of mice developed heterogeneous hepatocellular tumors. Tumor-selected integrations were interpreted against an empirical reference from SB-based therapeutic gene delivery to hepatocytes, enabling correction for gene targetability. Background-aware filtering prioritized 315 driver candidates, including 243 protein-coding genes. Tumors were multiclonal, and identical integration-marked lineages expanded differently across lesions, supporting context-dependent clonal expansion. SB hits within young L1 subfamily members were selectively enriched in tumors. Human orthologues of candidate drivers were enriched for Cancer Gene Census genes and protein-altering pan-cancer mutations. Together, 3xfOnc3 reveals a background-corrected driver landscape linking driver selection to clonal tumor evolution and cross-species cancer relevance.

A single-cell comprehensive atlas of leukemia and its microenvironment.

Bofei Wang, Yu-An Kuo, +5 authors, Hussein A Abbas

Abstract: Acute myeloid leukemia (AML) remains highly lethal, with treatment failure and relapse driven by dynamic cellular states and evolving interactions within the leukemic ecosystem. In this study, we built a comprehensive atlas of leukemia and its microenvironment (CALM) by integrating single-cell profiling from 73 AML samples and 9 healthy donors, bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) from 87 newly diagnosed patients, and 4 paired bone marrow and peripheral blood single-cell data. All datasets were linked to clinical, cytogenetic, molecular, and treatment-response annotations. Immune analysis revealed diverse T cell distribution across disease status, where patients with persistent disease exhibited greater clonal expansion. Machine learning modeling on conventional T cells identified a T-cell signature pertinent to AML and outcomes, which were validated in two cohorts. CALM is a publicly accessible and interactive portal (calm.mdanderson.org) for exploring cellular compartments, cell-cell interactions, and longitudinal transcriptional and clonal dynamics to enable hypothesis generation, biomarker discovery, and target validation.

A HUWE1 regulatory helix gates ASCL1 degradation through its C-terminal phospho-degron in small cell lung cancer.

Keita Masuzawa, Kevin D Dong, +22 authors, Matthew G Oser

Abstract: Lineage-defining transcription factors are key oncogenic drivers but remain difficult to target pharmacologically due to the absence of ligandable pockets. The molecular rules governing substrate recognition by large HECT ubiquitin ligases also remain incompletely understood, limiting efforts to exploit these enzymes for targeted protein degradation. Here we combine genome-wide CRISPR knockout screening with base editor tiling screens at amino acid resolution, both coupled to an endogenous knock-in reporter of the SCLC lineage oncogenic transcription factor ASCL1, to systematically interrogate the mechanisms governing its degradation. These complementary screens unbiasedly identify the HECT ubiquitin ligase HUWE1 as the dominant regulator of ASCL1 stability in small cell lung cancer (SCLC) and resolve a conserved C-terminal phospho-degron centered on Ser207 and terminal Trp/Phe residues that are required for HUWE1 docking and ubiquitin-mediated degradation. Unexpectedly, base editor screening further uncovers a previously unrecognized regulatory module within HUWE1: a short negatively charged helix that functions as an autoinhibitory gate controlling access of phospho-degron substrates to HUWE1. Charge-flipping mutations within this regulatory helix relieve autoinhibition and accelerate degradation of multiple HUWE1 phospho-degron substrates, including ASCL1 and the canonical HUWE1 substrate DDIT4. Stabilization of ASCL1 through degron disruption paradoxically impairs SCLC proliferation, revealing that dynamic proteasome-coupled turnover is required for transcription factor function. Together, these findings reveal molecular rules governing HUWE1 phospho-degron recognition and identify a regulatory gate controlling substrate engagement. They also illustrate a generalizable strategy for resolving degradation mechanisms of undruggable transcription factors in their endogenous cellular context.
